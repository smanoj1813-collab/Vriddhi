# Vriddhi — investor deck speaker notes

Companion to `Vriddhi_Investor_Deck.pptx` and `investor-deck.html`.
Replace every `<angle bracket>` placeholder before you send or present this deck.

**Total run time:** 10–12 minutes for the deck, then let them question you for 20.
**Rule:** after each slide's 'say' line, stop talking. Silence is where they lean in.

---

## Slide 1 · Vriddhi

*On screen:* The AI-native academic operating system for Indian colleges

Open with the wedge, not the feature list: 'Vriddhi writes a college's question papers, runs the exam, grades it, and manages fees, attendance and results — in six Indian languages, for ₹449 per student per year.' Then say the stage out loud in the first minute: product complete, pre-revenue, funding the first 25 colleges.

## Slide 2 · 48,000 colleges are buying software this decade

*On screen:* And they are already paying for far less than what we ship.

Sources: AISHE 2026 (48,246 colleges, 4.50 crore students); university-ERP pricing benchmarks ₹300–1,200/student/year; IMARC India online education 2026. Land the asymmetry: 'These colleges pay more per student for less software than most SaaS companies charge for an entire seat.'

## Slide 3 · A college runs on 9 disconnected tools — and one weekend-eating ritual

Tell one concrete story — the HOD who builds 14 papers in a fortnight — then name the cost: faculty hours, delayed results, and accreditation risk. Do not over-dwell: 90 seconds maximum.

## Slide 4 · We get in through the exam paper, then own the semester

This is the slide that separates you from every 'we are an ERP' pitch. Say: 'Anyone can sell attendance. The exam paper is where the college feels pain every single week, and it is the only wedge that gives us their questions, their results and their learners in one move.'

## Slide 5 · Seven role portals. One product. Shipped.

*On screen:* Not a prototype, not a mock-up — verified in the repository at commit 091c6d5.

Say the number that matters to a technical partner: 'A team building this conventionally would have spent ₹75 lakh to ₹1 crore and 10–14 months. That is our sunk cost and our head start.' Then hand over the audit document if they want proof.

## Slide 6 · One assessment loop, closed — with a human on every decision

Pause after 'with a human on every decision' — it is the answer to the biggest AI objection from educators AND from investors. Have the ₹23/student AI cost ready to say unprompted; it proves you have engineering control over the biggest variable cost in the business.

## Slide 7 · Global software cannot be configured into an Indian college

Use the Kannada booklet in the room — a physical artefact in Kannada lands harder than any slice on a slide. Then state the rule: 'Compliance is data in our system, not code. A new university is a 2-day configuration, not a 2-month project.'

## Slide 8 · The learning business is already built

*On screen:* Institutions pay for software; learners pay for outcomes. We already have both in one product.

This is the slide for the eLearning investor — see docs/INVESTOR/04_ELEARNING_INVESTOR_BRIEF.md. The line to say: 'Every edtech company in India is buying the distribution we already have. We are the only content seller whose learners are already logged in.'

## Slide 9 · Where every other product stops

Do not gloat. Read the table as a market observation: 'Legacy ERPs sell compliance paperwork; the AI tools sell a teacher a PDF with no campus data. Nobody joins the exam loop to the system of record. That junction is the company.'

## Slide 10 · Four revenue layers on one platform

Make the layering explicit: 'Layer 1 gets us into the college. Layers 2 and 3 are sold to a buyer who already trusts us — our acquisition cost on expansion revenue is close to zero.' If asked why not per-student-only: because learning is priced per learner, not per enrolment.

## Slide 11 · A 5,000-student college costs ₹18,123 a year to run

*On screen:* Fully serverless, Mumbai region, no idle servers. Cost model reproducible from the repository.

This slide does the persuading for you. Let them do the mental maths out loud, then add the second-order point: 'Every new college improves gross margin, because the expensive part is already built and the marginal cloud cost rounds to zero.'

## Slide 12 · What is proven today, and what this round proves next

Say the gap first, without being asked: 'We have not yet collected a rupee. That is exactly what this round removes.' Investors forgive a stated gap; they never forgive discovering one. Fill the two <placeholders> in this slide before you present.

## Slide 13 · Where the money goes, and what it buys

Frame the ₹50 L against this: 'Horizon 1 is what the bridge pays for — it is the list of reasons a college says no today. Horizon 2 is not a new company: the delivery engine, the assessment engine and the distribution already exist, so the money buys content and a marketplace, not infrastructure.'

## Slide 14 · A second business, sitting inside the first

For the eLearning investor, this IS the pitch: distribution + engine + content, with licence revenue paying the bills while the learning layer scales. Offer the two-way deal: their content library on our rails, or our certificates into their corporate base.

## Slide 15 · Why this gets harder to displace every semester

Rank them out loud: 'Data gravity is the real one. Everything else shortens the sales cycle; data gravity is why a college that uses us for two semesters never leaves.'

## Slide 16 · Who is building this

Investors bet on the person, not the product. Be specific about why YOU: your time inside a college, the years you spent watching this pain, and the fact that you built the thing alone. Never say 'we have no competition' or 'we just need funding to scale' in this section.

## Slide 17 · ₹50 L for 8–10 % — and three specific introductions

Ask for the specific things, not 'mentorship'. This is the ₹50 L bridge (Option A in docs/INVESTOR/03_EXPANSION_AND_USE_OF_FUNDS.md §6): 8–10 % on CCPS or a convertible note with a 20 % discount and a valuation cap, 9 months of runway, and the milestones on this slide. Then stop talking and let them negotiate.

## Slide 18 · Four windows are open at the same time

*On screen:* This combination will not be open in 2028.

Close the body of the pitch here. 'Why now' is the slide that turns a nice product into an investable moment: the policy push, the mandatory portal, and the collapse in AI cost all landed in the same 24 months.

## Slide 19 · Let's grow together.

*On screen:* Vriddhi (वृद्धि) — growth through knowledge.

End with a concrete next step and a date: 'I will send the pilot plan tonight and the first college's numbers in 30 days. If it works, I'd like you in the round.' Then ask for the introduction list before you leave the room — and put the follow-up meeting in the calendar.

## If you only get five minutes

1. Slide 1 — what it is, in one sentence.
2. Slide 4 — the wedge (exam paper first, campus second, learning third).
3. Slide 5 — 160 functions, 257 K lines, 1,540 tests: it is built.
4. Slide 11 — ₹18,123/year infrastructure and ₹23/student AI.
5. Slide 12 — say the gap first: 'no paying college yet; this round fixes that.'

Then ask for the three introductions. A five-minute meeting that ends with names in your
inbox beats a fifty-minute meeting that ends with 'very interesting'.
