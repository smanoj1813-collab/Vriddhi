# 05 · How to impress an investor (playbook)

An investor's real question is never "is this a nice product?". It is:

> **"Is this a person who will still be standing here in 40 meetings, with numbers that grew?"**

Everything below is built to answer that question. The product is already impressive; the risk is
*how you talk about it*.

---

## 1. The four rules

1. **Lead with the wedge, not the feature list.** "AI that writes a college's question papers, runs
   the exam and grades it — inside the software the college already uses for fees and attendance."
   Nobody remembers 12 modules.
2. **Quantify everything.** "We wrote 257,000 lines, 1,540 tests, ₹18,000/year to run a 5,000-student
   college." Numbers you can defend are the difference between a founder and a hobbyist.
3. **Name your gap before they do.** "No paying college yet" said first is a position of strength;
   said second, it is a lie of omission.
4. **Ask for something specific.** "Will you introduce me to three principals in Bengaluru?" beats
   "let us know how you feel".

---

## 2. The pitch at four lengths (memorise all four)

**15 seconds**
> "Vriddhi is the AI-native operating system for Indian colleges. It writes the question paper, runs
> the exam, grades it, and manages fees, attendance and results — in six Indian languages. We're
> live as a product, ₹449 per student per year, and the same engine sells certificate courses to
> 48,000 colleges that no edtech company can reach."

**60 seconds**
> "Every Indian college runs on 8–10 disconnected tools and a spreadsheet. The pain concentrates in
> the exam cycle: faculty hand-build papers for every subject, invigilate, mark scripts on weekends,
> then re-enter everything for the university. We built the whole cycle as one workflow — curriculum,
> question bank, AI paper generation with approval, secure online exams with auto-grading, grade
> records and transcripts — inside a full campus platform with fees, attendance, library and payroll.
> Two things make it defensible: six Indian languages with native-script AI, and state compliance —
> Karnataka's UUCMS portal and BCU pass rules — encoded as configuration. The platform costs
> ₹18,000 a year to run for a 5,000-student college and sells for ₹22 lakh. We're pre-revenue with
> the product complete; this round funds 25 colleges, the content catalogue and the three
> procurement features colleges ask for."

**3 minutes** — 60-second version + the three proof points:
- "We have 4 certificate programmes and 984 assessment items already authored to a validated format."
- "AI costs us ₹23 per student per year because we cache, cap and fall back to a deterministic parser."
- "Our unit economics at 10 colleges: 45 % gross margin, with infrastructure at 0.08 % of revenue."

**10 minutes** — walk the deck (below) and stop talking after each slide. Let them interrupt;
questions are the buy signal.

## 4. The 12-minute demo (only if they ask for one)

Order matters: show the *outcome* before the mechanism.

| Min | Screen | What to say | Why it impresses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–1 | Principal dashboard with the college's own name and logo | "This is the whole college on one screen." | Personalisation = product maturity |
| 1–3 | Question paper: pick topics → generate → blueprint shows unit/difficulty coverage → HOD approval | "This is what used to take a weekend. Note the approval trail." | The wedge, in 90 seconds |
| 3–4 | Same paper exported as PDF and DOCX in the university's format | "It leaves the system print-ready." | Proof it is not a chatbot wrapper |
| 4–6 | Student takes a test on a phone: instructions → section → autosave → submit | "If the network drops mid-exam, nothing is lost." | Engineering depth investors rarely see |
| 6–8 | Faculty grading queue: AI suggestion with confidence, rubric breakdown, human override, publish | "AI drafts; faculty decide. Every override is logged." | Answers the AI-trust objection live |
| 8–9 | Result import from a university XLSX → SGPA/CGPA, grade records → transcript | "University format in, transcripts out." | Compliance credibility |
| 9–10 | Fees: structure → challan → payment → receipt; defaulter list | "Every rupee traced." | The money door |
| 10–11 | Student phone: attendance %, timetable, results, ID card, courses catalog | "The student opens this daily." | The eLearning distribution argument |
| 11–12 | Close on the roadmap slide | "These are the three things your money buys." | Returns control of the narrative |

**Demo rules:** use a real college's name and logo, never "Demo College". Have a phone and a hotspot.
Rehearse twice, click nothing unrehearsed, and never show a screen with an empty state or lorem ipsum.

## 6. The 14 objections, answered

| Objection | Answer (short, then stop) |
| --- | --- |
| "No customers — why should I believe adoption?" | "Fair. That is exactly what this round removes. Here is the 90-day pilot plan, the two colleges in conversation, and the metric we'll be judged on." |
| "How is this different from ChatGPT?" | "A question paper is a controlled document: blueprint coverage, approvals, traceability, print format. And we work with no AI key at all — a deterministic parser reads the uploaded paper." |
| "Camu/MasterSoft already have 550+ institutions." | "They own the installed base; we sell to colleges choosing a system this year, at a third of the price with AI included. We are not betting on migrations." |
| "What stops a big ERP from copying you?" | "Nothing stops the feature; price, content and speed stop the deal. They can't sell AI-native at ₹449 with content included on a per-student licence model." |
| "Why per-student pricing? Isn't that capped?" | "It matches how colleges budget and how the market already prices (₹300–1,200/student/yr). Our cost per student is measured at ₹23 of AI plus cloud at 0.08 % of revenue, so margin expands as we add colleges — and learning programmes are priced per learner, not per student." |
| "AI costs will eat you." | "₹23 per student per year at base volume, ₹46 budgeted with a 2× buffer, with tiers, cache and per-college circuit breakers already in the code." |
| "Your valuation is too high." | "Then let's structure it — a smaller cheque at this cap, or a milestone tranche tied to colleges live and first learning revenue. I care about the partner more than the last 10 % of the cap." |
| "Two founders can't run sales *and* product." | "Agreed. The first two hires are implementation and content; here is who I'm targeting and what it costs (₹18 L combined)." |
| "What if a college asks for on-premise?" | "We decline on-prem and say why — serverless in Mumbai gives them better uptime and no server to maintain. It has not been a blocker in Karnataka private colleges." |
| "What about DPDP/privacy with student data?" | "Mumbai residency, claim-based tenant isolation, 1,827 lines of audited rules, no biometrics, no data sold. We'll do a DPIA before a large institutional deal — it is in the budget." |
| "Why hasn't anyone else done this?" | "Lots have — as ERPs, as LMSs, as paper tools. The hard part is joining the assessment engine to the system of record. That's a multi-year codebase, and we have it." |
| "What's your burn and runway?" | Have the number: "₹X lakh/month, 18 months at this round" — with the hire plan and a month-by-month table. |
| "How do I exit?" | "Two paths: strategic acquisition by an ERP/LMS consolidator buying distribution and content, or continued scale as a profitable category leader at ₹20–50 Cr ARR in this segment. Both start with the same thing — 25 colleges and a proven learning revenue line." |
| "Send me more information." | "I'll send the deck, the cost model and the audit tonight, and I'll follow up Friday with the pilot update. Which two things would you want to see first?" *(Never end a meeting without a next step with a date.)* |

## 7. What impresses (and what kills the room)

**Impresses**
- You lead with the number they will ask for anyway ("₹18,000 a year to run 5,000 students").
- You volunteer a weakness and a fix in the same sentence.
- You know your pipeline college-by-college, by name, with student counts.
- You have a written plan with owners and dates — and can email it, not just describe it.
- You say "I don't know, I'll find out by Thursday" and then do it.
- You ask *them* a real question: "Which of the three expansion paths would you back first?"

**Kills the room**
- "No competition." (There is; naming it shows you know the market.)
- Every screen is a mock-up or says "Demo College".
- A 40-slide deck with feature lists and no metrics.
- Vague numbers: "huge market", "millions of students", "we'll capture 1 %".
- Overclaiming on the site or deck (e.g. biometrics, parent app) that the demo doesn't show.
- Hiding a co-founder dispute, an unpaid contributor, or unassigned IP until diligence.
- Waiting for them to ask what you want.

## 9. Money mechanics (India, 2026) — get advice before signing

- **Instruments:** CCPS (compulsorily convertible preference shares) for priced rounds; SAFE and
  convertible notes are faster but check enforceability and tax treatment with a CA. Angels often
  invest via a simple share subscription + SHA.
- **Typical seed in this segment:** ₹1–3 Cr for 10–20 %, 18-month runway, one board seat for a lead
  investor, information rights for everyone else.
- **Protect yourself:** a vesting schedule for founders (yes, voluntarily — it signals commitment),
  an ESOP pool of 8–12 % carved *before* the round, no full-ratchet anti-dilution, no veto list
  longer than a page, and a defined drag/tag position you can live with.
- **Valuation:** do not anchor on a number you cannot justify from ARR or from
  replacement value (₹1–1.2 Cr of build already sunk, 103 person-weeks of documented effort). If
  pushed, use milestones: "a tranche at this cap now, a step-up when 10 colleges are live."
- **Tax/compliance basics:** 18 % GST on the licence, TDS on vendor payments, keep clean books from
  month one (investors and acquirers read bank statements, not spreadsheets), assign IP from every
  contributor and freelancer in writing.
