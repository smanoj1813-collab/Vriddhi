# 5.2 Quality planning, testing and acceptance

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Design acceptance evidence and defect triage without lowering quality to meet a date.
- Produce a reviewable quality checklist and defect log using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

The booking flow looks correct in a demonstration, but two students can reserve the final slot simultaneously. The sponsor asks whether the launch can proceed because the majority of tests passed. The team must consider consequences, not just the percentage of green checks.

The running organisation, Namma Campus Services, is fictional. All dates, costs, workloads and stakeholders in these exercises are synthetic. Work with role names rather than real student details. The case is deliberately small enough to model in a spreadsheet, but its approval, scheduling and communication problems resemble those encountered in entry-level coordination roles.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Build quality into the work

Quality planning identifies standards, acceptance criteria, review methods and responsibilities before testing begins. Quality assurance concerns the processes that support good work; quality control checks outputs. A peer review can prevent errors earlier than a final inspection. Keep the concepts practical: define how the team will detect duplicate reservations, inaccessible controls and unauthorised access before live use.

### 2. Risk-based tests beat a raw count

A test case specifies preconditions, inputs, steps, expected outcome and actual result. Include ordinary, boundary and failure paths. Ten cosmetic tests passing cannot compensate for a severe access-control failure. Severity describes impact; priority describes the urgency of addressing it in context. They can differ. Triage should be recorded with an owner and rationale, not decided silently by whoever updates the board.

### 3. Acceptance and handover need honest exceptions

A named acceptor reviews evidence against agreed criteria. Record unresolved defects and the conditions under which any exception is approved. Do not bypass mandatory safety, privacy or institutional controls because most features work. A release decision, a project acceptance decision and a Scrum Definition of Done are related but not interchangeable. Re-test the repair and relevant regression paths before declaring a defect closed.

## Worked example

Twenty of twenty-one test cases pass, a pass proportion of about 95.2%. The failed case allows duplicate allocation of the final slot and violates a mandatory acceptance condition. The percentage alone therefore does not justify release. Log the defect, prevent live use of the unsafe path, repair the concurrency rule and repeat both the failing test and related cancellation/rebooking tests.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: quality checklist and defect log (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Write six test cases including the last-slot boundary and a rejected unauthorised request.

2. Create a defect record with reproducible steps, expected result and actual result.

3. Assign severity and priority separately and explain each.

4. Prepare an acceptance recommendation using the mandatory criteria, not only the pass proportion.

5. Specify re-test and regression checks for a repaired duplicate-booking defect.

**Deliverable:** submit your quality checklist and defect log, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Using pass rate as the only release criterion.
- Confusing severity with urgency.
- Closing a defect because someone says the code was changed.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Build quality into the work:** Quality planning identifies standards, acceptance criteria, review methods and responsibilities before testing begins.
- **Risk-based tests beat a raw count:** A test case specifies preconditions, inputs, steps, expected outcome and actual result.
- **Acceptance and handover need honest exceptions:** A named acceptor reviews evidence against agreed criteria.
