# 5.3 Changes, issues and escalation

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Assess a change request, preserve the baseline and escalate with options and a clear decision request.
- Produce a reviewable change impact and escalation brief using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

Two days before pilot testing, the sponsor requests WhatsApp reminders and expects the original date and cost to stay unchanged. The coordinator should not refuse reflexively or promise silently. The request needs an impact assessment and an authorised decision.

The running organisation, Namma Campus Services, is fictional. All dates, costs, workloads and stakeholders in these exercises are synthetic. Work with role names rather than real student details. The case is deliberately small enough to model in a spreadsheet, but its approval, scheduling and communication problems resemble those encountered in entry-level coordination roles.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. A request is not an approval

Record the requester, reason, desired outcome, urgency and affected deliverables. Separate correction of a defect against approved scope from an enhancement that changes scope. The classification depends on the agreed baseline, not on whether the request seems small. Capture the decision owner and status. Verbal requests should be summarised and confirmed through the institution’s agreed process.

### 2. Evaluate options across constraints

Compare at least three options: accept now, defer, or offer a smaller alternative. Assess time, cost, quality, risk, dependencies and operational impact. A reminder feature may require consent, integration, testing and ongoing support. Do not price only the visible screen. If approved, update affected plans, acceptance criteria and communications through a controlled version change. Preserve the old baseline and the approval reference.

### 3. Escalation is a decision service

Escalate when a threshold is reached or authority is insufficient, not only when someone is angry. State the issue, impact, deadline for decision, options, recommendation and consequence of no decision. Distinguish what is known from an estimate. A useful escalation makes a decision easier; it does not transfer every detail to the sponsor or publicly blame a colleague.

## Worked example

The team estimates reminders at 12 development hours plus 6 testing hours. At a classroom cost of ₹500 per hour, direct effort cost is ₹9,000 before any provider charges. With three focused hours available daily from the relevant specialist, 18 sequential hours require six working days. An option to defer reminders preserves the pilot date, while adding them requires a revised commitment or a justified scope trade-off.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: change impact and escalation brief (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Draft a change request with a baseline reference and the reason for reminders.

2. Calculate the effort cost and duration with explicit exclusions.

3. Compare accept, defer and a smaller non-automated alternative.

4. Write a one-page escalation with decision owner and deadline.

5. Record an example decision and show which plans and stakeholders must be updated.

**Deliverable:** submit your change impact and escalation brief, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Estimating only coding effort for a new feature.
- Treating urgency as approval authority.
- Changing a baseline without keeping the decision trail.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **A request is not an approval:** Record the requester, reason, desired outcome, urgency and affected deliverables.
- **Evaluate options across constraints:** Compare at least three options: accept now, defer, or offer a smaller alternative.
- **Escalation is a decision service:** Escalate when a threshold is reached or authority is insufficient, not only when someone is angry.

## Reflection journal

1. What did you change after checking the example or receiving a review comment? Name the exact input, statement or calculation.
2. Which assumption has the greatest effect on your recommendation, and how would you test it before a real decision?
3. How would you explain this work in a two-minute interview for Project Coordinator without claiming a classroom simulation was paid experience?

Write 80–120 words in English, Kannada or Hindi with clear technical labels. Keep a specific correction or decision in your portfolio journal.
