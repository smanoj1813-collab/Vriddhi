# 1.3 Stakeholders, RACI and communication

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Map stakeholder needs and assign decision responsibilities without overloading every meeting.
- Produce a reviewable stakeholder map and communication plan using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

The pilot has a sponsor, placement staff, a student accessibility representative, an IT administrator and a vendor. A status meeting has fifteen invitees but no one can say who accepts the booking workflow. An important accessibility concern arrives only on launch day.

The running organisation, Namma Campus Services, is fictional. All dates, costs, workloads and stakeholders in these exercises are synthetic. Work with role names rather than real student details. The case is deliberately small enough to model in a spreadsheet, but its approval, scheduling and communication problems resemble those encountered in entry-level coordination roles.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Interest, influence and impact are different

Map people by what they need, what decisions they can influence and how the change affects them. A low-power student can still face high impact and must have an accessible feedback route. Do not use an influence grid to silence affected groups. Stakeholder maps are working hypotheses; confirm needs through discussion and revise them as the project changes. Use role-based entries in classroom work instead of collecting real personal details.

### 2. RACI clarifies one deliverable at a time

Responsible means doing the work; Accountable means answerable for the decision or result; Consulted means input is sought; Informed means kept up to date. In this course, assign one accountable role per deliverable to avoid unresolved ownership. Several responsible roles can contribute, but someone coordinates them. RACI is an agreement to confirm with the people involved, not a way to appoint absent colleagues without their consent.

### 3. Communication is a designed service

Specify audience, information, format, cadence, sender and escalation trigger. A sponsor may need a weekly decision brief; a student representative may need a short prototype review before acceptance. Status meetings should produce decisions or actions with owners and dates, not merely oral task lists. Confidential commercial details do not belong in a public board. Select channels according to sensitivity and accessibility as well as convenience.

## Worked example

For pilot acceptance, the placement sponsor is Accountable, the coordinator Responsible for assembling evidence, the student representative and IT administrator Consulted, and participating staff Informed. If IT owns a separate security approval, give that approval its own row with its own accountable owner rather than forcing two unrelated decisions into one row.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: stakeholder map and communication plan (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. List five stakeholder roles, their needs and the impact of a failed rollout on each.

2. Create separate RACI rows for charter approval, workflow testing, security approval and handover.

3. Check that each row has one accountable role and that role has actual authority.

4. Design a weekly sponsor brief and a learner feedback route with different information levels.

5. Roleplay a disagreement and record a decision, an action owner and an escalation date.

**Deliverable:** submit your stakeholder map and communication plan, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Using power as the only reason to listen to someone.
- Putting several accountable roles in one ambiguous row.
- Copying confidential details into broad status messages.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Interest, influence and impact are different:** Map people by what they need, what decisions they can influence and how the change affects them.
- **RACI clarifies one deliverable at a time:** Responsible means doing the work; Accountable means answerable for the decision or result; Consulted means input is sought; Informed means kept up to date.
- **Communication is a designed service:** Specify audience, information, format, cadence, sender and escalation trigger.
