# 7.2 Vendor coordination and procurement basics

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Compare supplier offers and manage delivery evidence within approval and procurement rules.
- Produce a reviewable supplier comparison and delivery tracker using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

Two vendors quote for event check-in devices. One offer is cheaper but excludes setup and support; another includes both but requires an earlier deposit. A coordinator must compare like with like without awarding a contract outside their authority.

The running organisation, Namma Campus Services, is fictional. All dates, costs, workloads and stakeholders in these exercises are synthetic. Work with role names rather than real student details. The case is deliberately small enough to model in a spreadsheet, but its approval, scheduling and communication problems resemble those encountered in entry-level coordination roles.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Specify the need before seeking prices

Define required capabilities, acceptance evidence, service period, delivery location and constraints. A clear statement of work reduces mismatched offers. Follow the institution’s procurement thresholds and conflict-of-interest rules; this lesson does not authorise purchases. Keep suppliers’ confidential pricing in approved storage. Clarifications should be fair and documented rather than giving one supplier hidden information.

### 2. Compare total relevant cost and risk

Purchase price is only one element. Include delivery, setup, training, support, recurring charges and any applicable taxes under a consistent basis. Compare assumptions and exclusions explicitly. The cheapest offer can still be best, but only after assessing whether it meets requirements and whether risk is acceptable. Weighted scoring supports a recommendation; mandatory criteria and approval authority still apply.

### 3. Track obligations and acceptance

Record milestones, deliverables, acceptance conditions, invoice references and responsible roles. Do not certify receipt of a service that has not been verified. Handle scope changes through the contract’s authorised mechanism rather than casual chat messages. Escalate late delivery with evidence, alternatives and impact. A supplier relationship benefits from clarity and fairness, not from either blind trust or constant blame.

## Worked example

Vendor A quotes ₹18,000 plus ₹4,000 setup and ₹3,000 support, total ₹25,000 on the stated comparable basis. Vendor B quotes ₹24,000 including those services. If both meet mandatory requirements, B is ₹1,000 lower on this basis, but payment timing, service quality and exclusions still need review. The comparison is a recommendation, not a purchase order.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: supplier comparison and delivery tracker (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Write a short statement of work with measurable acceptance conditions.

2. Normalise the two quotes and list any tax or scope assumptions still unresolved.

3. Define three non-price criteria and separate mandatory requirements from preferences.

4. Create a delivery tracker with evidence and approval fields.

5. Draft a supplier-delay escalation that offers a fallback without making an unauthorised commitment.

**Deliverable:** submit your supplier comparison and delivery tracker, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing headline prices with different inclusions.
- Approving an invoice before verifying delivery.
- Treating a classroom supplier score as procurement authority.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Specify the need before seeking prices:** Define required capabilities, acceptance evidence, service period, delivery location and constraints.
- **Compare total relevant cost and risk:** Purchase price is only one element.
- **Track obligations and acceptance:** Record milestones, deliverables, acceptance conditions, invoice references and responsible roles.

## Reflection journal

2. Which assumption has the greatest effect on your recommendation, and how would you test it before a real decision?
3. How would you explain this work in a two-minute interview for Project Coordinator without claiming a classroom simulation was paid experience?
