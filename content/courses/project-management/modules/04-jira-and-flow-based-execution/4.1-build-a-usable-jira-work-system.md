# 4.1 Build a usable Jira work system

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Create a coherent issue hierarchy and workflow using Jira or an equivalent offline board.
- Produce a reviewable sandbox board specification and issue register using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

A project coordinator opens a Jira sandbox and sees different names for work items than a colleague’s site. Rather than memorising clicks, the team must represent the same project clearly: a larger outcome, small testable items, ownership, status and acceptance evidence.

The running organisation, Namma Campus Services, is fictional. All dates, costs, workloads and stakeholders in these exercises are synthetic. Work with role names rather than real student details. The case is deliberately small enough to model in a spreadsheet, but its approval, scheduling and communication problems resemble those encountered in entry-level coordination roles.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Represent work at useful levels

Jira configurations may use terms such as project or space, and issue or work item; permissions and templates vary. The concepts are stable: larger outcomes can group smaller stories or tasks, and subtasks can organise implementation work. An epic is not proof of strategic value, and a subtask is not automatically a usable deliverable. Choose a simple hierarchy that supports navigation without creating unnecessary administration.

### 2. Workflow states need entry and exit rules

A board column is meaningful only when people agree what it means. “In review” might require an attached test record and a named reviewer; “Done” might require accepted evidence and no unresolved blocking defects. Separate blocked information from completion status so a blocked task does not disappear. Jira workflow configuration does not create team agreement by itself. Test the rules with a small case before expanding the board.

### 3. Permissions and traceability matter

Use a sandbox with fictional data. Do not put passwords, personal student information or confidential contracts in descriptions, comments or attachments. Record a clear owner, acceptance criteria, dependency links and evidence location. Screen captures can support a portfolio only after redaction. If Jira access is unavailable, use a spreadsheet with the same columns and transitions; the learning objective is a reviewable work system, not possession of a paid account.

## Worked example

Create an epic “placement registration pilot” and three items: create a booking, cancel a booking and view remaining capacity. A task cannot move from Review to Done until a reviewer has checked the acceptance examples. An offline board can store ID, parent, title, owner role, status, blocker, due date and evidence link. It demonstrates the same control logic even without automated workflow enforcement.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: sandbox board specification and issue register (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Draw a hierarchy containing one epic, three stories or tasks and two useful subtasks.

2. Define To do, In progress, Review and Done with explicit transition evidence.

3. Create six fictional records in Jira or the supplied register template; link one dependency.

4. Move a sample item through the workflow and record why each transition is allowed.

5. Export or capture a redacted board and include a permission/access note.

**Deliverable:** submit your sandbox board specification and issue register, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming every Jira site has the same menus or issue names.
- Moving cards to Done without evidence.
- Putting personal or confidential data in a public sandbox.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Represent work at useful levels:** Jira configurations may use terms such as project or space, and issue or work item; permissions and templates vary.
- **Workflow states need entry and exit rules:** A board column is meaningful only when people agree what it means.
- **Permissions and traceability matter:** Use a sandbox with fictional data.

## Reflection journal

1. What did you change after checking the example or receiving a review comment? Name the exact input, statement or calculation.
2. Which assumption has the greatest effect on your recommendation, and how would you test it before a real decision?
3. How would you explain this work in a two-minute interview for Project Coordinator without claiming a classroom simulation was paid experience?

Write 80–120 words in English, Kannada or Hindi with clear technical labels. Keep a specific correction or decision in your portfolio journal.
