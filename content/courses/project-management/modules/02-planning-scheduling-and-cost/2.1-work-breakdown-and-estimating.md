# 2.1 Work breakdown and estimating

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Decompose deliverables into estimable work packages and distinguish effort from duration.
- Produce a reviewable work breakdown structure and estimate sheet using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

A coordinator lists “design, build, test” and promises the placement pilot in two weeks. The list hides user testing, translation, staff training and permission checks. Two volunteers each have only six hours per week; neither is available full time.

The running organisation, Namma Campus Services, is fictional. All dates, costs, workloads and stakeholders in these exercises are synthetic. Work with role names rather than real student details. The case is deliberately small enough to model in a spreadsheet, but its approval, scheduling and communication problems resemble those encountered in entry-level coordination roles.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Start with deliverables, then work

A work breakdown structure decomposes the agreed scope into manageable pieces. Begin with the booking workflow, training materials, test evidence and handover package, then identify the work required for each. The coverage check is that all approved scope is represented without double counting. A WBS is not a schedule: it shows what must be delivered, while dependencies and dates explain when and in what order.

### 2. Estimate with assumptions and ranges

Estimate small work packages using comparable past work, informed judgement or a three-point range. A number without a basis invites false certainty. Record what is included, who estimated it, the skill assumptions and what could make it vary. Do not add the same contingency in every task and then again at the whole-project level without explaining it. A range can support a better decision than a precise but unsupported deadline.

### 3. Effort does not equal elapsed time

Effort is person-hours of work; duration is elapsed working time. Twelve hours of effort at three available hours per day needs at least four working days before dependencies and interruptions. Two people do not necessarily halve duration: some tasks are sequential and collaboration creates overhead. Confirm availability with the person’s manager. Do not plan someone at full capacity across two projects simply because both schedules look feasible separately.

## Worked example

A training guide is estimated at 6 optimistic, 9 most likely and 18 pessimistic hours. A simple triangular average is (6 + 9 + 18)/3 = 11 hours. A weighted PERT estimate is (6 + 4×9 + 18)/6 = 10 hours. These are different conventions; state which you used. At two available hours daily, ten hours of effort requires five working days.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: work breakdown structure and estimate sheet (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Break the pilot into at least eight work packages grouped by deliverable.

2. For each package record owner role, estimate basis, exclusions and acceptance evidence.

3. Estimate the training guide using both conventions and label the result.

4. Convert two effort estimates into durations using explicit daily availability.

5. Identify a missing piece of scope and revise the WBS without double counting it.

**Deliverable:** submit your work breakdown structure and estimate sheet, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Using task names as proof that all scope is covered.
- Treating estimated hours as calendar days.
- Assuming two people always work twice as fast.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Start with deliverables, then work:** A work breakdown structure decomposes the agreed scope into manageable pieces.
- **Estimate with assumptions and ranges:** Estimate small work packages using comparable past work, informed judgement or a three-point range.
- **Effort does not equal elapsed time:** Effort is person-hours of work; duration is elapsed working time.
