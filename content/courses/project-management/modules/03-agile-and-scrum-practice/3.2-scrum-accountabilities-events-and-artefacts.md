# 3.2 Scrum accountabilities, events and artefacts

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Explain Scrum accountabilities and the purpose of its events, artefacts and commitments.
- Produce a reviewable Scrum simulation board and event map using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

A team labels its weekly staff meeting a Daily Scrum and asks the sponsor to assign every task. The board is busy, but there is no Product Goal, no Sprint Goal and no shared understanding of Done. Learn the framework before borrowing its vocabulary.

The running organisation, Namma Campus Services, is fictional. All dates, costs, workloads and stakeholders in these exercises are synthetic. Work with role names rather than real student details. The case is deliberately small enough to model in a spreadsheet, but its approval, scheduling and communication problems resemble those encountered in entry-level coordination roles.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Three accountabilities, not a command chain

The Scrum Team contains a Product Owner, a Scrum Master and Developers. The Product Owner is accountable for maximising product value and effective Product Backlog management. Developers create a usable Increment and manage their plan. The Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum and helping improve effectiveness. These accountabilities do not make the Scrum Master a task-dispatch manager or make the Product Owner the boss of every technical decision.

### 2. Events create opportunities to inspect and adapt

A Sprint is a fixed-length event of one month or less containing the other events. Sprint Planning establishes the Sprint Goal and plan; the Daily Scrum is a 15-minute event for Developers to inspect progress and adapt their plan. The Sprint Review inspects the outcome with stakeholders; the Retrospective plans improvements in quality and effectiveness. The Review is not a mandatory release gate, and refinement is an ongoing activity rather than an official Scrum event.

### 3. Artefacts have commitments

The Product Backlog is linked to the Product Goal, the Sprint Backlog to the Sprint Goal, and the Increment to the Definition of Done. These commitments support transparency and focus. An Increment must be usable and meet the Definition of Done. Work that does not meet Done is not part of the Increment merely because someone moved a card. During a Sprint, scope can be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as learning occurs without endangering the Sprint Goal.

## Worked example

A two-week Sprint Goal is “students can book and cancel a placement slot safely”. The team selects three backlog items and discovers an access-control defect. It can negotiate removing a low-value optional reminder while protecting the goal, but it cannot declare the unsafe workflow Done. A Review can gather feedback even when some planned items remain unfinished; unfinished work must be represented honestly.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: Scrum simulation board and event map (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Assign the three accountabilities to role cards for a classroom simulation.

2. Map each event to its purpose, participants and output rather than only a meeting time.

3. Write a Product Goal, Sprint Goal and a short Definition of Done for the pilot.

4. Simulate a mid-Sprint defect and negotiate scope while preserving the goal.

5. Explain the difference between a Sprint Review and a Retrospective using separate agenda items.

**Deliverable:** submit your Scrum simulation board and event map, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Making the Daily Scrum a sponsor status report.
- Calling refinement an additional official Scrum event.
- Treating the Review as the only time a usable Increment can be released.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Three accountabilities, not a command chain:** The Scrum Team contains a Product Owner, a Scrum Master and Developers.
- **Events create opportunities to inspect and adapt:** A Sprint is a fixed-length event of one month or less containing the other events.
- **Artefacts have commitments:** The Product Backlog is linked to the Product Goal, the Sprint Backlog to the Sprint Goal, and the Increment to the Definition of Done.
