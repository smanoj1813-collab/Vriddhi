# 8.3 Demo Day, feedback and professional development

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Present an integrated delivery simulation and turn feedback into a focused development plan.
- Produce a reviewable capstone presentation and development plan using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

The capstone jury has five minutes for your presentation and five minutes for questions. They cannot inspect every file while you speak. You need to show the decision path, reveal limitations and demonstrate that you can reproduce the key calculations.

The running organisation, Namma Campus Services, is fictional. All dates, costs, workloads and stakeholders in these exercises are synthetic. Work with role names rather than real student details. The case is deliberately small enough to model in a spreadsheet, but its approval, scheduling and communication problems resemble those encountered in entry-level coordination roles.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Build a decision narrative

Use five slides or sections: problem and boundaries, plan and delivery approach, a disruption and decision, evidence of acceptance and handover, and learning. Every claim should be traceable to the portfolio. Select the details that explain judgement, not every task completed. Show the synthetic-data label prominently. A clear explanation of an unresolved limitation is better than an unsupported claim of flawless execution.

### 2. Prepare for evidence questions

Expect to reproduce a dependency calculation, a cost forecast and a risk response. Know which file contains the source values and which assumption is most sensitive. If a question goes beyond the evidence, say so and describe how you would investigate. The presentation is not an external professional certification exam, and completing this course does not confer Scrum, Jira or PMI credentials.

### 3. Turn feedback into practice

Separate feedback about content accuracy, communication and tool fluency. Choose a small number of actions with practice tasks, evidence and review dates. For example, rebuild a schedule from a new case, audit a dashboard filter or facilitate a short decision meeting. Avoid a vague plan to “become an expert”. Map each action to an entry-level responsibility and seek supervised experience rather than exaggerating current competence.

## Worked example

A reviewer finds that your EAC uses a baseline remainder instead of the latest estimate to complete. A useful response is to show the formula, acknowledge the mismatch, recompute from actual cost and the updated remaining estimate, and record the correction. A development action is to reconcile three new forecast cases and have a peer check them next week, not simply to add more animation to the slides.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: capstone presentation and development plan (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Create a five-minute presentation using the five-section narrative.

2. Rehearse reproducing one schedule and one cost calculation without reading the answer.

3. Prepare responses to a scope change, a critical defect and a missing approval.

4. Capture jury feedback as specific correction or practice actions.

5. Write a thirty-day development plan with two tasks, evidence and review dates.

**Deliverable:** submit your capstone presentation and development plan, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing a course completion certificate with an external credential.
- Responding to an evidence gap with confident invention.
- Making a development plan entirely about presentation design.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Build a decision narrative:** Use five slides or sections: problem and boundaries, plan and delivery approach, a disruption and decision, evidence of acceptance and handover, and learning.
- **Prepare for evidence questions:** Expect to reproduce a dependency calculation, a cost forecast and a risk response.
- **Turn feedback into practice:** Separate feedback about content accuracy, communication and tool fluency.

## Reflection journal

1. What did you change after checking the example or receiving a review comment? Name the exact input, statement or calculation.
2. Which assumption has the greatest effect on your recommendation, and how would you test it before a real decision?
3. How would you explain this work in a two-minute interview for Project Coordinator without claiming a classroom simulation was paid experience?

Write 80–120 words in English, Kannada or Hindi with clear technical labels. Keep a specific correction or decision in your portfolio journal.
