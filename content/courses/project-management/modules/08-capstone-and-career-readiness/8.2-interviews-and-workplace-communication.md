# 8.2 Interviews and workplace communication

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Answer coordination, PMO and operations scenarios using evidence, trade-offs and appropriate authority.
- Produce a reviewable interview response and first-week plan using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

An interviewer asks what you would do if a supplier missed a date and the sponsor demanded a green report. A memorised definition of project management will not answer the question. The interviewer is testing judgement, communication and integrity.

The running organisation, Namma Campus Services, is fictional. All dates, costs, workloads and stakeholders in these exercises are synthetic. Work with role names rather than real student details. The case is deliberately small enough to model in a spreadsheet, but its approval, scheduling and communication problems resemble those encountered in entry-level coordination roles.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Use structure without scripting away the facts

STAR means Situation, Task, Action and Result; add a reflection when useful. Keep the situation brief, describe your responsibility and make actions specific. For a hypothetical case, say what you would do rather than claiming it happened. Do not invent metrics to make a result sound impressive. Explain how you would verify the impact and who has authority for the next decision.

### 2. Diagnose before proposing a solution

Clarify the deadline, dependency, impact, available alternatives and current approval rules. Separate known facts from estimates. Present options with consequences and a recommendation. For a delayed vendor, you might check the recovery plan, evaluate a fallback and escalate a milestone risk. Do not promise a new date or approve extra spend without evidence and authority. Honest status is more useful than a demanded colour.

### 3. Connect skills to entry-level responsibilities

Project Coordinators maintain plans, actions and communication; PMO Analysts reconcile reporting and support governance; Operations Associates run and improve repeatable services. Titles vary by employer, so read the actual job description. A first-week plan should include learning the workflow, confirming stakeholders, checking data definitions and understanding approval boundaries. It should not promise to redesign the organisation before listening.

## Worked example

A strong answer is “I would confirm the supplier’s latest committed date, identify the affected milestone and compare recovery options. I would apply the agreed RAG threshold, not change it under pressure, and escalate the decision required with evidence.” It recognises urgency while preserving truthful reporting. A weak answer is “I would mark it green and work harder” because it hides the decision risk.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: interview response and first-week plan (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Choose one job description for each target role or use the role summaries here.

2. Prepare one STAR response from a course artefact and one hypothetical delay response.

3. Record a two-minute answer that distinguishes your authority from the sponsor’s.

4. Ask a partner to challenge the evidence, trade-off and decision route.

5. Draft a first-week plan with three discovery questions and two low-risk contributions.

**Deliverable:** submit your interview response and first-week plan, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Giving only definitions instead of actions.
- Inventing a numeric result for a hypothetical case.
- Promising authority or expertise beyond the entry-level role.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Use structure without scripting away the facts:** STAR means Situation, Task, Action and Result; add a reflection when useful.
- **Diagnose before proposing a solution:** Clarify the deadline, dependency, impact, available alternatives and current approval rules.
- **Connect skills to entry-level responsibilities:** Project Coordinators maintain plans, actions and communication; PMO Analysts reconcile reporting and support governance; Operations Associates run and improve repeatable services.

## Reflection journal

1. What did you change after checking the example or receiving a review comment? Name the exact input, statement or calculation.
2. Which assumption has the greatest effect on your recommendation, and how would you test it before a real decision?
3. How would you explain this work in a two-minute interview for Project Coordinator without claiming a classroom simulation was paid experience?

Write 80–120 words in English, Kannada or Hindi with clear technical labels. Keep a specific correction or decision in your portfolio journal.
