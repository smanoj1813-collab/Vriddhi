# 2.3 Technical and local SEO

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Explain what makes a site technically healthy: reachable, fast, mobile-first and indexable.
- Use Google Search Console to see a site exactly as Google sees it.
- Set up and optimise a Google Business Profile for local visibility.
- Run a technical checklist and prioritise fixes by impact and effort.

## Opening scenario

A Dharwad physiotherapy clinic had spent a year writing good content. It still did not appear for "physiotherapy clinic Dharwad". A student spent one evening in Google Search Console and found the problem in fifteen minutes: the site had accidentally left "Discourage search engines" on — a single checkbox from the website-builder days. Every page was healthy, well-written and invisible.

Technical SEO is unglamorous plumbing: if it works, nobody notices; if it breaks, nothing else matters. Local SEO is its cousin — being findable for "near me" intent — and for most Indian small businesses it is the single highest-return channel they will ever set up. Both are learnable in an evening, exactly as this lesson does.

## Core ideas

### 1. What "technically healthy" means

Four tests, in order:

1. **Reachable:** Can crawlers and humans reach every page? No orphan pages (unlinked), no dead links, a working navigation and a sitemap submitted in Search Console.
2. **Fast:** Does the page load quickly on a mid-range phone on 4G? India's mobile-first reality makes this a ranking factor and a business factor — a page that takes five seconds on Jio loses a third of its visitors. Big uncompressed images are the usual culprit.
3. **Mobile-friendly:** Google indexes the mobile version of a site first. Tiny tap targets, horizontal scrolling and pop-ups that cannot be dismissed are both ranking risks and lost customers.
4. **Indexable:** One clear version of each page. Watch for duplicate content at www vs non-www, http vs https, and "Discourage search engines" left on. Redirects (301) should point old URLs to new ones, not into the void.

### 2. Google Search Console: the free window into Google's brain

Search Console is Google telling you, for free, how it sees your site:

- **Coverage / Pages report:** which URLs are indexed, which are excluded and why (the clinic's nightmare lives here).
- **Performance report:** the queries your site appears for, positions, clicks — real demand data for your keyword map.
- **Sitemaps:** where you submit your sitemap so discovery is guaranteed.
- **URL inspection:** "how does Google see this exact page right now?" — the first tool to open on any SEO problem.
- **Core Web Vitals / Mobile usability:** the speed and experience reports.

Verify ownership once (a small code record or file), and Search Console becomes the diagnostic backbone of every audit you will ever run.

### 3. Local SEO and the Google Business Profile

For "near me" searches, Google shows the **map pack** — the three businesses with pins — above ordinary organic results. Ranking in the map pack is decided by three things: **relevance** (your profile matches the query), **distance** (how close you are to the searcher) and **prominence** (how known and well-reviewed you are). You control relevance and heavily influence prominence:

- **Claim and complete every field:** category (choose the primary one carefully), hours, services with prices, service area, description with natural local keywords, attributes (wheelchair access, women-owned), photos.
- **Posts and offers:** regular updates keep the profile alive and give searchers a reason to act.
- **Reviews:** ask every happy customer, reply to every review (kindly to the angry ones — prospective customers read your replies, not just the stars), and never buy fake reviews.
- **NAP consistency:** your Name, Address, Phone must be identical on Google, your site, Justdial, IndiaMART and anywhere else you are listed. Inconsistent details split your identity across listings.

Local intent is the highest-converting intent there is: someone searching "cake shop near me" is usually standing somewhere hungry.

### 4. Prioritising fixes: impact × effort

A technical audit that lists forty issues is a report; an audit that says "do these three first" is a plan. Score every finding: **High impact / low effort** = do now (the noindex checkbox, missing title tags, the unclaimed profile). **High impact / high effort** = plan and budget (site speed overhaul). **Low impact** = ignore for now, no matter how much the checklist item itches. This prioritisation discipline is what Project Block 1 grades most heavily.

## Try it now

Search "best [your favourite food] near me" and study the map pack. Open each of the three profiles: how complete are they? How recent are the reviews, and do the owners reply? You are seeing local SEO's whole game in one screen.

## Hands-on lab: technical + local health check (45 min)

1. **Speed and mobile (10 min):** Run your lab business site (or any local site) through Google's free PageSpeed test on mobile. Note the score and the top two opportunities (usually images or fonts).
2. **Search Console orientation (10 min):** If the site has Search Console, open the Pages report and find one exclusion that surprises you. If it does not, use the URL inspection on the home page of any site to see what data Google exposes.
3. **Business Profile audit (15 min):** Find the business on Google Maps. Score it: complete or incomplete fields (hours, services, prices), photos (how many, how fresh), reviews (count, recency, owner replies), posts (any?). List the five missing fields you would fill first.
4. **NAP check (5 min):** Compare name/address/phone across Google, the website and one directory. Note any mismatch.
5. **Prioritise (5 min):** Write the top three fixes in impact × effort order, one line each.

> **Deliverable:** a technical + local checklist with scores, five profile fixes and a prioritised top-three list — a required section of Project Block 1.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing more content on a broken site.** Content cannot rank if the site is noindexed, slow or unreachable.
- **Setting up the profile once and never touching it again.** Map-pack prominence rewards activity: posts, photos, replies.
- **Buying or swapping fake reviews.** Platforms detect patterns; the penalty can remove the profile entirely — the opposite of the plan.
- **Treating speed as a developer problem.** Compressing images and choosing sensible fonts is usually a marketing-team fix achievable in an afternoon.

## Key takeaways

- Technical health = reachable, fast on mobile, mobile-friendly, indexable — in that order of blame.
- Search Console shows you your site as Google sees it; the Pages and Performance reports anchor every audit.
- The Google Business Profile drives the map pack: completeness, activity and genuine reviews win.
- Prioritise findings by impact × effort; a good audit names the first three actions, not all forty problems.
