# 2.1 How search works and keyword research

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Explain in plain language how search engines crawl, index and rank pages.
- Classify search intent as informational, navigational, commercial or transactional.
- Build a keyword map using free tools: autocomplete, People Also Ask, Google Trends and Keyword Planner.
- Assign each keyword to a page on a site instead of collecting keyword lists that go nowhere.

## Opening scenario

Two classmates optimise the same bakery website. The first targets the word "cake" — huge search volume, he says, so it must be valuable. The second spends an hour finding phrases like "eggless birthday cake Tumakuru" and "custom photo cake under 800 rupees". Six weeks later the bakery ranks on page one for three of her phrases and gets weekly enquiries. His page, chasing "cake", is on page nine, competing with national portals and recipe sites.

The difference was not effort — he worked hard. It was understanding that search is a **matching engine between someone's intent and a page that answers it**. Keyword research is how you find out, in the customer's own words, what intents exist — and pick the ones you can actually win.

## Core ideas

### 1. Crawl, index, rank

A search engine works in three stages:

1. **Crawl:** automated visitors ("crawlers" or "spiders") follow links and sitemaps to discover pages. A page no crawler can reach — unlinked, blocked by robots.txt, or behind a form — is invisible.
2. **Index:** the engine reads and stores each page's content, understanding what it is about. Duplicate, empty or near-identical pages dilute your presence in the index.
3. **Rank:** for every query, the engine scores which indexed pages best answer it — considering relevance (does the page match the words and the intent?), authority (do trusted sources link to it?), and experience (is the page fast, mobile-friendly and pleasant?).

You cannot pay Google to rank organically; "SEO" is the craft of making pages that deserve to rank for queries you care about. Paid results at the top of the page are a separate system (Module 4) — the map pack, organic listings and ads all live on the same results page and all matter.

### 2. Intent beats volume

Every query carries an intent — what the searcher wants to happen next:

| Intent | The searcher wants | Example | Best page type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Informational** | To learn or understand | "how to store fondant cake" | Guide, article, video |
| **Navigational** | One specific place | "Baskin Robbins Jayanagara" | Home page / business profile |
| **Commercial** | To compare before deciding | "best eggless cake Tumakuru" or "cake shop reviews" | Comparison list, review, category page |
| **Transactional** | To act now | "order photo cake Tumakuru delivery" | Product / order page with clear action |

The fatal beginner mistake is matching a high-volume word with the wrong page type — targeting "cake" (informational, national) with an order page. Small local businesses win by owning specific commercial and transactional phrases with clear local words: the "keyword research" effort is 90% intent mapping and 10% volume hunting.

### 3. The free keyword research workflow

1. **Seed list (10 phrases).** Write what customers actually say — from your Lesson 1.3 interviews and reviews. Include the city and the products.
2. **Expand with autocomplete and People Also Ask.** Type each seed into Google and harvest the completions; open the People Also Ask boxes for question phrasing. Do the same on YouTube for video-intent topics.
3. **Check interest with Google Trends.** Compare phrases over time and by region — you will discover seasonal swings (cake spikes in November–December) and regional vocabulary differences.
4. **Estimate volume and difficulty with Keyword Planner.** Google Ads' free planner gives ranges (not exact numbers — that is fine; ranges are enough for priorities). Ubersuggest's free tier offers similar estimates. Expect buckets like "100–1k" — decisions need ranges, not decimals.
5. **Score and select.** For each phrase note: intent, volume bucket, difficulty, and whether your business can genuinely serve it. Keep a mix: two or three "money" transactional phrases, several commercial phrases, and a few informational ones to build topical authority.
6. **Map to pages.** This is where research becomes SEO: one primary keyword (plus close variants) → one page. "Order eggless cake Tumakuru" → the order page. "Eggless cake price list" → a category/pricing page. Never send two pages after the same phrase — they will compete with each other ("keyword cannibalisation").

### 4. The keyword map: the actual deliverable

A keyword map is a table, not a list. Columns: **Keyword | Intent | Volume bucket | Difficulty | Target page | Notes**. When it is finished, every important page on the site has a reason to exist, and every phrase has a home. This table becomes the backbone of your SEO audit in Project Block 1 — you will compare the phrases a site *should* own against what it actually has.

## Hands-on lab: keyword map for your lab business (45 min)

1. **Seed from the customer's mouth (10 min):** From your 1.3 dossier, list 10 seed phrases exactly as customers say them (include city, include product words).
2. **Expand (15 min):** For each of five seeds, harvest autocomplete suggestions and two People Also Ask questions. Aim for 25–40 total phrases.
3. **Classify (10 min):** Label each phrase with intent (I/N/C/T) and note the best page type. Mark the three most valuable transactional/commercial phrases with a star.
4. **Estimate and select (5 min):** Put the starred phrases through Keyword Planner or Ubersuggest; record volume buckets and difficulty. Drop phrases you cannot realistically win (difficulty far beyond a small site).
5. **Map (5 min):** Assign each kept phrase to an existing page (URL) or a "new page needed" note.

> **Deliverable:** a keyword map table with at least 15 phrases, fully classified, with page assignments. This table is a required section of Project Block 1 (SEO Audit).

## Common mistakes

- **Chasing volume.** A phrase with 90 searches a month that converts weekly beats a 90,000-volume phrase you will never rank for.
- **Ignoring intent.** Matching an informational phrase with a sales page frustrates the searcher and the algorithm.
- **One page, many keywords.** Trying to rank a single page for ten unrelated phrases produces a page that ranks for none.
- **Keyword lists that end in a drawer.** Unmapped keywords are trivia; the map is the work.

## Key takeaways

- Search engines crawl, index and rank; your job is to be reachable, understandable and genuinely the best answer.
- Intent (informational, navigational, commercial, transactional) decides what kind of page should target a phrase.
- Free tools — autocomplete, People Also Ask, Trends, Keyword Planner — are enough for a professional keyword map.
- One primary keyword per page, mapped in a table, is what turns research into rankings.
