# 1.2 Customer journeys and funnels

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Describe the customer journey from first awareness to advocacy as a series of questions the customer is asking.
- Draw a funnel with realistic stage conversion rates and do the arithmetic from traffic to customers.
- Explain why funnels leak, and name the fixes for each stage.
- Match marketing channels and content to the journey stage they serve.

## Opening scenario

Meera, a final-year B.Com student in Hubballi, wants running shoes. Her journey: a reel about "budget running shoes under ₹3,000" catches her eye while she scrolls (awareness). She searches "best running shoes under 3000" and opens three articles (consideration). She adds one pair to a cart on two different sites (intent). One site emails her for three days; the other stays silent. She buys from the one that answered her size question in the email (purchase). Two weeks later she leaves a review and answers a friend's "which shoes?" question on WhatsApp (advocacy).

Two brands spent money to reach her. Only one had a map of where she was. The funnel is that map — and the arithmetic behind it decides budgets, targets and expectations for everything you will do in this course.

## Core ideas

### 1. The journey is a series of customer questions

Behind every stage of the journey is a question the customer is silently asking:

| Stage | Customer's real question | What earns the next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Awareness** | "This exists? Interesting." | A clear, specific promise where they already are |
| **Consideration** | "Is this right for me? Better than the others?" | Honest comparisons, proof, reviews |
| **Conversion** | "Is it safe/easy to go ahead?" | Frictionless flow, trust signals, one clear action |
| **Retention** | "Was it as good as promised?" | Delivery, support, useful follow-up |
| **Advocacy** | "Should I tell others?" | An easy, rewarded moment to share |

When a campaign "isn't working", it is usually answering the wrong question for its stage — showing a price before the customer believes the problem is understood, or asking for a purchase before trust exists.

### 2. The funnel and its arithmetic

A funnel is the journey with numbers attached. The classic shape:

**Traffic → Enquiry/Lead → Qualified → Customer → Repeat customer**

Marketing runs on ratios between stages. You do not need statistics — you need honest multiplication:

```
1000 visitors
× 3% enquiry rate          = 30 enquiries
× 40% qualified rate       = 12 serious prospects
× 25% close rate           = 3 customers
```

Three customers from a thousand visitors can be a triumph or a disaster depending on the value of a customer and the cost of the traffic. Which leads to the only question that makes a funnel useful:

**Cost per acquisition (CPA) = spend ÷ customers** and it must sit below what a customer is worth (**LTV**, lifetime value). A ₹400 CPA for a ₹299 monthly subscription that customers keep for two years is a good trade; the same ₹400 to sell one ₹499 product once is a loss.

### 3. Why funnels leak — and the patch for each hole

- **Top leaks (awareness):** wrong audience, boring hook, or a channel where your customer simply is not. *Fix:* revisit research (Lesson 1.3), sharpen the promise.
- **Middle leaks (consideration):** traffic arrives and finds no answers — thin content, no proof, no comparison. *Fix:* content that answers the stage's real question.
- **Bottom leaks (conversion):** slow pages, forms asking for too much, no trust signals, price surprise at checkout, a call-to-action the customer cannot find. *Fix:* remove steps, add proof, show the total early.
- **After-purchase leaks (retention):** silence after payment. No onboarding, no follow-up, complaint handled badly in public. *Fix:* the email and CRM module (Module 7) exists for this.

The habit to build now: when numbers disappoint, **do not reach for more traffic first**. Fixing a 1% to 2% conversion rate doubles results at zero extra spend.

### 4. Channels map to stages

- **Awareness:** social organic and paid social, display and video, influencer content, PR.
- **Consideration:** SEO articles and guides, YouTube explainers, comparison pages, reviews, retargeting ads ("you viewed this").
- **Conversion:** search ads on high-intent keywords, landing pages, email to cart-abandoners, WhatsApp follow-ups.
- **Retention and advocacy:** email newsletters and journeys, communities, loyalty and referral nudges.

This is why Module 1 said channels assist each other: social creates the awareness that search converts; email harvests the relationship that social began.

Recall your last online purchase above ₹1,000. Write the five stages and, next to each, what the brand did (or failed to do) for you at that stage. Where was the funnel weakest — and did the brand lose you or keep you because of it?

## Hands-on lab: funnel maths for a coaching centre (45 min)

A Bengaluru coaching centre sells a ₹25,000 CA-Foundation course. Average student stays one year. They plan to spend ₹60,000 on a two-month admission campaign.

1. **Draw the funnel (10 min):** Traffic → Enquiry form/WhatsApp → Counselling call → Visit/Demo → Admission. Put an empty box for the rate between each stage and for final admissions.
2. **Run three scenarios (15 min):** Pessimistic (2% enquiry, 50% call show-up, 40% demo attendance, 25% admission from demo), Expected (4%, 60%, 50%, 30%), Best (6%, 70%, 60%, 35%). Compute admissions and CPA for each. (Expected scenario: 60,000/4% click cost is not needed — assume spend buys 20,000 visitors and compute from there.)
3. **Sanity-check against value (10 min):** With ₹25,000 revenue per admission, which scenarios are profitable? How much headroom is there for a second year of study (LTV)?
4. **Find the leverage (10 min):** Which single rate, if improved by 10 percentage points, changes profit most? Write one sentence recommending where the centre should focus.

> **Deliverable:** the funnel with three computed scenarios, CPAs, and your one-sentence leverage recommendation. Keep the sheet — Project Block 3 asks for the same arithmetic.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming linear journeys.** Real customers loop, stall, and return weeks later. The funnel is a budgeting tool, not a description of human behaviour.
- **Only measuring the top.** Likes and reach are awareness-stage inputs; a business lives at the bottom of the funnel.
- **Optimising stages that are not the bottleneck.** Doubling traffic into a leaky conversion stage just doubles the waste.
- **Ignoring existing customers.** Retention and advocacy are the cheapest "channel" most businesses never build.

## Key takeaways

- Every journey stage answers a customer question; marketing fails when it answers the wrong one.
- Funnels are arithmetic: rates between stages, CPA against customer value, decide everything.
- Fix leaks before buying traffic — conversion improvements are usually cheaper than awareness.
- Channels have home stages: social for awareness, search and email for conversion, email and community for retention.
