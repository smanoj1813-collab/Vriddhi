# 1.1 What digital marketing really is

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Define digital marketing as a measurable system for attracting, converting and keeping customers — not "posting on Instagram".
- Sort any marketing touchpoint into owned, earned and paid media, and explain how the three reinforce each other.
- Describe the job of each major channel: search, social, content, email and advertising.
- Choose a sensible starting channel mix for a small business using its goal and its budget.

## Opening scenario

Ashok runs a twelve-seat idli-and-filter-coffee kitchen in Jayanagara, Bengaluru. His nephew told him: "Uncle, you must do digital marketing," and put the café on Instagram. For six months someone posted photos of sambar whenever there was time. Likes came from relatives. Orders did not change.

Then a final-year BBA student on a college project sat with Ashok for an hour and asked different questions. Who actually buys here? Office workers between 8 and 10 a.m. Do they search or scroll? They search — "breakfast near Jayanagara 4th block". Who already loves you? Forty regulars on WhatsApp. The student's plan had three lines: a Google Business Profile filled with photos, hours and menu so the café appears in those searches; a WhatsApp broadcast with a weekly breakfast combo for the regulars; and ₹150 a day on search ads targeting a 2 km radius at breakfast hours. Two months later, morning orders were up by roughly a third.

Same café, same city, same internet. What changed was thinking in a **system**: where the customers already are, what job each channel does, and what gets measured. That system is digital marketing. Posting was only activity; this was marketing.

## Core ideas

### 1. The definition that matters: attract, convert, keep — measurably

Marketing has always done three things: make people aware, help them decide, and keep them coming back. Digital marketing does the same through internet-connected channels, with one decisive difference — **you can measure almost everything**, so you can improve it. A hoarding on Hosur Road cannot tell you how many people looked at it, acted on it, or bought because of it. A search ad, an email, a landing page and a dashboard can. In this course, every campaign you build ends in numbers: how many saw, clicked, signed up, bought, came back. If you cannot measure it, you cannot defend it in a budget meeting.

### 2. Owned, earned and paid — the three kinds of media

Every digital touchpoint belongs to one of three buckets, and healthy marketing uses all three:

| Bucket | You control it? | Examples | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Owned** | Fully | Your website, blog, app, email list, WhatsApp community | Compounds over time; no per-use cost | Needs patience and consistency to grow |
| **Earned** | Not directly | Google rankings, reviews, shares, mentions, press | Most trusted by customers; free | Slow, cannot be switched on |
| **Paid** | Yes, per rupee | Search ads, social ads, display, influencer fees | Instant, precisely targeted | Stops the moment you stop paying |

Ashok's Google Business Profile is earned-and-owned (Google hosts it, he fills it); his WhatsApp list is owned; his ₹150/day is paid. A campaign that runs only on paid media is renting growth. A campaign that builds owned and earned assets keeps paying after the invoice is forgotten. Most small-business mistakes in India come from over-paying for what they could own.

### 3. The channels and their actual jobs

- **Search (SEO and SEM):** answers a question the customer is asking *right now* — "best B.Com college in Mysuru", "iPhone screen repair near me". Highest intent; the customer arrives already shopping.
- **Content (blogs, guides, videos):** earns attention and trust over weeks; it is the reason search engines rank you and the material your emails and social accounts share.
- **Social:** builds familiarity and community where people already spend hours; brilliant for discovery and for proof (reviews, reels, conversations), weaker at closing.
- **Email and messaging:** your only channel that you truly own; converts and retains like nothing else, because the audience opted in.
- **Paid media (SEM, social, display):** a multiplier — puts the right message in front of the right person at the right time, and buys the data that teaches your other channels what works.

Notice the pattern: **channels do not compete, they assist.** Social teaches you which messages work; you put the winner into paid; the traffic lands on content; the email list captures it; email brings people back.

### 4. Always-on vs campaign

Digital marketing runs in two rhythms at once. **Always-on marketing** — the business profile, the SEO'd pages, the weekly newsletter, the community replies — compounds quietly every day. **Campaigns** are focused bursts with a goal and an end date: admissions season, Diwali sale, a new course launch. A common student mistake is to design campaigns for a business that has no always-on foundation — like advertising a shop with no signboard. In your capstone you will run both.

## Try it now

Pick any brand you interact with weekly — your bank, a food-delivery app, your college. Make three columns: owned, earned, paid. In ten minutes, list at least six touchpoints in the right column. Then ask: which bucket is this brand weakest in, and what would you add first?

## Hands-on lab: channel map for a real business (45 min)

Choose a real small business you can visit or call — a café, a tuition centre, a boutique, a clinic near your home.

1. **Inventory (10 min):** Find every digital presence it has — website, Google Business Profile, Instagram, WhatsApp number, listings on Justdial or Zomato, reviews. Screenshot or note each.
2. **Classify (10 min):** Sort each presence into owned / earned / paid in a three-column sheet.
3. **Diagnose (10 min):** For each channel write one sentence: what job is it doing, and is it doing it? (Example: "Instagram — exists, but last post was four months ago, so it does discovery for nobody.")
4. **Recommend (10 min):** Write the first three actions you would take, in order, with a one-line reason each. Rank by impact-per-effort, not by what is trendy.
5. **Wrap-up (5 min):** Write the business's single most important missing asset — usually an owned one, like a claimed Google profile or an email/WhatsApp list.

> **Deliverable:** a one-page channel map (three columns, at least eight touchpoints) plus three prioritised recommendations. You will reuse this business in later labs, so choose one you can keep visiting.

## Common mistakes

- **Activity ≠ marketing.** Posting regularly proves effort, not effect. Always tie activity to a measurable job.
- **Channel worship.** "Our customers are on Instagram, so we should only do Instagram" ignores the customer who searches Google at the moment of need.
- **Paid-first thinking.** Ads send traffic somewhere. If the somewhere (page, profile, listing) is weak, the money is wasted.
- **Copying big brands.** A ₹500-crore brand can afford awareness theatre. A local business needs response, this week.

## Key takeaways

- Digital marketing is the measurable system of attracting, converting and keeping customers through digital channels.
- Owned media compounds, earned media convinces, paid media accelerates; strong plans use all three.
- Every channel has a job: search captures intent, content earns trust, social builds community, email retains, paid multiplies.
- Always-on foundations come before campaigns, and every action must end in a number you can check.

## Reflection journal

Write 150–200 words: think of a local business you know well. Which of its channels have you personally responded to, and why that one? Which bucket (owned/earned/paid) earned your trust — and what would the business need to own before you would recommend it spend on ads?
