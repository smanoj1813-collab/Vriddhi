# 1.4 Goals, KPIs and the marketing brief

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Write a SMART goal a client or manager can hold you to.
- Build a KPI tree that connects a business goal to channel-level numbers.
- Distinguish goals, KPIs and metrics — and explain why the difference decides budgets.
- Fill the one-page marketing brief that will front every project in this course.

## Opening scenario

A college startup weekend team pitched: "Our goal is to increase brand awareness for the campus store." The judge asked three questions. Increase by how much? Among whom? By when? The team had no answers — so nobody could ever tell whether the plan worked, and the ₹10,000 they asked for could not be defended.

Compare the brief the winning team submitted: "Get 300 of the college's 2,400 students to use the store's WhatsApp ordering before the November exams, at a cost per first order under ₹40." One sentence, and suddenly everything was decided — the channel (WhatsApp, where students already are), the metric (first orders), the deadline (exams), and the efficiency line (₹40). When they hit 340 orders at ₹31 each, the store owner did not need convincing to fund the next quarter.

The discipline that separates those two briefs takes one lesson to learn and a career to master. It is the difference between doing marketing and being able to *prove* it.

## Core ideas

### 1. SMART goals: the sentence that ends arguments

A usable goal is:

- **Specific** — names the change, the audience and the channel.
- **Measurable** — a number, and a source for the number.
- **Achievable** — grounded in a benchmark or baseline, not hope.
- **Relevant** — connected to money, mission or growth the client actually cares about.
- **Time-bound** — a date, because "someday" cannot fail and therefore cannot be managed.

"Awareness" fails because it is not measurable. "500 qualified enquiries from first-year students in October, tracked in the admission CRM" passes. Note the pattern: *number + audience + channel/source + deadline.*

### 2. The KPI tree: how a goal becomes daily numbers

A KPI tree links the business outcome at the top to the numbers some channel team can move at the bottom:

```
Business goal: 120 paid admissions this term
├─ Marketing-sourced enquiries: 800
│   ├─ Website enquiry form: 300   ← form conversion rate × traffic
│   ├─ WhatsApp clicks from ads: 320
│   └─ Walk-in/phone from offline: 180
├─ Enquiry → admission rate: 15%   ← counselling quality, follow-up speed
└─ Cost per admission: ≤ ₹4,000    ← budget guardrail
```

Each branch pairs a **KPI** (the number that matters for a decision) with **diagnostic metrics** (the numbers that explain it — opens, clicks, reach, scroll depth). Confusing the two is how dashboards fill with noise: page likes are diagnostics, not KPIs, because nobody makes a decision from them. In Module 6 you will build a dashboard around exactly this tree.

### 3. Goal → KPI → metric: the budget logic

Here is why the hierarchy decides money. If the client's goal is admissions, and enquiry-to-admission conversion is weak, the correct spend may be *follow-up staff and CRM discipline*, not more ads. A marketer who can only report top-of-funnel metrics (reach, impressions) will keep buying awareness; a marketer who can read the whole tree moves money to where the constraint actually is. That reading skill — not creativity alone — is what the three roles this course targets are hired for.

### 4. The one-page brief

Every project block in this course starts from this brief. Copy it, fill it, and do not start work until every line has an answer:

| Field | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Client / business** | Who this is for and what they sell |
| **Business goal** | The SMART sentence |
| **Audience** | The persona name + the one barrier that matters most |
| **Message / offer** | The single promise, in customer language |
| **Channels & roles** | Each channel and the journey stage it serves |
| **Numbers** | Baseline → target for 3–5 KPIs, and where each is measured |
| **Budget & time** | Money, dates, and who does what |
| **Out of scope** | What you are explicitly NOT doing (this line prevents scope creep) |
| **Risks & assumptions** | What could break this, and what you are assuming |

The **out of scope** line is the most underrated: it is how professionals say no without conflict, and how you keep a two-week project two weeks long.

## Try it now

Take the sentence "we want more followers" and rewrite it SMART for a bakery near your home. Then list two KPIs and two diagnostic metrics for it. Two minutes — but if it feels hard, that difficulty is exactly what the lab will fix.

## Hands-on lab: write the brief for your lab business (45 min)

Using your channel map (1.1) and research dossier (1.3), produce the one-page brief.

1. **Baseline hunt (10 min):** Find or estimate today's numbers — monthly footfall, enquiries, orders, followers. If the business tracks nothing, write your best estimate and mark it "assumed". Baselines can be rough; they cannot be missing.
2. **The goal sentence (10 min):** Write the SMART goal. Test it aloud: could a stranger tell, on the deadline date, whether you succeeded?
3. **The KPI tree (15 min):** Sketch the tree from goal down to at least two channel-level branches, each with baseline → target. Mark which KPIs you can measure today and which need setup (Module 6 covers the setup).
4. **The full brief (10 min):** Fill all nine fields, including out-of-scope and risks. Trade drafts with a classmate: their job is to find one vague line and ask "how would we know?".

> **Deliverable:** the completed one-page brief with a KPI tree. This document is the front page of Project Block 3 and your capstone — you will thank yourself for its quality later.

## Common mistakes

- **Vanity goals.** "Grow followers" without a business link invites wasted spend and fake followers.
- **No baseline.** Without today's number you can never prove you moved it.
- **Metric soup.** Reporting fifteen numbers so that no single one can be judged. Three to five KPIs, full stop.
- **The unbounded brief.** Every line you do not explicitly exclude will arrive at your door by week two.

## Key takeaways

- SMART goals (number + audience + source + deadline) make success checkable and failure fixable.
- A KPI tree turns one business goal into channel-level numbers that teams can actually move.
- KPIs drive decisions; diagnostics explain them — never let the diagnostics run the meeting.
- The one-page brief, especially its out-of-scope line, is the professional's first deliverable on any campaign.
