# 3.4 Repurposing and distribution

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Run the repurposing waterfall: one strong idea into many channel-native assets.
- Adapt one message across channels without copy-paste sameness.
- Execute a distribution checklist that treats publishing as the start, not the end.
- Decide when to stop promoting a piece and make the next one.

## Opening scenario

A Mangaluru CA coaching centre wrote one excellent guide: "CA Foundation results are out: your next 4 options, honestly explained." It went on the blog. A month later, a student on the marketing team turned the same guide into: an Instagram carousel ("Option 1 vs 2 vs 3 vs 4 — swipe"), two reels (one per option, 40 seconds each), a WhatsApp broadcast with the four options and the guide link, an email to the enquiry list, answers to five Quora and Google Questions, and a one-page PDF counsellors now send to confused students.

Total extra writing time: about four hours. The guide had cost a week. **The centre got seven assets from one week of work — and the carousel, not the blog, became their biggest lead source that quarter.** Every reader has a home channel; repurposing is how one idea visits them all.

## Core ideas

### 1. Create once, distribute everywhere — the waterfall

The unit of content is not the post; it is the **idea**. One strong, well-researched idea flows down a waterfall:

```
Pillar guide (the source of truth)
   ├─ Carousel: the guide's structure, one point per slide
   ├─ Short videos: one sub-idea each, 30–60 seconds
   ├─ Email section: the two most useful takeaways + link
   ├─ WhatsApp broadcast: 5 lines, plain language + link
   ├─ Q&A answers: genuinely answering related public questions
   ├─ Quote cards / stat cards: the single most striking line
   └─ Counsellor one-pager: the operational version (PDF)
```

Rules that keep the waterfall honest:

- **Every asset links back to the pillar** — the guide is the destination that converts and ranks.
- **Channel-native, not copy-paste.** A carousel is not a chopped blog; each asset uses its channel's grammar (Lesson 5.2 covers the formats).
- **Order of operations:** the pillar ships first; assets follow in the same week while the idea is fresh.

### 2. Distribution: publishing is the start

The most common failure in content marketing is the silent publish. A distribution checklist for every piece:

- **Own channels first (day 0):** site/blog, email list, WhatsApp community, story announcements.
- **Social posts (day 0–2):** channel-native versions, posted at that channel's good hours, each with a reason to click.
- **Communities (day 1–7):** relevant groups and forums where you are a genuine member — share where it answers a real question, never as a broadcast.
- **Questions and comments (ongoing):** answer related public questions by using the piece, with disclosure of affiliation where required.
- **Repurposed drip (week 2–4):** schedule the quote cards, second videos, and follow-up ("we wrote this last month; here is what changed").
- **Paid amplification (optional):** Module 4 — put a small budget behind the asset that earned the best organic response, not the one you liked most.

Ten minutes of distribution planning per piece typically multiplies its reach several-fold. In your calendar (Lesson 3.1), the distribution column is where this checklist lives.

### 3. Iteration beats volume: make more of what worked

After two weeks, read the evidence (Lesson 6.1 formalises this):

- Which asset earned the most engagement *relative to its channel's average*?
- Which drove the actual goal — enquiries, orders, sign-ups?

The professional move is asymmetric: **double down on the format/topic that worked; stop making what did not.** The coaching centre discovered carousels out-reached everything, so the next guide started life with its carousel planned before the blog outline was finished. Teams that cannot read their own evidence keep producing in the original proportions and wonder why nothing compounds.

### 4. The kill criteria

Deciding what NOT to make is half of distribution discipline. Before planning the next cycle:

- Pieces with no engagement and no search demand after a fair trial (two to three cycles) are retired, not repeated out of stubbornness.
- Channels where your audience demonstrably is not active get minimum effort, not presence for presence's sake.
- Every new idea must trace to research (keyword, persona, reviews) or to evidence (what already worked). "It felt right" is not a content strategy.

## Try it now

Find one strong blog post or guide from any brand you follow and list every other format you can find it in. Most brands publish once and stop. Count the misses — that is the opportunity you just learned to capture.

## Hands-on lab: waterfall one idea (45 min)

Take the pillar piece you outlined in Lesson 3.1's calendar.

1. **The pillar skeleton (10 min):** Extract the guide's structure — the 5–7 sub-ideas that could each stand alone.
2. **Build the waterfall (20 min):** Produce drafts for at least four assets: a carousel outline (one sub-idea per slide, hook slide, CTA slide), one 40-second video script (hook, one idea, CTA), the WhatsApp broadcast text (5 lines + link), and the email section (two takeaways + link).
3. **Distribution plan (10 min):** Write the checklist for this piece: which channels, which days, which communities — with the specific groups or forums by name.
4. **Metrics and kill criteria (5 min):** Name the two numbers you will read in two weeks and the threshold at which you would make more like this — or stop.

> **Deliverable:** pillar skeleton + four drafted assets + dated distribution plan + your metrics/kill-criteria lines. This completes the machinery for Project Block 2.

## Common mistakes

- **Publish-and-pray.** The piece goes live; nobody is told; nothing happens; the team concludes "content doesn't work".
- **Copy-paste across channels.** Same caption everywhere trains every audience to ignore it.
- **Equal effort forever.** Spreading effort evenly across topics and channels regardless of evidence guarantees mediocrity everywhere.
- **No memory.** Not recording what worked, so every month starts from zero.

## Key takeaways

- The unit of content is the idea; one pillar flows into many channel-native assets that all link back.
- Publishing is the start: run the distribution checklist for at least two weeks per piece.
- Read the evidence, double down on winners, and retire what the audience already voted against.
- Every new piece must trace to research or evidence — "it felt right" is not a strategy.
