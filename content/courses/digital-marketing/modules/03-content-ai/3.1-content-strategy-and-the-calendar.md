# 3.1 Content strategy and the calendar

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Explain what a content strategy is: topics chosen from research, formats chosen from the funnel, published on a rhythm the team can sustain.
- Design a pillar-and-cluster topic structure that supports SEO and demonstrates expertise.
- Choose formats by funnel stage and effort, not by trend.
- Build a 30-day content calendar that survives contact with a real week.

## Opening scenario

A Bengaluru study-abroad consultancy posted daily for three months — random reels, festival greetings, motivational quotes, the occasional course listing. The founder was exhausted; enquiries had not moved. A student intern asked to see the keyword map from Lesson 2.1 and the persona from 1.3, then deleted 80% of the plan. What remained: four pillar guides ("IELTS vs PTE for Karnataka students", "Education loan documents: the actual list", "Visa interview: 20 real questions", "Country comparison for B.Com graduates"), each supported by short social posts answering one sub-question each. Posting dropped to four times a week. Enquiries from content tripled in two months.

More content was never the strategy. **The right content, in a structure, on a rhythm** — that is the strategy.

## Core ideas

### 1. Content strategy = topics × formats × cadence

Three decisions, in this order:

1. **Topics** come from research, not inspiration: the keyword map (what people search), the persona's questions and barriers (1.3), the reviews of competitors (gaps to own), and the business goal (1.4). Every topic should trace to at least one of these.
2. **Formats** serve the funnel stage: a guide builds awareness and SEO authority; a comparison page serves consideration; a case study or offer page converts; an email newsletter retains. Short-form video is discovery; long-form text is depth.
3. **Cadence** is what you can sustain for six months, not what excites you in week one. Two good pieces a week for a quarter beats daily posting that dies in three weeks — both audiences and algorithms reward consistency.

### 2. Pillars and clusters: the structure of authority

Choose three to five **pillar topics** — the broad themes where you want to be known (for the consultancy: test preparation, financing, visas, country choice). Each pillar gets one comprehensive **pillar page** (the guide that could rank for the head term), surrounded by a **cluster** of narrower pieces that each answer one specific question and link back to the pillar:

```
PILLAR: Education loans for studying abroad
   ├─ "Documents banks actually ask for"        (cluster)
   ├─ "Collateral vs no-collateral: the real difference" (cluster)
   ├─ "How much can you borrow on a ₹6 lakh salary?"     (cluster)
   └─ "Education loan vs scholarship: the honest maths"  (cluster)
```

This structure works three ways at once: search engines see an organised authority on the topic; readers get a path from their exact question to the full guide; and your calendar fills itself — every cluster piece is a scheduled post with a clear job.

### 3. Choosing formats honestly

| Format | Funnel job | Effort | Reuse potential |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Long-form guide | Awareness + SEO | High | Splits into many social posts |
| Short video / reel | Awareness + discovery | Medium | High — one idea per reel |
| Carousel / thread | Consideration | Medium | Becomes email + blog |
| Case study | Conversion | Medium | Proof for every channel |
| Newsletter | Retention | Low–medium | Harvests everything above |
| Live Q&A / webinar | Consideration + community | Medium | Recording becomes 5+ clips |

The mistake is choosing format first ("we need reels!") and topic second. Strategy chooses the topic, the funnel stage chooses the format.

### 4. The 30-day calendar that survives

A calendar is a promise to your future self. Columns: **date | pillar | topic | format | funnel stage | owner | status | distribution**. Two rules make it real:

- **The 50% rule:** plan at most 50% of your capacity. Weeks contain exams, festivals, sick days and client emergencies. A calendar at 100% capacity is a calendar that fails by day ten.
- **Distribution is part of the content:** each row says where the piece goes *after* publishing — which social channels, which newsletter, which WhatsApp group. Publishing is the start of the work, not the end (Lesson 3.4 makes this a system).

## Try it now

Open any brand's social page you admire. Classify its last ten posts by funnel stage. Most brands discover they post 90% awareness and nothing for consideration or retention. That gap is your opportunity when you plan your own.

## Hands-on lab: the 30-day calendar (45 min)

Using your keyword map (2.1) and persona (1.3) for your lab business:

1. **Pillars (10 min):** Write three pillar topics. Under each, list four cluster questions from your keyword map and People Also Ask harvest.
2. **Format mapping (10 min):** Assign a format to each cluster piece using the format table. Check the mix: roughly 60% awareness, 30% consideration, 10% conversion/retention.
3. **The calendar (15 min):** Lay the pieces on a 30-day grid at 50% capacity (e.g., 3 posts a week = 12–13 pieces). Fill every column including owner and distribution.
4. **Stress test (5 min):** Circle the two pieces you would cut first in a bad week — knowing your cut order in advance is what keeps a calendar alive.
5. **One-line strategy (5 min):** "We publish [cadence] about [pillars] for [persona], measured by [KPI from 1.4]." If you cannot say it in one line, the strategy is not finished.

> **Deliverable:** pillar/cluster map + the 30-day calendar with format mix and cut order. This becomes the front half of Project Block 2.

## Common mistakes

- **Posting to be active.** Random content trains the audience to ignore you.
- **Trend-chasing formats.** Reels because everyone makes reels, on topics nobody searched for.
- **100% capacity planning.** The calendar that cannot absorb one bad week dies in week two.
- **Publish-and-pray.** No distribution plan means your best piece reaches your followers once and nobody else ever.

## Key takeaways

- Strategy is topics (from research) × formats (from funnel stage) × cadence (from honest capacity).
- Pillar-and-cluster structures demonstrate authority to search engines and give readers a path.
- Every format has a job and an effort level; strategy picks topics before formats.
- Plan at 50% capacity and decide your cut order in advance; distribution is part of content.

## Reflection journal

Write 150–200 words: audit your own social feed or a club/college page you know. What share of the last twenty posts served awareness, consideration, retention? What one pillar — if it existed — would have changed results? What would you cut?

## Going further (optional)

- Study one brand's blog in a category you like and draw its pillar/cluster structure on paper. Most brands have no structure — seeing it is learning it.
- Read any "content strategy" guide from a major marketing site and notice how much of it reduces to these three decisions.
