# 5.4 Creators, UGC and influencer marketing

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Choose between creator tiers (nano, micro, macro) with realistic expectations and budgets.
- Evaluate a creator with honest metrics — engagement, audience match, brand safety — not follower counts.
- Write a creator brief that protects both sides: deliverables, usage rights, disclosure.
- Design a UGC engine that turns customers into your content team.

## Opening scenario

A Belagavi salon spent its entire ₹25,000 collaboration budget on one reel from a 500k-follower lifestyle influencer. The reel was beautiful. The bookings were zero — her audience was 70% outside Karnataka and mostly students, while the salon's customers were local professional women 28–45.

The next ₹25,000 went differently: five nano-creators (2k–15k followers each) — all local teachers, homemakers and office workers with genuinely engaged local followers — each paid ₹3,000–5,000 for one honest "my appointment" reel, plus ₹5,000 boosting the best performer. Result: forty-two bookings in three weeks, most citing "saw a reel". Same money, ten times the outcome.

The lesson is not that big creators are bad — it is that **influence is audience match times trust, not follower count**. Buying followers is renting a stadium in the wrong city.

## Core ideas

### 1. The tiers and what each is actually for

| Tier | Followers | What you buy | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Nano** | 1k–10k | Trust of a real community; authenticity | Local businesses; cost-per-result champions |
| **Micro** | 10k–100k | Niche authority + reach | Category brands; sustained campaigns |
| **Macro** | 100k+ | Reach and aspiration | Launches, awareness bursts |
| **Celebrity** | 1M+ | Attention, press, prestige | Brand campaigns with real budgets |

For a local business, nano and micro creators dominate because their audiences are dense in exactly one place: the creator's own city and niche. Their engagement rates are typically the highest of any tier — their followers actually know them.

### 2. Evaluating a creator honestly

Before any money moves, check:

1. **Engagement rate** over follower count: (likes + comments) ÷ followers, on recent posts. Under ~1% on a mid-size account signals ghost followers; look for comment *substance*, not emoji walls.
2. **Audience match:** ask for (or screenshot) their audience insights — city, age, gender split. A Belagavi salon needs Belagavi-heavy geographies; follower totals cannot tell you this.
3. **Content quality and consistency:** their last nine posts — do they make content your product would fit naturally into?
4. **Brand safety:** scan for controversies, fake-engagement patterns (sudden spikes), past undisclosed ads.
5. **The test comment:** message them before the deal. Response speed and tone during negotiation predicts the collaboration experience.

### 3. The brief: protect both sides

A professional creator collaboration has a written brief covering:

- **Deliverables:** number of posts/reels/stories, formats, dates, drafts-for-review policy (usually one revision round — respect their craft).
- **Message anchor:** the one thing the content must convey (the offer, the differentiator) — without scripting their voice.
- **Usage rights:** can you reuse the content in ads, on your page, for how long? (Paid ad usage usually costs extra — negotiate it upfront; Lesson 4.3's boost strategy depends on it.)
- **Disclosure:** paid partnerships must be labelled — platforms' branded-content tools and, in India, ASCI's influencer-disclosure guidelines require clear labelling ("#ad", "paid partnership"). Undisclosed paid promotion is both illegal risk and trust suicide.
- **Payment terms:** half upfront or on delivery, within X days — agreed in writing, however friendly the chat.
- **Exclusivity (if any):** not promoting a direct competitor for N weeks.

The creator keeps their voice; you keep the rights you paid for; the audience keeps the honesty. All three survive on a good brief.

### 4. UGC: the engine that compounds

User-generated content — customer photos, reviews, unboxings, before/afters — is creator marketing at zero cost, with built-in trust. The engine:

1. **Ask at the moment of delight:** the delivery message, the table thank-you, the "tag us in your [product] photo and we'll feature the best" invitation.
2. **Make it worth their while:** features, small vouchers, monthly spotlights — recognition beats cash here.
3. **Collect with permission:** a saved folder plus a one-line usage permission ("Can we share this on our page? 💚"). Rights matter even for friendly reposts.
4. **Deploy everywhere:** UGC is your best ad creative (Lesson 4.3: native beats studio), your best landing-page proof (4.4), and your review engine — with consent, a happy public post becomes a review request.

Five pieces of real UGC routinely outperform one professional shoot, because audiences have learned to discount polish and price authenticity.

## Try it now

Open any influencer's profile and compute the engagement rate on their last three posts. Then read ten comments: real sentences or emoji walls? You now know more about evaluating influence than most brands' first campaigns ever did.

## Hands-on lab: the creator plan (45 min)

1. **Tier strategy (10 min):** With a ₹25,000 budget for your lab business, decide the tier mix and justify it in three lines (audience density, trust, cost per expected result).
2. **Shortlist (15 min):** Find three real candidate creators (any tier) in your locality/niche. For each: followers, engagement rate (compute it), audience-match hypothesis, one brand-safety note.
3. **The brief (10 min):** Write the full brief for your top candidate: deliverables, message anchor, usage rights (including paid usage), disclosure line, payment terms, revision policy.
4. **UGC engine (10 min):** Design yours: the ask (where, exact words), the incentive, the permission line, the collection habit, and the first place each piece of UGC will be deployed (ad / landing page / feature slot).

> **Deliverable:** tier strategy + three-candidate scorecard + one complete brief + UGC engine design. Completes the creator section of Project Block 3.

## Common mistakes

- **Follower-count purchasing.** A stadium in the wrong city, rented per post.
- **Scripting the creator.** You bought their voice; writing their script converts trust into an ad everyone skips.
- **Skipping usage rights.** Discovering you cannot run the best-performing reel as an ad — after it worked — is an avoidable heartbreak.
- **Undisclosed ads.** ASCI guidelines and platform rules both require labelling; the audience forgives sponsorship and punishes deception.

## Key takeaways

- Influence = audience match × trust; nano/micro creators dominate local and niche campaigns on cost per result.
- Evaluate creators on engagement rate, audience geography, consistency, brand safety and negotiation behaviour.
- A written brief protects deliverables, usage rights, disclosure and payment — both sides.
- UGC is the compounding engine: ask at delight, reward with recognition, collect with permission, deploy as your best creative.
