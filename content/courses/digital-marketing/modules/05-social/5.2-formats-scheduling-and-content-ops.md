# 5.2 Formats, scheduling and content ops

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Choose the right format (reel, carousel, story, static) for each job in the funnel.
- Build a weekly batching workflow that produces a month of content in a day.
- Use a simple brand kit so every post is recognisably yours without a designer.
- Schedule and manage a free-tool stack with a posting rhythm you can keep.

## Opening scenario

A student running a Kochi café's Instagram posted live: every day at 6 p.m. she would decide what to post, shoot it badly in bad light, write the caption twice, and publish whenever it was ready. Some weeks she managed daily posts; exam weeks, nothing for nine days.

The fix was not more discipline — it was a different system. One Sunday a month: she cooked and shot eight short videos in two hours in the good morning light; shot the carousel photos in the same session; wrote all captions from a template; and scheduled everything with a free tool. Weekdays became 20 minutes a day: reply to comments and DMs, post one story live from the café. The grid stopped looking abandoned; the account grew faster on less effort; and exam weeks no longer showed on the feed at all.

**Content ops** — the boring machinery of batches, templates and schedules — is what separates accounts that look professional from accounts that burn out.

## Core ideas

### 1. Formats and their funnel jobs

| Format | Best at | Craft notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reel / short video** | Discovery — reaching non-followers | Hook in 1–2 s, on-screen text, one idea, 15–40 s; native trends remade for your niche |
| **Carousel** | Consideration — teaching, comparing, proving | Hook slide → one point per slide → CTA slide; saves are the metric |
| **Story** | Relationship — daily presence, polls, behind-the-scenes | Informal, 24-hour life; polls and countdowns invite interaction |
| **Static post** | Announcements, offers, quotes | Cheapest to make; weakest reach; use sparingly |
| **Live** | Community, Q&A, launches | Announce in advance; the recording becomes clips |

The ratio follows the strategy: for discovery-led accounts (the café), roughly 60% reels / 25% carousels / 15% stories-extra. The proportions are a starting hypothesis, not a law — your analytics (Lesson 6) adjust them.

### 2. Batching: the monthly engine

The professional rhythm is **make in batches, publish on schedule, engage in person**:

1. **Monthly batch day (2–4 h):** shoot and draft a month of pillar content — all videos in one session (same light, same setup, costume changes as needed), all carousel outlines, all captions from templates.
2. **Weekly touch (30 min):** review what performed, adjust the coming week's schedule, note ideas that arrived from comments.
3. **Daily presence (15–20 min):** reply to every comment and DM (community, Lesson 5.3), one live story, watch the feed for trends worth adapting.
4. **Capture always:** a running idea list on your phone — screenshots of questions asked, trends noticed, customer moments.

The rules of thumb that make batching work: never make one piece when you can make six; templates for captions and covers; a fixed shot list; and the 3.4 waterfall doing its job (one pillar idea → carousel + reel + story).

### 3. The brand kit: recognisable without a designer

A one-page kit makes everything recognisably yours and cuts production time in half:

- **Palette:** two main colours + one accent (grab them from your logo; use them in covers, text, charts).
- **Fonts:** one heading font, one body font, in any free tool (Canva).
- **Cover template:** reels' cover frames and carousels' first slides share one layout, so the profile grid looks curated.
- **Voice:** the Lesson 3.2 voice sheet — same adjectives, same banned words.
- **Photo rules:** natural light, real products and people, minimal filters; one signature angle if you can.

Consistency compounds: an account that looks coherent at a glance earns the follow; a random grid makes even good content look amateur.

### 4. Scheduling and the free stack

- **Scheduling:** Meta Business Suite (free, native, handles Instagram + Facebook), Buffer/Later free tiers for cross-platform. Schedule at your audience's active hours — insights show them; before you have data, 12–2 p.m. and 7–9 p.m. are Indian defaults.
- **Creation:** your phone's camera, a ₹300 tripod, a window, and Canva. Nothing else is needed.
- **Linking:** a single link-in-bio page (free tiers exist) routing to the two or three destinations that matter.
- **Calendar:** the 3.1 content calendar IS the social calendar — one source of truth, distribution column included.

**Do not automate the human part.** Scheduled posts + abandoned comments = a shop with a shutter that never opens for conversation.

## Try it now

Open the profile grid (not the feed) of any account you admire. Look at the covers of their last nine reels/carousels: what makes them recognisable in two seconds — colour? layout? typeface? You have just seen a brand kit at work.

## Hands-on lab: build the content machine (45 min)

1. **Format plan (10 min):** From your 5.1 strategy one-liner, set your format ratios (e.g., 4 reels, 2 carousels, daily stories per week). Write which pillar each format serves.
2. **The batch run (20 min):** Produce, in this lab, the raw material for one week: two reel scripts (hook line, shot list of 4–6 shots, on-screen text), one carousel outline (hook slide, 5 point slides, CTA slide), and three story ideas (one poll, one behind-the-scenes, one countdown).
3. **Brand kit (10 min):** Write your palette (2 colours + accent, hex codes), fonts, cover layout sketch, and three voice-sheet lines. Make the carousel's first slide in Canva (or sketch it).
4. **The schedule (5 min):** Put the week into a calendar with dates, times and the tool you will use. Mark the daily 20-minute engagement slot as an unmissable meeting.

> **Deliverable:** format ratios + a full week's raw content + brand kit + dated schedule. This is the operating system your Project Block 3 campaign runs on.

## Common mistakes

- **Live posting every day.** Decision fatigue kills consistency; batch, schedule, engage.
- **Trend-chasing without niche fit.** A trend remade for your audience works; a trend copied naked makes you noise.
- **No cover discipline.** Random covers make the grid look abandoned even when it is active.
- **Automating replies.** Scheduled content is efficient; scheduled *humanity* is visible and costs trust.

## Key takeaways

- Reels discover, carousels teach and prove, stories build relationship, statics announce — ratios follow the strategy.
- Batch monthly, adjust weekly, engage daily; never make one piece when you can make six.
- A one-page brand kit (palette, fonts, cover template, voice) buys instant professionalism.
- Schedule with free tools at your audience's hours — but keep the conversation human.
