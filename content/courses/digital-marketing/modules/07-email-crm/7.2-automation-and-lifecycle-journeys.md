# 7.2 Automation and lifecycle journeys

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Map a customer lifecycle and locate the moments where email earns its keep.
- Design three core automated journeys: welcome, nurture, and win-back.
- Configure a journey in a real tool: triggers, delays, branches, exits.
- Use behavioural triggers (opens, clicks, purchases) to make email feel personal at scale.

## Opening scenario

The Dharwad physiotherapy clinic had a problem no amount of new patients could fix: **missed second visits.** Patients came once, got better or forgot, and never returned — the doctor's careful follow-up advice evaporated. A simple automated journey changed the economics: every patient's email entered a sequence — day 1, exercise guide; day 3, "how the recovery usually feels"; day 7, a booking link; day 21, a check-in. No staff time, no memory required. Second-visit rates rose by a third.

That is automation's promise: **the right email arrives because of what the customer did, not because someone remembered to send it.** This lesson builds the journeys that do it.

## Core ideas

### 1. The lifecycle map

Every business has a lifecycle; email's job lives at its joints:

| Stage | Who | Email's job |
|---|---|---|
| Subscriber | Just joined | Welcome: deliver promise, set cadence, first value |
| Lead / Enquiry | Raised a hand | Nurture: prove competence, answer objections, invite action |
| Customer (first) | Just bought | Onboard: get success fast, prevent regret |
| Customer (active) | Repeat behaviour | Deepen: relevant next offers, loyalty |
| At risk / dormant | No activity 60–180 days | Win-back: re-earn attention, then let go |
| Churned | Unsubscribed / unresponsive | Exit gracefully; suppress, don't spam |

Map your own business's stages before building anything: the map IS the automation plan.

### 2. Journey anatomy: trigger → steps → exit

A journey (the tools call it automation, workflow, or campaign flow) has fixed parts:

- **Trigger:** the event that starts it — "joins list", "form submitted", "made a purchase", "clicked X but didn't buy", "no activity for 90 days".
- **Steps:** a sequence of emails with **delays** ("wait 2 days") and **branches** ("if clicked → path A; if not → path B").
- **Exit conditions:** goal achieved (they booked → stop selling), journey end, or manual removal.
- **Suppression:** the rules preventing overlap — a customer mid-purchase journey should not also receive the generic newsletter offer for the same thing.

The discipline: **draw it on paper first.** Boxes and arrows, every branch labelled, before touching the tool. Complex journeys fail because nobody drew them.

### 3. The three core journeys (build these first)

**1. The welcome series (3–4 emails, days 0–10).** The highest-attention window a subscriber ever gives you. Email 1 (day 0): deliver the promise + one standout resource. Email 2 (day 2): the origin story or the "how we're different" — relationship, not pitch. Email 3 (day 5): social proof — testimonials, results, the UGC from Lesson 5.4. Email 4 (day 10): the soft first offer with a clear CTA. Branch: if they click the offer early, skip ahead; if they never open, slow the cadence.

**2. The lead-nurture series (4–6 emails over 2–3 weeks).** For enquiries who aren't ready (the education-admissions world lives here). Each email answers ONE objection: "Is it worth the fee?" (proof + outcomes), "Will it fit my schedule?" (format explanation), "What if it doesn't work?" (guarantee/demo), "Why now?" (deadline, honestly framed). Behavioural branch: clicked the pricing page twice → route to a personal follow-up (that is CRM territory, Lesson 7.3).

**3. The win-back series (2–3 emails, then stop).** Email 1: genuine "we noticed, here's something useful" — no ask. Email 2: the honest offer or the new-thing announcement. Email 3: "Should we part ways?" — the goodbye email (famously high open rates; it either revives or cleans your list). Then exit and suppress. Unending win-backs train people to ignore you.

### 4. Behavioural triggers: personal without being creepy

The spectrum from broadcast to behaviour:

- **Broadcast:** same email to everyone (newsletter).
- **Segmented:** different email by cohort (Lesson 6's segments: new vs repeat, Kochi vs Bengaluru).
- **Behavioural:** the email exists BECAUSE of an action — abandoned cart, pricing-page visit, missed class, lapsed 30 days.
- **Predictive:** the tool scores likelihood (enterprise territory; know it exists).

Behavioural beats broadcast on every metric, but the creep line is real: "We saw you staring at the ₹499 package for six minutes" is surveillance, not service. The professional rule: **trigger on what they did, speak to what they want, never narrate the watching.** "Still thinking it over? Here's the demo video again" — fine. "You added yoga pants to your cart 14 times" — no.

### 5. Measure journeys, not emails

A journey's health metrics: **completion rate** (what % reach the goal step), **drop-off step** (which email loses them — usually email 2), and **goal conversions per entrant** (bookings per 100 leads entering nurture). Journey-level thinking is the module's bridge to CRM: the journey is the machinery, the CRM is the memory.

## Try it now

Find an automated journey you are already inside (any brand's welcome flow). Map it on paper: trigger, emails, delays, branches — and one thing you would fix.

## Hands-on lab: build the welcome journey (45 min)

1. **Map (10 min):** For your PB3 business, draw the full lifecycle map (stages + emails at each joint), then the welcome journey in boxes-and-arrows: 4 emails, delays, one branch (clicked offer / no open), exit condition.
2. **Write (15 min):** Full copy for emails 1 and 3 of the welcome series — subject, preview, body ≤120 words, CTA. Email 1 must deliver the signup promise; email 3 must use social proof.
3. **Configure (15 min):** In your tool from Lesson 7.1, build the welcome automation: trigger = signup form join, delays as designed, the branch (or two parallel paths if branching is a paid feature — note it). Send yourself the test of every email.
4. **Plan measurement (5 min):** Define the journey's goal step, its completion target, and the one drop-off you will watch first. Note the suppression rule versus the newsletter.

> **Deliverable:** Lifecycle map + drawn welcome journey + copy for emails 1 & 3 + tool screenshot/note + measurement plan. This journey goes into the capstone campaign.

## Common mistakes

- **Building before drawing.** Undrawn journeys become unfixable hairballs.
- **The endless win-back.** Three emails, then suppress — dignity is a retention strategy.
- **Creepy triggers.** Narrate the benefit, never the surveillance.
- **Judging emails, not journeys.** A great email 2 with 70% drop-off at email 1 is a bad journey.

## Key takeaways

- Lifecycle stages locate the emails: welcome, nurture, onboard, deepen, win-back, exit.
- A journey is trigger → delayed steps → branches → exit, with suppression rules; draw before you build.
- Build the three core journeys first: welcome (0–10 days), nurture (objection per email), win-back (3 then stop).
- Behavioural triggers beat broadcasts; speak to the want, never narrate the watching; measure completion and drop-off.
