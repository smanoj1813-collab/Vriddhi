# 7.1 Email marketing essentials

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Explain why owned email outperforms rented social reach for repeat business.
- Set up a compliant email programme: tool, sender identity, and list hygiene.
- Write the five emails every business needs, using a repeatable structure.
- Read the four metrics that judge an email, with realistic benchmarks.

## Opening scenario

A Mysuru wedding photographer had 18,000 Instagram followers and a booking crisis. An algorithm change cut her reach by 70% overnight; the enquiry tap simply closed. Meanwhile a competitor with just 2,400 email contacts kept booking — every month she emailed her list, and 4% of them enquired. One number to understand: **18,000 × 7% reach = 1,260 people saw the post. 2,400 × 95% delivered = 2,280 people received the email.** The smaller list reached more humans.

Social platforms are **rented land** — the landlord changes the rules and your reach disappears. Email is **owned land**: the list is yours, the delivery rules are stable, and it consistently produces the highest return of any channel for small businesses.

## Core ideas

### 1. The strategic role: retention and repeat

Email is rarely your discovery channel. Its job is the second half of the funnel: turning one-time buyers into repeat customers, enquiries into bookings, dormant customers into returning ones. For a tuition centre, the email list of parents is the admission-season goldmine. For the café, the weekend-special list IS the weekend crowd. Acquisition happens elsewhere (Modules 2, 4, 5); email multiplies what acquisition brings.

### 2. The mechanics: tool, sender, list

A working programme needs four pieces:

- **A tool.** Mailchimp, Brevo, Zoho Campaigns, Google's bulk-sending via GMass — pick one; the mechanics transfer.
- **A sender identity.** Send from a real domain (hello@brandname.in), not gmail. Set up **SPF/DKIM/DMARC** authentication — the tool's setup guide walks you through it; unauthenticated mail lands in spam. Under Google/Yahoo bulk-sender rules (since 2024), authentication and one-click unsubscribe are mandatory for bulk senders.
- **A list, built with permission.** Every address comes from a signup form, a lead magnet, or a customer who ticked a box. **Never buy a list** — it is legally toxic (Lesson 7.4) and deliverability suicide.
- **Hygiene.** Remove hard bounces immediately; prune addresses with zero opens for 6–12 months. A smaller clean list outperforms a big dead one.

### 3. The five emails every business needs

Before automation (Lesson 7.2), master the fundamentals:

1. **The welcome email** — sent immediately on signup. The most-opened email you will ever send (often 50%+). Deliver the promised resource, set expectations ("weekly tips, one offer a month"), introduce the brand voice.
2. **The newsletter** — a rhythm (weekly/biweekly/monthly) with genuine value: one tip, one story, one offer. Consistency beats brilliance.
3. **The offer** — a clear, honest promotion. One CTA, one deadline, no fake urgency.
4. **The win-back** — to dormant subscribers: "We miss you" + a reason to return (not just a discount).
5. **The thank-you/receipt-plus** — post-purchase: confirm, then help ("How to get the best out of your first class") — the seed of lifecycle email.

### 4. The repeatable structure

Every email follows the same anatomy:

- **Subject line (≤45 characters):** the promise, not the poem. Curiosity or benefit, honestly delivered.
- **Preview text:** completes the subject — they are read together.
- **One idea per email:** one topic, one CTA button repeated at most twice. Two CTAs halve clicks.
- **Scannable body:** short paragraphs, bold key phrases, a button, not a wall.
- **Plain-text feel:** emails that feel like a note from a person outperform emails that look like a brochure.

Subject-line craft: specificity beats cleverness — "Your weekend plan: 2 new pastries" outperforms "Exciting news!". Test by writing five, choosing the one YOU would open (real A/B testing arrives with Lesson 7.2's tools and Module 6's method).

### 5. The four metrics and honest benchmarks

- **Delivery rate** — landed in the inbox (not spam/bounced). Target 98%+. Below 95%: fix authentication and hygiene first.
- **Open rate** — opened / delivered. Healthy: 20–40% for small engaged lists. Falling? Your subject lines, or a decaying list.
- **Click rate** — clicked / delivered. Healthy: 2–5%. This is the number that pays.
- **Unsubscribe + spam rate** — under 0.3% unsubscribes, under 0.1% spam complaints, per email. Above that, your content is failing its audience.

One reframe from Module 6: opens are near-diagnostic (Apple Mail auto-opens distort them), clicks are the KPI. And segment-level truth beats list-level averages — the "average open rate" hides a dead half-list.

## Hands-on lab: the five-email starter kit (45 min)

1. **Tool and identity (10 min):** Create a free account on any email tool (Brevo/Mailchimp/Zoho). Walk the sender setup; note the SPF/DKIM steps the tool shows. If you have no domain, note what you would do for the café.
2. **The signup form (5 min):** Build a simple signup form or landing page for your PB3 business with a one-line promise ("Weekly Bengaluru food finds — one email, every Friday").
3. **The welcome email (10 min):** Write it fully: subject, preview, body (≤120 words), CTA. Set it as an automated welcome in the tool if possible.
4. **The newsletter (10 min):** Draft one full edition using the one-tip-one-story-one-offer structure. Write FIVE subject-line candidates and mark your pick with a reason.
5. **The metrics plan (10 min):** Set up (or sketch) the tool's report view: delivery, open, click, unsubscribe targets for the first three sends. Note the two decisions each metric would trigger.

> **Deliverable:** Sender-setup notes + signup promise + welcome email + newsletter draft with 5 subject lines + metrics plan. Lesson 7.2 wires these into journeys.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying a list.** Instant deliverability damage plus legal exposure — the fastest way to burn the channel.
- **The monthly brochure.** All design, no idea, no CTA — deleted on arrival.
- **Two ideas, two CTAs.** Each additional ask halves the response.
- **Quoting opens as success.** Auto-open distortion makes clicks the honest KPI.

## Key takeaways

- Email is owned land: smaller lists can out-reach big follower counts; its job is retention and repeat.
- Tool + authenticated domain + permission-built, hygienic list = the working foundation.
- Master the five fundamentals — welcome, newsletter, offer, win-back, thank-you — before automation.
- One idea, one CTA, honest subject; judge by clicks (2–5%), not opens.
