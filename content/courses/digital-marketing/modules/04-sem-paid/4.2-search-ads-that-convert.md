# 4.2 Search ads that convert

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Structure a search campaign correctly: campaign → ad groups → tightly themed keywords.
- Choose keyword match types deliberately and defend the choice with negatives.
- Write search ad copy that earns the click by answering the query.
- Read the core metrics of a live search campaign and know what to do next.

## Opening scenario

A Pune gym-equipment seller ran one ad group containing 300 keywords — "dumbbells", "gym setup", "treadmill price", "fitness tips", "protein" — with a single ad: "Best Gym Equipment — Buy Now!" In ten days: ₹9,000 spent, 41 clicks, one sale of a ₹900 skipping rope.

A restructure took one evening: five ad groups by product line (dumbbells, plates, benches, treadmill, full home-gym setup); in each, exact-to-phrase keywords with real prices in the ads ("Hex dumbbells 5–50 kg — ₹68/kg, Pune delivery in 2 days") and negative keywords blocking "free", "tips", "how to" and "used". Same budget the next month: 220 clicks, 18 sales, CPA down 80%.

Same platform, same products, same money. **Structure is the strategy** in search ads — this lesson is that structure.

## Core ideas

### 1. Campaign architecture: theme everything

Google's hierarchy is Campaign → Ad Group → Keywords → Ads. The professional pattern:

- **One campaign per goal/budget/geography** (e.g., "Pune — home equipment — search").
- **One ad group per tight theme** — the products or intents inside that campaign (dumbbells, benches…). Tight themes let you write ads that match every keyword in the group, which raises relevance, which lowers cost (Lesson 4.1's auction).
- **Ads that mirror the theme's keywords** in headlines, and land on the theme's page — never the home page.

The test of a good ad group: *every keyword in it could share one ad and one landing page.* If not, split it.

### 2. Match types and the negative shield

Match types control which searches can trigger your ad:

| Match type | Symbol | Triggers on | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exact | [keyword] | The phrase (or very close) | Lowest volume, highest control |
| Phrase | "keyword" | Searches containing the phrase | Balanced default |
| Broad | keyword | Searches *related* to the phrase (synonyms, variants) | Volume and waste without a negative list |

The discipline that makes broad match survivable — and the beginner's most commonly skipped step — is **negative keywords**: searches for which your ads must *never* show. Add "free", "used", "second hand", "diy", "how to", "salary", "jobs" (whatever plausibly wastes your budget) at campaign level, and review the actual search-terms report weekly to keep pruning. Negative keywords are where wasted budgets are reclaimed; one session of reading what you actually paid for is the fastest marketing education available.

### 3. Writing the ad: answer the query

A search ad is small: a few headlines (~30 characters each) and descriptions (~90). The craft:

- **Headline 1 mirrors the query** ("Hex Dumbbells 5–50 kg"); Headline 2 adds the differentiator (price, speed, locality); Headline 3 can carry the proof or CTA ("Pune Delivery in 2 Days").
- **Descriptions answer the searcher's next question:** what does it cost, how fast, what is included, why trust you. Specifics ("₹68/kg") beat adjectives ("best quality").
- **Extensions/sitelinks** are free real estate — link to price lists, reviews, "order on WhatsApp". Use every one available.
- **The promise must be kept on the landing page** — message match (Lesson 4.4) is half of Quality Score's landing-page component.

The professional habit: before writing, search the phrase yourself and read the *whole results page* — the ads and the organic results tell you exactly what Google believes this searcher wants.

### 4. Reading a live campaign

The five numbers to check, in order:

1. **Search terms (lost IS – budget / rank):** are you missing impression share to budget or to rank? Fix with budget or quality respectively.
2. **CTR (click-through rate):** the market's verdict on your ad's relevance. Low CTR → the ad does not match the query.
3. **CPC:** your cost of attention; falling CTR raises it.
4. **Conversion rate:** the landing page's verdict. Good CTR + bad conversion = the page, not the ad.
5. **CPA against target:** the only number the client ultimately reads (Lesson 1.4's KPI tree).

Weekly ritual: read the search-terms report, add negatives, pause the worst ad group or keyword, push budget toward the best. Twenty minutes, every week, forever.

Search "course fees [something you'd study]" and score the top three ads against the copy rules: does Headline 1 mirror the query? Any specific number? Where would each land? Predict the CTR ranking before you look at anything else — you now can.

## Hands-on lab: build the campaign skeleton (45 min)

Using your pre-flight sheet (4.1) and keyword map (2.1):

1. **Architecture (10 min):** Write the campaign name and 3–5 ad groups, each with a tight theme and its intended landing page (from your site or a planned page).
2. **Keywords + negatives (15 min):** Per ad group, list 5–10 keywords with chosen match types. Then write the campaign-level negative list (at least 15 words/phrases) — hunt for them by brainstorming "what would waste this budget?"
3. **Ads (15 min):** For the two most important ad groups, write 3 headlines + 2 descriptions each, with the mirror-the-query rule, one specific number, and one differentiator. Mark the CTA and sitelink ideas.
4. **The metrics plan (5 min):** Write your target numbers: CTR, CPC, conversion rate, CPA. These become the campaign's "healthy/vampire" thresholds for the weekly ritual.

> **Deliverable:** campaign architecture sheet with ad groups, keywords + match types, negatives, two full ad drafts and target metrics. Core of Project Block 3.

## Common mistakes

- **The 300-keyword ad group.** One ad cannot match 300 intents; structure is the strategy.
- **Broad match with no negatives.** A budget vacuum disguised as reach.
- **Clever headlines.** Search ads reward mirror-the-query clarity, not wordplay.
- **Optimising the ad when the page leaks.** CTR tells you about the ad; conversion rate tells you about the page. Diagnose before you adjust.

## Key takeaways

- Campaign → ad group → keywords → ads: tight themes let ads match queries, raising quality and cutting cost.
- Match types are dials of control; negative keywords are the shield that makes any of them safe.
- Ads mirror the query in headlines, answer the next question in descriptions, and land on a page that keeps the promise.
- Read search terms, CTR, CPC, conversion rate and CPA in that order, weekly — and act on what they say.
