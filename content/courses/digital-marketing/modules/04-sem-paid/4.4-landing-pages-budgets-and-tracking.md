# 4.4 Landing pages, budgets and tracking

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Design a landing page that keeps the ad's promise and removes every excuse not to act.
- Set budgets with pacing logic and match them to realistic outcomes.
- Set up conversion tracking and UTMs BEFORE launch, and explain why the order is non-negotiable.
- Run a launch-day and first-week checklist for any paid campaign.

## Opening scenario

An educational institute spent ₹40,000 on ads driving to a page with: a carousel of campus photos auto-rotating, an enquiry form with eleven fields (including "mother's occupation"), no fees anywhere, and a phone number in the footer. 1,100 clicks. Nine enquiries.

The rebuilt page had one headline matching the ad ("B.Com with CA coaching — Kalaburagi, admissions open"), fees and batch timings in a table, a three-field form (name, phone, programme), the WhatsApp button, and four student reviews. Same ads, same ₹40,000: 240 enquiries.

The ads did not improve. The **destination did**. A landing page is where a paid campaign's economics are actually decided — and tracking is how you ever find that out.

## Core ideas

### 1. The landing page: one promise, zero friction

The page has one job: convert the specific intent that clicked the ad. The anatomy:

- **Message match:** the headline completes the sentence the ad started. "B.Com with CA coaching" in the ad → the same words as the page's H1. Mismatched pages leak money silently.
- **The answer above the fold:** price, date, or whatever the clicker's next question is (Lesson 2.2's answer-first pattern, applied to ads).
- **Proof stack:** reviews with names, numbers ("2,400 alumni"), photos of the real thing.
- **One action, low friction:** one CTA, repeated down the page. Every form field is a tax on conversion — ask for what you need to follow up, nothing more. Three fields beat eleven, always.
- **Objection handling near the CTA:** return policy, EMI option, "no spam" promise, WhatsApp alternative.
- **No exits:** no nav menu, no "read our blog" — the page is a corridor, not a hall.

Test like a tired phone user on 4G: if the page takes five seconds, you already lost a third of them (Lesson 2.3).

### 2. Budgets and pacing

- **Match budget to the arithmetic:** from your pre-flight (4.1), clicks needed × CPC = the minimum meaningful budget. Below that, the campaign never gathers enough signal to optimise.
- **Daily budget = monthly ÷ days**, and pace it: spend evenly. A budget exhausted by noon daily (or by day 4 of the month) means you bought only the cheapest hours/days — often the worst ones.
- **Start narrow:** one or two ad groups, best keywords/audiences, learn, then widen. Spreading ₹5,000 across seven audiences buys seven pieces of nothing.
- **Seasonality:** admissions, festivals, exam results — bid up when intent peaks; pause honestly when it does not exist.

### 3. Tracking before launch — the non-negotiable order

You cannot optimise what you cannot see, and you cannot retro-fit data you never collected:

1. **Define the conversion:** the event that matters (form submit, WhatsApp click, purchase) — one primary per campaign.
2. **Install the tag/pixel** on the thank-you page or as a click event, and **test it**: submit a test lead, confirm the platform received it. An untested tag is a story, not tracking.
3. **UTM every link:** `?utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=admissions-oct&utm_content=adgroup-dumbbells` — so analytics (Module 6) can attribute traffic to the exact ad group even before platform reporting is trusted.
4. **Baseline the KPI tree numbers** (1.4) so week-one results have meaning.

The professional sequence is always: **page → tracking → ads**. Launching ads to an untagged page is driving at night with the lights off, then asking what hit you.

### 4. Launch-day and first-week checklist

**Launch day:** page loads fast on a real phone; conversion tag fires on a test submission; UTMs present on every ad link; negative keywords loaded; budget and schedule set; bid strategy appropriate (start manual/conservative, move to automated once data exists).

**Day 2–3:** read the search-terms report (social: placement/audience reports) — prune waste early; confirm spend pacing; check CTR against your target.

**Week 1:** compute CPA per ad group/audience; pause the worst 20%; move budget to the best; check frequency and creative fatigue; review the landing page's conversion rate against the assumption in your pre-flight — if reality is 1% vs the assumed 4%, the page is the project, not the ads.

## Try it now

Find any ad (search or social), click it, and audit the landing page against the anatomy above: message match? Answer above the fold? How many form fields? Exits? Score it out of 6 — most real pages score 2.

## Hands-on lab: the full launch kit (45 min)

1. **The landing page spec (20 min):** For your campaign, spec the page: headline (message-matched to your 4.2 ad), the answer that appears above the fold, the proof stack (list real items), the form fields (max 3) or WhatsApp CTA, two objection-handling lines, and what is deliberately absent (nav, links).
2. **Tracking plan (10 min):** Name the conversion event and where the tag fires. Write the exact UTM for two of your ad groups. Describe your test (who clicks what, what you should see where).
3. **Budget and pacing (10 min):** From your pre-flight arithmetic, set the daily budget, the schedule, and the rule for when you would raise or pause it.
4. **The checklists (5 min):** Write your launch-day and week-one checklists (5 items each), including the specific metric that triggers your first pause/boost decision.

> **Deliverable:** landing page spec + tracking plan with UTMs + budget/pacing rules + two checklists. Together with 4.1–4.3 this completes Project Block 3's paid component.

## Common mistakes

- **Ads to the home page.** The click is paid; the welcome mat is not a corridor.
- **Eleven-field forms.** Every field is a tax; collect what you need to follow up.
- **Launch first, track later.** "Later" never comes — the data was never born.
- **Judging on day one.** Auction algorithms need a learning period; judge pacing day 2–3 and performance at week one, with the page as the first suspect.

## Key takeaways

- The landing page keeps the ad's promise: message match, answer above the fold, proof, one low-friction action, no exits.
- Budgets follow the arithmetic and pace evenly; start narrow and widen with evidence.
- Page → tracking → ads, in that order: test the tag, UTM every link, baseline the KPI tree.
- Run the launch-day and week-one checklists; when conversion underperforms, the page is the project.
