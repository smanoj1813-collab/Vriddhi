# 4.3 Display, video and social ads

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Explain the different job of interruption media (display, video, social) versus demand capture (search).
- Choose audiences on social platforms: core targeting, lookalikes, custom audiences and retargeting.
- Write creative that survives the scroll: the 3-second rule for video, the single-idea rule for images.
- Plan a retargeting sequence with frequency discipline.

## Opening scenario

A Chennai boutique selling handloom sarees ran the same ad everywhere: a studio photo of five sarees, the caption "Latest collection available", targeting "women 18–65, all India". Results: cheap reach, almost no sales. The owner concluded "Instagram ads don't work for sarees".

The reboot treated the medium honestly: a 20-second reel of one saree being draped, shot on a phone, with on-screen text "Kalamkari cotton, hand-stitched, ₹2,400 — 11 left in this batch", targeted first at people who had visited the site in 30 days (retargeting) and a lookalike of past buyers, then a second creative for cold audiences telling the weaver's story. Same ₹8,000 budget: cost per sale fell 70%, and the reel itself became the shop's best organic post of the year.

Search ads answer a question someone just asked. **Display, video and social ads must interrupt someone who asked nothing** — a different psychology, a different craft, and the difference between those two sentences is this lesson.

## Core ideas

### 1. Interruption media: the job and the price

Nobody searches for your banner. Interruption media works by earning attention that was headed somewhere else — so it is paid in **relevance to the moment, not to a query**:

- **Display (banners):** broad awareness and retargeting at very low cost per impression; weak for direct response. Works when the offer is visual and the frequency builds memory (retargeting).
- **Video:** the strongest awareness format — sound-off design, 3 seconds to prove relevance, one message per video. Skippable in-stream means you often pay only when someone chooses to watch.
- **Social feed/reels/stories:** the discovery engine of the decade — creative IS the targeting (the platform learns who engages), and message-match to the audience's feed is everything.
- **Click-to-WhatsApp / messaging ads:** the Indian small-business workhorse — the ad's job is only to start a conversation, which the salesperson closes.

The rule from Lesson 1.2 applies strictly: these formats live at **awareness and consideration**. Expecting last-click conversion from interruption media, then calling it a failure, is a category error.

### 2. Audiences: the real levers of social

Social targeting vocabulary (Meta's, which most platforms mirror):

- **Core audiences:** demographics, interests, behaviours, locations. The starting point — and the most over-used. "Women 18–65, all India" is not an audience; it is the absence of one.
- **Custom audiences:** your own people re-found on the platform — website visitors (via the pixel), app users, customer lists (emails/phones), engagers with your profile or videos. Small, warm, high-converting.
- **Lookalike audiences:** the platform finds strangers who resemble your custom audience. The single most powerful growth lever once you have a few hundred customers.
- **Retargeting:** custom audiences used with a specific message — "you looked at this" — at consideration/conversion stages.

The professional sequence for a small budget: **retargeting first** (cheapest conversions), **lookalike second** (scale with the algorithm's best knowledge), **core last** (when you must, with a sharp definition).

### 3. Creative that survives the scroll

- **The 3-second rule:** a video must prove its relevance in the first three seconds — the product visible, the promise legible, sound optional (most feeds are muted). If the logo appears before the story, you have paid to be skipped.
- **One idea per asset:** the saree reel sold one saree, not the collection. Multiple messages per creative average out to zero.
- **Native, not billboard:** the best social ad looks like the feed it lives in — phone-shot, human, with text-on-screen. Studio polish can *underperform* rough authenticity, which is excellent news for small budgets.
- **Specifics on screen:** "₹2,400 — 11 left" does the work adjectives cannot.
- **The creative is the targeting:** on modern algorithms, who sees the ad is decided largely by who engages with it. A blurry message finds a blurry audience.

### 4. Frequency and the retargeting sequence

Frequency — how often one person sees your ad — has a sweet spot: too low and there is no memory; too high and there is irritation (and rising costs). Practical rules: awareness ~2–3 per week; retargeting can run higher because it is relevant; cap or rotate creative when frequency climbs past ~5 with flat results.

A minimal retargeting sequence: (1) to all 30-day visitors — the proof ad (reviews, bestseller); (2) to product-viewers — the specific product with its price and stock; (3) to cart-abandoners — the objection-handling message (return policy, WhatsApp help). Three messages, one job each, and then — this matters — **exclude the converted** so buyers stop seeing requests to buy.

## Try it now

Scroll any social feed for five minutes and log every ad you skipped versus every one that stopped you. For the one that stopped you: what happened in the first three seconds? That single deconstruction is worth more than a glossary of ad terms.

## Hands-on lab: the paid social plan (45 min)

Extend your lab business plan into paid social.

1. **Audience ladder (10 min):** Define three audiences in priority order: a retargeting audience (source: website/engagers), a lookalike (source: customers or enquirers), and one sharp core audience. Write a one-line rationale each.
2. **Creative briefs (15 min):** Write two creative briefs — one for the retargeting audience (proof/product/price, one idea) and one for cold audiences (story/offer). Each: hook (first 3 seconds), on-screen text, CTA, format (reel/story/banner), and the landing destination.
3. **The sequence (10 min):** Map the three-step retargeting sequence with its exclusions (converted buyers excluded, viewers advanced from step 1 to 2).
4. **Budget and guards (10 min):** Split a ₹10,000 budget across the three audiences with your reasoning. Set the frequency guideline and the weekly kill/boost thresholds (which metric decides).

> **Deliverable:** audience ladder + two creative briefs + retargeting sequence + budget split with thresholds. Completes the paid half of Project Block 3.

## Common mistakes

- **Expecting search-style conversions from interruption media.** Different funnel stage, different success metric.
- "'Everyone' is our audience." The absence of an audience definition, priced per impression.
- **Logo-first creative.** Paying to be skipped: the story must lead.
- **Never excluding buyers.** Burning budget asking the converted to convert, while they wonder if you know them at all.

## Key takeaways

- Display, video and social earn attention that asked for nothing; they live at awareness and consideration.
- Audience ladder: retargeting first, lookalikes second, sharp core audiences last.
- Creative survives on the 3-second rule, one idea per asset, native look and specifics on screen — and the creative itself shapes who sees it.
- Manage frequency, sequence the retargeting, and always exclude the converted.
