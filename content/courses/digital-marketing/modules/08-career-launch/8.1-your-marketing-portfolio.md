# 8.1 Your marketing portfolio

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Explain why marketing hiring runs on proof of work, not credentials alone.
- Structure every portfolio piece as a case study: problem, action, result, evidence.
- Select and present your five strongest artefacts from this course for three target roles.
- Publish a portfolio that survives the recruiter's ninety-second scan.

## Opening scenario

Two candidates apply for a Digital Marketing Executive role at a Bengaluru agency. Candidate A's résumé lists fifteen skills — "SEO, SEM, Canva, content writing, analytics, social media…" — with nothing behind them. Candidate B's one-page portfolio links four case studies: "PB1 SEO audit — found 23 crawl errors, fixed, organic clicks +34% in 6 weeks (screenshots + audit table)"; "PB3 campaign — ₹5,000 spent, 61 enquiries, CPA ₹82, what I'd fix next time." The hiring manager spends ninety seconds on each. Candidate B gets the interview before A's résumé is scrolled a second time.

Marketing is a proof-of-work profession. Everything you built in this course — audits, content engines, campaign plans, dashboards — is employable evidence **if it is packaged as decisions and results, not as documents**. This lesson does the packaging.

## Core ideas

### 1. The case-study structure: PARE

Every portfolio piece, from a ₹5,000 campaign to a college fest promo, tells one story in four moves:

- **Problem** — the business situation in one honest paragraph. "A Jayanagara café had 4.2★ reviews but 70% of customers couldn't recall the name. Footfall flat for six months."
- **Action** — what YOU did, in decisions not activities. "Repositioned around 'filter coffee + workspace'; rebuilt the Google Business Profile; a 2-week content plan (format mix: 4 reels, 3 carousels)." Not: "handled social media."
- **Result** — numbers with baselines (Lesson 6.1 discipline). "Map views +48%, branded searches doubled, 22 weekend footfall mentions of 'saw your reel'."
- **Evidence** — screenshots, before/after, the dashboard, the audit table. No evidence, no case study — it's a claim.

And one optional fifth move that senior interviewers love: **"What I'd do differently"** — the honest reflection log from your journals, weaponised as maturity.

### 2. The three target roles and their evidence

This course targets three entry roles. Each reads your portfolio differently:

| Role | What they scan for | Your strongest evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Digital Marketing Executive** (agency/in-house generalist) | Breadth + execution + client safety | PB3 campaign plan, PB2 content engine, social/case reply samples |
| **SEO Executive** | Technical audit ability + content judgement | PB1 audit (with the crawl table), keyword research sheet, a before/after ranking screenshot |
| **Marketing Analyst** | Measurement thinking + honest reporting | PB4 dashboard + insight log, the A/B and incrementality designs |

Same projects, different lead story. For the SEO role, your audit is the headline; for the analyst role, it's a supporting exhibit. **Re-order the portfolio per application** — the first piece must match the job description's first requirement.

### 3. The portfolio vehicle

Simplest professional standard: a Notion page or a simple site, with:

- **A 3-line identity block:** who you are, which role you want, one line of proof ("Ran 4 campaigns with documented results; SEO audit work featured in course demo day").
- **Four to six case studies**, each one scroll deep: PARE structure, evidence images, a skills tag row (SEO · GA4 · Meta Ads).
- **The certificate** (this course's practitioner certification) and any tool certificates (Lesson 8.2).
- **A contact line and a "download my résumé" link.**

What NOT to include: unstarted ideas, password-protected pieces, raw exports with no story, ten near-identical social posts. **Curation IS the skill being judged.** Five pieces with stories beat fifty artefacts without.

### 4. Handling the "no experience" objection

You have no agency internship — so what? Three honest bridges:

1. **Reframe the projects:** "I chose a real local business, ran a real audit/campaign with real constraints (₹5,000, 2 weeks), and documented the results" — that IS experience, self-generated.
2. **One live client, however small:** a relative's shop, a college club, a temple festival committee. One real stakeholder with a real deadline upgrades every case study from exercise to work.
3. **The demo-day pitch (this module's capstone):** presenting results aloud, including failures, is the closest simulation of agency life in the whole course — and becomes a case study itself.

### 5. The ninety-second scan test

Recruiters spend 60–120 seconds on a portfolio. Test yours:

- In 10 seconds: can a stranger tell which role you want? (Identity block.)
- In 30 seconds: can they see one number-led result? (First case study's Result line.)
- In 90 seconds: have they met evidence twice? (Screenshots, dashboards, tables.)

If any check fails, fix the layout, not the content — the scroll order IS your pitch order.

## Try it now

Open any marketer's LinkedIn "Featured" section or portfolio link (agencies proudly link their teams'). Give it the ninety-second scan test and note what made you stay or leave. You are developing the eye that will grade your own page.

## Hands-on lab: build the portfolio skeleton (45 min)

1. **Inventory (10 min):** List every artefact from this course: PB1–PB4, capstone drafts, journals, dashboards, the reply matrix, quiz scores worth showing. Mark each: strong / medium / cut.
2. **Pick the lead case study (5 min):** For the role you actually want first, choose the exhibit that matches its #1 job requirement. Write why in one line.
3. **Write one full PARE (20 min):** Take your strongest completed project (PB1, PB2 or PB3) and write the complete case study: Problem paragraph, Action in decisions, Result with baseline numbers (simulated data labelled honestly), Evidence list, and "What I'd do differently."
4. **Assemble the vehicle (10 min):** Create the Notion page/site skeleton: identity block, case-study sections (one filled, four stubbed with titles), certificate placeholder, contact line. Apply the ninety-second test to your own page and write what failed.

> **Deliverable:** Inventory with cuts + lead-case rationale + one complete PARE case study + the published skeleton link. Lesson 8.2 attaches this to résumé and LinkedIn; the capstone adds its case study at the end of this module.

## Common mistakes

- **The skill list portfolio.** Fifteen skills, zero evidence — it reads as "attended a course."
- **Activities instead of decisions.** "Ran social media" vs "cut from 4 dead platforms to 1, chose reels over statics, doubled profile actions."
- **Numbers without baselines.** "+300%!" from what? The 6.1 discipline is itself a hiring signal.
- **No curation.** Fifty artefacts with no story says "I cannot prioritise" — the one skill every role demands.

## Key takeaways

- Marketing hiring runs on proof of work: package projects as PARE case studies with evidence.
- Lead with the exhibit that matches the target role's #1 requirement; reorder per application.
- A Notion page with 4–6 curated case studies + certificate + contact line is the professional minimum.
- Self-generated client work with real constraints and honest numbers defeats the "no experience" objection.
