# 8.2 Résumé, LinkedIn and certifications

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Write a résumé that survives the six-second scan and the applicant-tracking system.
- Turn project work into quantified bullet points using the action-verb + mechanism + result pattern.
- Optimise a LinkedIn profile for recruiter search: headline, About, Featured, activity.
- Choose certifications worth your time and present them honestly.

## Opening scenario

A hiring manager in Bengaluru posts one Digital Marketing Executive opening on a Monday. By Friday: 340 applications. Each résumé gets a scan — recruiters report 6–8 seconds for the first pass. 300 of them say some version of "hardworking digital marketer, passionate about brands, skilled in SEO/SEM/SMM/Canva/Analytics." They blur into one. The 40 that get shortlisted share one property: **specific verbs, specific mechanisms, specific numbers, near the top.**

"Ran a ₹5,000 Meta campaign for a Mysuru café; 61 enquiries at ₹82 CPA; cut waste by excluding broad-match singles" gets read. "Managed social media ads and gained experience in campaign optimisation" does not. Your work from this course is already in the first category — this lesson moves it onto the page.

## Core ideas

### 1. The résumé: structure for the scan

One page (two maximum if you have real history). Order for a fresher in marketing:

1. **Header:** name, city, phone, LinkedIn URL, portfolio link. (The portfolio link IS the differentiator — Lesson 8.1.)
2. **Summary, 2 lines:** role target + strongest proof. "Digital marketing trainee; ran 4 documented campaigns with ₹5–15k budgets across SEO, social and paid. Portfolio: [link]."
3. **Projects & Experience** (the heart): 3–5 entries, each with quantified bullets.
4. **Skills** (keyword row for the ATS): grouped — SEO (Search Console, keyword research, on-page), Analytics (GA4, Looker Studio), Paid (Meta Ads Manager, Google Ads)…
5. **Education & Certifications.**

**The ATS truth:** most Indian companies screen via keyword-matching software. Mirror the job description's exact terms (within honesty) — "SEO Executive" roles search "on-page SEO", "Google Search Console", "keyword research". A beautiful PDF that never matches a keyword never meets a human.

### 2. The bullet pattern: verb + mechanism + result

Every experience bullet follows: **[Action verb] + [what/how, specific] + [result with number and baseline].**

| Weak (activity) | Strong (bullet pattern) |
|---|---|
| "Handled SEO for a website" | "Audited a 40-page café site; fixed 23 crawl issues and rewrote 8 title/meta sets; organic clicks +34% in 6 weeks" |
| "Managed Instagram page" | "Grew a café's Instagram from 400 to 1,900 followers in 10 weeks with a 3-reel-a-week format system; 41% of new walk-ins cited the page" |
| "Ran ad campaigns" | "Managed ₹5,000 Meta budget across 2 audiences; 61 enquiries at ₹82 CPA; paused the loser after week 1 using the CPA rule" |
| "Made reports" | "Built a 5-panel Looker Studio dashboard (GA4 + manual sheet); cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 10 minutes" |

Numbers discipline from 6.1 applies: baselines or "from X to Y". If a number is simulated (course practice), present it as course work: "Course campaign (practice client): …" — honest labelling protects you in interviews, where every bullet may be probed (Lesson 8.3).

### 3. LinkedIn: being findable and credible

Recruiters search LinkedIn like a search engine — the optimisation is SEO on yourself:

- **Headline** (not just "Student"): "Digital Marketing Trainee | SEO audits, Meta Ads, GA4 dashboards | Documented campaigns: [portfolio link]" — keywords + proof, 220 characters.
- **About:** first 3 lines visible before "see more" — role target, one proof paragraph, portfolio link, contact. Write like the summary, warmer.
- **Featured section:** pin your 3 best case studies (from 8.1) + certificate. This is your portfolio's lobby.
- **Experience/Projects:** mirror the résumé bullets; add the local-business work as real entries.
- **Activity:** one meaningful post a week — a result you got, a tool tip, a campaign teardown observation. Consistency signals a practitioner; silence signals a spectator.
- **The settings that matter:** "Open to work" (recruiter-only if you prefer), skills list matching your résumé, a professional photo. The photo is not vanity; it is click-through rate.

### 4. Certifications: which, why, and honest presentation

Worth your time as a fresher (all free or cheap):

- **This course's practitioner certification** — the portfolio-backed one; list it with the capstone result.
- **Google Skillshop:** Google Ads certifications (Search, and the measurement/analytics ones) — the most recognised free tier in India.
- **Meta Blueprint** (Associate-level digital marketing) — free courses, modest exam fee.
- **Google Analytics certification** (via Skillshop) — pairs naturally with your PB4 dashboard.
- **HubSpot Academy** (Inbound, Email) — free, respected for content/lifecycle roles.

Honest presentation rules: never "certified in" a tool you cannot demo; expiry dates respected (Ads certs expire yearly); certifications are the garnish, not the meal — under the portfolio in every section order. One line in the interview: "I have the Analytics cert, and here's the dashboard I built with it" — that sentence is the whole game.

### 5. The application system

Track applications like a CRM (Lesson 7.3 transfers exactly): company, role, date applied, contact, status, next step, tailoring notes. A spreadsheet with pipeline stages (Applied → Screen → Interview → Offer) — because job search IS a funnel, and measuring it (response rate by résumé version) is Module 6 applied to yourself.

## Try it now

Search LinkedIn for "Digital Marketing Executive" jobs in Bengaluru. Read five postings; list the recurring keywords. Those exact terms — matched honestly — belong in your headline, About, and skills.

## Hands-on lab: the application kit (45 min)

1. **Bullets (15 min):** Rewrite 5 experience bullets from your course work using verb + mechanism + result. Include at least one from each of PB1, PB3, PB4. Baselines mandatory; simulated data labelled.
2. **Résumé skeleton (10 min):** Assemble the one-page structure in a doc: header with portfolio link, 2-line summary, projects section with your 5 bullets, grouped skills row, education + certifications (list the ones you will actually pursue).
3. **LinkedIn headline + About (15 min):** Write the headline (keywords + proof) and the About's first three lines; list the three Featured items you will pin and their captions.
4. **The tracker (5 min):** Create the application sheet with pipeline stages and the columns from the lab. Set your first weekly review slot in your calendar — the ritual from 6.1, applied to the search.

> **Deliverable:** 5 bullets + résumé skeleton + headline/About draft + tracker. Lesson 8.3 prepares you for what happens when the tracker moves to "Interview".

## Common mistakes

- **The passion paragraph.** "Passionate about brands" persuades no one who has read 300 résumés this week.
- **Keywords by creativity.** Writing "S.E.M." when the posting says "SEM" — the ATS matches literally.
- **The empty LinkedIn.** A profile with no Featured items and last activity in 2023 contradicts "active practitioner".
- **Certification theatre.** Eleven badges, zero demos. One cert + one dashboard beats the wall of badges.

## Key takeaways

- One page, portfolio link at top, projects with verb + mechanism + result bullets — built for the 6-second scan and the ATS alike.
- LinkedIn is SEO on yourself: keyword headline, proof-first About, Featured case studies, weekly activity.
- Pursue the recognised free certifications (Skillshop, Blueprint, GA4); present them with the demo that proves them.
- Track your job search as a funnel — the CRM and measurement habits of Module 7 and 6, turned on your own career.
