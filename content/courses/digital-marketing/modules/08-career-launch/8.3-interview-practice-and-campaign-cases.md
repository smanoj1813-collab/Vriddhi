# 8.3 Interview practice and campaign cases

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Use the STAR pattern for behavioural questions, adapted to marketing evidence.
- Answer campaign case questions with a structured framework under time pressure.
- Present a campaign honestly — including what failed and what you changed.
- Handle the ten most common digital marketing interview questions with proof-first answers.

## Opening scenario

The interview is going well until the panel slides a printed page across the table: "A Bengaluru cloud-kitchen brand has ₹50,000 and three months to double weekend orders. Walk us through what you'd do." No warning, no data, four eyes waiting. The candidate who rambles ("First I'd do social media, maybe some ads, content is important…") is gone in the panel's mind within a minute. The candidate who thinks aloud in structure wins — and structure is learnable.

Marketing interviews run on three tracks: **behavioural questions** (your past work), **case questions** (a business problem, solved live), and **proof interrogation** (every claim on your résumé, probed). This lesson equips all three — and it is the last content lesson of the course, so everything from Modules 1–7 gets compressed into answers.

## Core ideas

### 1. STAR, adapted for marketing evidence

The behavioural pattern: **Situation → Task → Action → Result.** The marketing adaptation adds evidence and reflection — STAR-R:

- **Situation:** the business in one sentence ("a Mysuru café, 70% of footfall from walk-ins, no digital presence beyond a listing").
- **Task:** your mandate, with constraints ("bring weekend footfall with ₹5,000 and two weeks").
- **Action:** decisions in sequence, with reasoning ("audience research said location-based discovery; chose GBP + reels over ads because budget; built a 2-week format calendar").
- **Result:** numbers with baselines ("weekend footfall +28% over baseline weekends; 22 customers cited the reel").
- **Reflection:** "If I repeated it, I'd set up the how-did-you-hear log from day one — measurement was late."

Prepare 5 STAR-R stories from this course: one SEO (PB1), one content (PB2), one paid campaign (PB3/m4 labs), one analytics decision (PB4), one crisis/community (m5 labs). Five stories cover 90% of behavioural questions — interviewers ask differently, you answer from the same shelf.

### 2. The campaign case framework: G-S-M-A-P

When they hand you a business and a budget, structure your thinking aloud:

1. **Goal & metrics** — restate the objective and name the KPIs + guardrail ("double weekend orders; guardrail: cost per order must stay under ₹120; leading indicators: map actions, branded search, repeat rate").
2. **Situation & audience** — what you'd need to know ("current channels, order data, review scores; who orders on weekends and from how far").
3. **Strategy & channel mix** — the 70/20/10 logic from Lesson 4.4: core channel (likely: local search + GBP, the highest-intent channel), secondary (retention email/WhatsApp for past customers — cheapest orders), experimental (one creator or one targeted paid test).
4. **Actions & timeline** — weeks 1–12 in three phases: fix the fundamentals (listing, menu, reviews), launch the core, then the experiments; the weekly ritual reading the dashboard.
5. **Prediction & measurement** — what success looks like numerically, what you'd measure weekly, and what would make you stop or scale (the decision rules from 6.4).

Then invite the panel in: "That's my frame — happy to go deeper anywhere." The invitation is a strength signal; monologue is not.

### 3. Presenting campaigns honestly

Every interviewer asks some version of "tell me about a campaign that didn't work." The failing answer denies ("all my campaigns did well"); the winning answer dissects:

- Name the miss with its number ("PB3's display test: ₹1,800 spent, 3 enquiries — my worst CPA").
- Name the diagnosis ("audience was too broad; I ignored my own lesson on intent").
- Name the change ("killed it in week 2, reallocated to search; wrote the rule 'no display without a remarketing list' in the log").
- Name the transfer ("now I ask for intent evidence before any spend").

Honest failure analysis is the single strongest trust signal available to a junior marketer — because it is the one thing that cannot be faked by someone who has never run anything.

### 4. The ten questions you will face

1. "Walk me through a campaign you ran." → STAR-R, numbers, evidence.
2. "How would you improve our [their client's] website/social/ads?" → Mini-GSMAP; ask one clarifying question first.
3. "What's your process for keyword research?" → The 2.1 pipeline: seed → tools → intent grouping → mapping to pages.
4. "A campaign's CPA doubled overnight. What do you do?" → The 4.4/6.4 diagnostic ladder: tracking first, then bids/budget, then creative fatigue, then audience, then landing page.
5. "How do you measure content that doesn't convert directly?" → Assisted conversions, brand-search volume, saves/shares, the how-did-you-hear log (6.4's incrementality honesty).
6. "Tell me about a time you handled negative feedback/criticism online." → The 5.3 reply pattern: public, owned, moved, followed through.
7. "Which tools do you know — honestly?" → Tool names + the demo you can show; no inflation.
8. "Why marketing?" → A specific story from this course, not "I'm creative."
9. "Where do you want to be in three years?" → A role path (Executive → Executive/Analyst with a specialism → Team lead) with the skills you'd add each step.
10. "Do you have questions for us?" → Always two: "What does the first 90 days look like for this role?" and "How does the team decide where budget goes — what's the review rhythm?" (Both prove you think like an operator.)

### 5. The rehearsal protocol

Interview skill is rehearsal, not talent:

- **Aloud, not in your head** — the mouth needs practice the brain cannot substitute.
- **Timed cases:** 10 minutes per case question, thinking aloud, recorded on your phone.
- **The mock loop:** a friend/peer plays the panel with the ten questions shuffled; swap and observe — noticing others' weak spots teaches your own.
- **The evidence pack:** your portfolio open in a tab; offer the screenshot the moment you cite a number ("I can show the dashboard").

## Try it now

Take question 4 (CPA doubled overnight) and speak the answer aloud, timed, for two minutes. Record it. Listen once for structure and once for filler words. You have just started the rehearsal protocol.

## Hands-on lab: the mock loop (45 min)

1. **Case practice (20 min):** Set a 10-minute timer. Answer aloud, recorded: "A Mangaluru dental clinic wants 30 new patient enquiries a month with a ₹15,000 budget. What do you do?" Follow GSMAP; invite follow-ups at the end.
2. **Behavioural round (15 min):** With a partner (or self-recorded), answer five of the ten questions, one STAR-R story each. Rotate roles if partnered — observe for structure, numbers, and the reflection beat.
3. **The failure answer (5 min):** Write then speak your "campaign that didn't work" answer using the four-name pattern: miss, diagnosis, change, transfer.
4. **Scorecard (5 min):** Grade yourself 1–5 on: structure, numbers, honesty, composure, questions-back. Write the one fix for next rehearsal.

> **Deliverable:** Your recorded case answer (or detailed written GSMAP) + the five STAR-R outlines + the failure answer + scorecard with the fix. The capstone's demo-day pitch is this skill, on stage.

## Common mistakes

- **Answering before structuring.** The panel grades the thinking, not the speed of the first sentence.
- **The deny-all-failures answer.** "Everything worked" reads as "never ran anything."
- **Tool inflation.** Claiming Semrush mastery invites a Semrust question you cannot survive; claim what you can demo.
- **No questions back.** It closes the interview on a zero-curiosity note — the opposite of a marketer's job.

## Key takeaways

- Five STAR-R stories from your projects answer 90% of behavioural questions.
- Campaign cases are answered with the GSMAP frame, aloud, ending with an invitation to go deeper.
- Honest failure dissection — miss, diagnosis, change, transfer — is the trust signal that cannot be faked.
- Rehearse aloud, timed, recorded; interview readiness is a practised skill, and demo day is the rehearsal that counts.
