# 6.3 Dashboards and automated reporting

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Choose between Looker Studio and a spreadsheet for a reporting need.
- Build a one-page Looker Studio dashboard from GA4 and ads data.
- Design the five-panel dashboard every stakeholder actually wants.
- Automate delivery so reporting survives busy weeks.

## Opening scenario

A marketing executive at a Mysuru education group spent every Monday building "the weekly report": exporting four Excel sheets from three platforms, deleting columns, pasting into a deck, fixing charts that had shifted. Four hours, every week. A colleague replaced it with one Looker Studio page connected to GA4 and the ads accounts, with a monthly PDF scheduled to the director's inbox. The four hours became ten minutes of reading.

The lesson of the story is not "Looker Studio good, Excel bad." It is this: **manual reporting is a tax you pay weekly for avoiding one hour of setup.** This lesson is that one hour.

## Core ideas

### 1. The tool choice: connected dashboards vs living spreadsheets

Two legitimate worlds:

| Need | Tool | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ongoing visibility; multiple sources (GA4, Ads, Search Console); drill-down; stakeholders | **Looker Studio** (free Google tool) | Native connectors, auto-refresh, shareable link, filters |
| Custom maths across odd sources (WhatsApp counts, manual tallies, CRM sheets); small team; maximum flexibility | **Google Sheets / Excel** | Formulas, IMPORT- functions, low learning curve |
| Both, together | Sheets as a data source in Looker Studio | Manual numbers appear beside platform numbers |

The professional pattern: the dashboard for rhythm, the spreadsheet for the numbers no platform has. Your **manual layer** — "How did you hear about us?" tallies, walk-in counts, WhatsApp conversation counts — lives in a sheet and is a first-class data source, not a second-class citizen.

### 2. The five-panel dashboard

Stakeholders do not want a wall of charts. They want the KPI tree of Lesson 6.1, drawn live. Five panels:

1. **Header KPIs** — the 4–6 numbers (this period vs previous): enquiries, conversion %, spend, CPA, top-channel share. Number widgets, not charts.
2. **Trend** — one time series of the primary KPI with the target line drawn.
3. **Channel breakdown** — the tree's second level: sessions/conversions by source. A bar or stacked chart.
4. **Funnel** — visits → engaged → enquiry → customer. GA4's exploration or a simple bar funnel.
5. **Anomaly notes** — a text panel or the insight log's last three entries, so the numbers carry their explanations.

Design rules: one page; date-range control top-left; every panel's title is a question or claim ("Where do enquiries come from?"), not a metric name ("Source report"); colour reserved for meaning (bad = red, nothing else red on the page).

### 3. Connecting the data

In Looker Studio: **Create → Data source →** pick Google Analytics (GA4), authorize, add; repeat for Google Ads, Search Console. For each connector check the fields — the GA4 source includes session source/medium, key events, engagement time. Blending (joining two sources on a shared dimension like date) is possible but is the number-one source of broken dashboards: prefer side-by-side panels over clever blends.

The spreadsheet route: GA4 add-ons and the ads platforms schedule exports to Sheets; `IMPORTXML`/`IMPORTANGE` pull elsewhere; a `QUERY` or pivot builds the panel. Slower, sturdier, fully yours.

### 4. Automation: make delivery boring

A dashboard nobody opens is a poster. Delivery patterns, cheapest first:

- **Share the link** with view access, opened in the Monday meeting.
- **Scheduled email** (Looker Studio: Share → Schedule email) — PDF lands in inboxes daily/weekly/monthly.
- **Alerts**: GA4 Insights (anomaly detection notifies you when a metric departs from forecast) and ads-platform custom alerts (spend above threshold, CPA above limit).
- **The standing agenda item**: the weekly ritual of Lesson 6.1 opens with the dashboard on screen. Culture is the strongest automation.

### 5. Reporting cadence and the audience principle

Match report to audience, and stop writing reports nobody reads:

- **Weekly, self/team:** the ritual — anomaly, diagnosis, action.
- **Monthly, stakeholder:** the five-panel dashboard PDF with three insight notes: what we did, what happened, what we changed.
- **Quarterly, owner/board:** outcomes against goals, cost per outcome, next quarter's one big bet.

The audience principle: a report's job is to provoke a decision or renew confidence — never to demonstrate effort. If a reader can do nothing with a panel, delete it.

## Try it now

Look at any report you receive regularly (even a WhatsApp daily update). Name, in one sentence, the decision it provoked last week. If you cannot — it is a poster, and you now know its replacement.

## Hands-on lab: the five-panel dashboard (45 min)

1. **Data source (5 min):** Open the GA4 demo property (from Lesson 6.2) or your own. In Looker Studio, create a blank report and connect GA4 as a source.
2. **Panels 1–2 (15 min):** Header numbers: Sessions, Key events, Key event rate, with the date comparison toggle on. Then one time-series of sessions with a breakdown by session default channel group.
3. **Panel 3 (10 min):** A bar chart: sessions by source/medium, top 8. Title it "Where do sessions come from?"
4. **Panels 4–5 (10 min):** A funnel of page_view → session_start-engaged → form_start → form_submit (or the demo's commerce events). Add a text panel: "Insights" — write two real observations from your own panels.
5. **Delivery (5 min):** Use Share to get the view link; schedule an email to yourself for tomorrow; note one GA4 Insight or ads alert you would set for your PB3 campaign.

> **Deliverable:** Dashboard link + your two written insights + the automation choices (cadence, recipients, alert). This becomes the shell of Project Block 4.

## Common mistakes

- **The wall of charts.** Twenty panels, zero decisions. One page, five panels.
- **Clever blends.** Joining sources breaks silently; side-by-side beats smart.
- **No previous-period comparison.** Every number needs its yesterday.
- **Building for the builder.** If the stakeholder won't act on it, it doesn't belong.

## Key takeaways

- Looker Studio for connected visibility; spreadsheets for manual data; use both.
- The five-panel dashboard IS the KPI tree, live: header KPIs, trend, channels, funnel, notes.
- Automate delivery: shared link, scheduled PDF, platform alerts, standing agenda item.
- Reports exist to provoke decisions or renew confidence — never to demonstrate effort.
