# 6.4 Attribution, experiments and optimisation

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Explain last-click and data-driven attribution, and why multi-touch matters in long journeys.
- Run a valid A/B test, including the honesty rules of significance.
- Use incrementality checks and geo experiments as realistic alternatives.
- Turn findings into an optimisation loop with a decision rule.

## Opening scenario

A Kochi home-bakery owner studied her analytics. "Instagram brings no sales," she declared — because last-click attribution credited almost everything to `direct/none` and WhatsApp. So she stopped posting. Two weeks later, enquiries halved. The mistake was not in the data; it was in the reading. Instagram was the **discovery** channel — people saw the reel, then searched or typed the URL days later, and the conversion got credited to the last click, not the first touch.

Attribution is the art and science of deciding which channel deserves credit. Get it wrong and you will fire a channel that was quietly feeding the funnel — the most expensive mistake in measurement.

## Core ideas

### 1. Models: from last-click to data-driven

When a customer's journey is one session, attribution is easy. When it is Instagram → Google search → direct visit → WhatsApp order (four touches over nine days), the single "conversion" must be distributed among them:

- **Last-click:** all credit to the final touch. The default in many older reports; systematically starves discovery channels and overfeeds brand search and direct.
- **First-click:** all credit to the first touch. The opposite bias.
- **Linear:** equal credit to every touch. Fair but naive.
- **Data-driven (DDA):** the platform's machine-learning distribution, GA4's default — it uses the actual patterns (which touch sequences precede conversion more than chance) to weight credit.

The professional stance: **know which model your report is speaking, and never compare numbers across models.** A channel's "conversions" under last-click and DDA are different currencies.

### 2. The honest question: "would it have happened anyway?"

Attribution distributes credit for what happened. It cannot tell you what would have happened **without** the channel. That question — **incrementality** — is the real one:

- Brand search converts brilliantly. But would those people have come anyway, from the reel they saw? Brand-search conversions are often partially incremental, partially harvesting.
- Retargeting ads convert at spectacular rates — to people who were often coming back regardless.

Cheap incrementality checks for small businesses:

- **The pause test:** turn a channel off for two comparable weeks (accepting some risk) and watch the total, not the channel's own number.
- **Geo split:** run the campaign in some cities, not others, and compare. Matched regions (Mysuru on, Mangaluru off) approximate a controlled experiment.
- **The "how did you hear" log** from Lesson 5.1/6.1: the unglamorous truth-teller, free of model bias.

### 3. A/B testing: the experiment that settles arguments

An A/B test compares two versions (A and B) differing in **exactly one thing** — headline, image, bid, landing page. The method:

1. **Hypothesis:** "Changing the headline from 'Best coaching' to 'Free demo class Saturday' will raise form conversion." One change, one mechanism, written in advance.
2. **Split:** the platform randomly assigns visitors to A or B. Randomisation is non-negotiable; a "before vs after" comparison is not an A/B test (time changed too).
3. **Run to sufficiency:** small samples produce noise. Use a significance calculator (any free online tool) before declaring winners; as a rule of thumb for landing pages, wait for at least 100–200 conversions per variant before believing a gap, and more for close calls.
4. **Read honestly:** a 52% vs 48% split on 50 visits means nothing. Also check **secondary effects** — B won form fills but attracted worse leads — before shipping.

Where you can A/B today: Google Ads responsive search ad assets and landing pages (via experiments), Meta A/B test campaigns, email subject lines (Module 7's world), page builders with split-testing.

### 4. The optimisation loop: from findings to actions

Optimisation is a loop with a fixed rhythm, powered by everything in this module:

1. **Read** the dashboard (weekly ritual, Lesson 6.1).
2. **Form ONE hypothesis** from the biggest gap or anomaly.
3. **Test** it — A/B where possible, incrementality check or simple change where not.
4. **Decide with a rule set in advance:** e.g., "If CPA < ₹150 for two weeks, scale budget +20%; if CPA > ₹300 for three days, pause and diagnose." Decision rules written before the data arrive prevent both panic-scaling and sunk-cost patience.
5. **Log and repeat** (the insight log). Compounding is the point: 5% better every month is 80% better in a year.

The budget-reallocation drill, done monthly: rank channels by CPA and by assisted value, move 10–20% of budget from the worst to the best, keep one line item for experiments. Small, continuous, documented — never a wholesale bet on one channel (that's Lesson 4.4's 70/20/10 at work again).

## Try it now

Next time two friends argue about which cafe is better, notice the experiment you'd design: what's the hypothesis, what's the split, how many "trials" before you'd believe the answer? You are already an experimentalist — now formalise it.

## Hands-on lab: the attribution and experiment kit (45 min)

1. **Attribution audit (15 min):** In the GA4 demo property (or your own), open Advertising → Model comparison. Compare Last click vs Data-driven for one conversion type; identify one channel whose credit changes materially and write the sentence: "Under last-click, X appears to ___; under DDA, ___."
2. **Incrementality design (10 min):** For your PB3 campaign, design one cheap incrementality check (pause test or geo split or the how-did-you-hear log): what exactly you'd compare, for how long, and what result would change your mind.
3. **A/B test design (15 min):** Write a full test plan: hypothesis (one change, one mechanism), the split mechanism (platform feature you'd use), sample-size estimate from a free calculator (state inputs), duration, and the decision rule written in advance.
4. **Optimisation loop (5 min):** Write your campaign's decision rules: scale/pause/diagnose thresholds with numbers and time windows.

> **Deliverable:** Model-comparison finding + incrementality design + A/B plan + decision rules. These four artifacts go straight into Project Block 4 and the capstone.

## Common mistakes

- **Reading last-click as truth.** Killing discovery channels that feed the funnel.
- **Comparing across models.** Last-click and DDA numbers are different currencies.
- **Calling before/after an A/B test.** Time changed too — that's not an experiment.
- **Declaring winners on tiny samples.** Noise wears the costume of insight.

## Key takeaways

- Attribution models distribute credit; know which one is speaking, and never mix them.
- The honest question is incrementality: "would it have happened anyway?" — pause tests, geo splits, and the how-did-you-hear log answer it cheaply.
- A/B tests need one change, random assignment, sufficient samples, and a decision rule written in advance.
- Optimisation is a documented loop: read, hypothesise, test, decide by rule, log — compounding monthly.
