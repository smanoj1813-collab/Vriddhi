# Final Assessment Blueprint — VDM-101 Digital Marketing

> **Format** · 90 minutes total · Part A: 60 MCQs in 60 minutes (closed — no tools, no notes) · Part B: two scenario responses in 30 minutes (AI tools permitted, with an AI-use statement) · Weight: 20% of the final grade

## Purpose

The final assessment confirms two things the certificate promises: that the learner **knows the course's core concepts cold** (Part A) and can **reason through a realistic marketing situation with sound judgement** (Part B). Button-clicking in ads managers is not tested here — tools change monthly; concepts and judgement do not. Execution is assessed by the project blocks and the capstone.

## Part A — 60 MCQs (60 marks, scaled to 14)

### Source and construction

- Items are drawn from the per-module quiz banks (`modules/*/quiz.json`, 235 items: 155 lesson items + 80 module-assessment items) plus a **held-back supplementary bank** of the same style maintained by the programme team and never published to learners. At least 40% of any paper must come from the held-back bank.
- Each paper is assembled per the module weighting below; item order and option order are randomised.
- One mark per item; no negative marking; single correct option.
- Items are reviewed each cohort for platform drift (ad-platform interfaces, GA4 changes, DPDP rules) — see the facilitator guide.

### Cognitive level mix (Bloom-lite)

| Level | Share | Example stem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recall | ≈ 30% | "Which metric sits at the top of the social value ladder?" |
| Understand | ≈ 35% | "Why does last-click attribution starve discovery channels?" |
| Apply / judge | ≈ 35% | "A campaign's CPA doubled overnight. What do you check first?" |

### Item-writing standards

- Stem states a complete problem; options parallel in form and length; exactly one defensible answer; distractors are the plausible misconceptions the lessons name.
- No "all of the above"/"none of the above"; no negatives unless bolded.
- Platform-agnostic wording (product families, not interface screenshots, versions or prices).
- Indian small-business contexts; no culturally exclusive references.
- Every item carries an explanation for post-assessment feedback.

## Part B — Two scenario responses (2 × 10 marks, scaled to 6)

### Format

Learners receive two scenarios chosen by the facilitator from the pool below (or new ones of equivalent structure), each answered in **200–300 words**. AI tools are permitted; each response must end with a one-line AI-use statement. Judgement and specificity are scored, not prose polish.

### Scenario pool (rotate; do not publish the chosen pair in advance)

1. **The owner's brief.** A bakery owner says "I want to be number one on Google." Write the conversation you would have: what you would ask, what you would measure first, and what you would realistically promise.
2. **The ₹10,000 campaign.** A tuition centre gives you ₹10,000 and one month for admission-season enquiries. Allocate the budget across channels with reasoning, and state the guardrails you would set before launch.
3. **The 2 a.m. complaint.** A customer posts publicly: "Worst service, ordered 3 days ago, no reply." Draft the public reply and describe the next 48 hours of actions.
4. **The vanity report.** Your client's agency reports "12 lakh impressions, 40k likes" for the quarter. Write the three questions you would ask and the numbers you would demand.
5. **The buying decision.** A client wants to buy a 50,000-address email list "to launch fast". Explain your answer, the risks, and what you would do instead.
6. **The flat campaign.** A campaign has 2,000 clicks and a 0.4% conversion rate after two weeks. Diagnose in order, and write the decision rule you would apply.
7. **The creator pitch.** A local food creator with 60k followers quotes ₹15,000 for one reel. Decide with numbers: what would you check, what would you counter-offer, and what clause protects the boost?
8. **The attribution argument.** The owner wants to cut Instagram because "it brings no sales; everything is direct and WhatsApp". Write your response with evidence you would gather.
9. **The consent audit.** A clinic's reception collects names and numbers "for records" and the owner texts offers to all of them monthly. What must change under DPDP-aligned practice, in what order?
10. **The interview case.** "A Bengaluru cloud kitchen has ₹50,000 and three months to double weekend orders. Walk me through it." Answer as you would in the interview, in structured form.

### Scenario rubric (10 marks each)

| Criterion | 0–1 | 2 | 3 (and 4 where marked) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Identifies the real issue(s)** (3) | Misses the core problem. | Identifies the main issue. | Identifies the main issue and second-order issues (e.g. measurement AND consent AND channel economics). |
| **Applies course frameworks correctly** (3) | None or misapplied. | One framework applied correctly. | Two or more frameworks combined (e.g. KPI tree + 70/20/10 + reply matrix + consent checklist). |
| **Specific, ordered actions** (3) | Vague ("do SEO"). | Concrete actions, partly ordered. | Concrete, ordered, with numbers, owners or thresholds, and what to say to whom. |
| **Honest limits and disclosure** (1) | Absent. | — | States what is unknown or what would be escalated; AI-use statement present. |

## Administration

- **Mode:** in-app (Part A auto-scored; Part B typed or uploaded) or paper for centres without reliable connectivity (Part B then written without AI tools — facilitators note the mode and adjust expectations).
- **Timing:** Part A 60 minutes, 5-minute break, Part B 30 minutes.
- **Permitted materials:** Part A — none. Part B — any AI tool plus the learner's own notes; no communication with other people.
- **Accommodations:** additional time (typically +25%) and reader/scribe for documented needs; Kannada/Hindi term glossaries available for Part A on request; Part B may be answered in English, Kannada or Hindi.
- **Integrity:** randomised papers; seat spacing or in-app lockdown where available; similarity checks on Part B; suspected misconduct handled under the certification policy.
- **Re-sits:** one re-sit within 30 days for learners scoring below 40% on the final; the re-sit mark is capped at 60% of the component (see certification policy).

## Scoring

- Part A raw /60 → scaled /14. Part B raw /20 → scaled /6. Final assessment total /20.
- Learners receive per-module Part A performance, Part B rubric scores, and explanations for every item answered incorrectly.

## Maintenance

- Review items each cohort for platform drift (ads interfaces, GA4 property changes, mailbox sender rules) and legal updates (DPDP Rules commencement, ASCI guidelines); retire items with facility > 0.95 or < 0.25; check discrimination.
- Log item statistics and scenario rubric distributions in the programme's assessment register.
