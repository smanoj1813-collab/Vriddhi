# Assessment Rubrics — VDM-101 Digital Marketing

This document collects every rubric used in the programme so that learners, facilitators and moderators work from the same standards. Each project brief in `projects/` repeats its own rubric; this file is the authoritative version.

## Grading scheme at a glance

| Component | Weight | Instrument | Where the rubric lives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lesson quizzes | 15 | 31 quizzes × 5 MCQs, best attempt, averaged then scaled | Auto-scored |
| Project Blocks 1–4 | 28 (7 each) | Rubrics below | §2 |
| Capstone | 30 | 7-criterion rubric | §3 |
| Final assessment | 20 | 60 MCQs + 2 scenarios | `final-assessment-blueprint.md` |
| Reflection journal | 7 | 31 prompts, holistic rubric | §4 |

**Pass:** ≥ 50 overall **and** capstone ≥ 12/30 **and** ≥ 3 of 4 project blocks submitted **and** ≥ 75% attendance in instructor-led batches **and** ≥ 60% on each of the eight module mini-assessments. **Grades:** Distinction ≥ 80 · Merit ≥ 65 · Pass ≥ 50 · Not yet < 50.

## 1. General marking principles

1. **Evidence over polish.** Marks follow demonstrated work — audits, calendars, dashboards, screenshots, logs — not design quality of the submission document.
2. **Honesty is rewarded.** Labelled practice data, documented failures, stated limits and kill decisions earn marks; concealment and inflated numbers lose them.
3. **AI use is expected and must be disclosed.** Every submission carries an AI-use statement. Missing statements lose the disclosure mark; misleading statements are misconduct.
4. **Numbers need baselines.** A result without a before ("+34%") is uninterpretable; the measurement discipline taught in Lesson 6.1 applies to every submission.
5. **Adaptation, not reproduction.** Templates and examples copied from the lessons without adaptation to the learner's actual business score 0 on the relevant criterion.
6. **Integrity caps.** Specific violations cap a block at 3/7 regardless of other quality: fabricated campaign data presented as real; messaging real people without consent; scraped or bought lists; undisclosed sponsored content; real customer personal data published without permission.
7. **Language equity.** Work in Kannada, Hindi or other Indian languages is assessed on the same criteria; facilitators arrange a competent reader where needed.
8. **Feedback within 10 days**, written against the criteria, with one specific improvement per criterion below the top band.

## 3. Capstone rubric (30 marks)

Scale per criterion: 0–1 · 2 · 3 (professional) · 4 (exemplary), as in the capstone brief.

| Criterion | Marks | Not yet | Meets | Professional | Exemplary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strategy | 4 | No one-pager or vague goal. | Goal numeric, reasoning thin. | One-pager complete; one-liner, KPI tree and 70/20/10 coherent. | Strategy visibly shaped execution choices; trade-offs explained. |
| Execution | 4 | <3 channels or <2 weeks. | 3 channels, asset quality uneven. | 3 channels, 2 weeks, brand-kit consistency. | Channels reinforce each other; owned channel compounding. |
| Measurement | 4 | Numbers missing. | Dashboard, ritual inconsistent. | Five-panel stack, three rituals, one experiment. | Incrementality evidence justifies a reallocation decision. |
| Lifecycle & consent | 4 | Absent. | Sequence present, consent thin. | Consent layer complete; pipeline active. | A/B on the lifecycle; lost-reasons feeding a change. |
| Honest reporting | 4 | Vanity metrics only. | Numbers without baselines. | Baselines, guards, labelled data. | Four-name failure analysis, unprompted depth. |
| The pitch | 6 | Untimed or read from notes. | Clear but overruns or lacks numbers. | 5 minutes, numbers on screen, failure owned. | Panel-grade: calm under probe, invited follow-ups. |
| AI use | 4 | No statement. | Generic statement. | Tools and uses specific; verification described. | AI multiplied output while judgment stayed human. |

**Capstone pass threshold: 12/30**, with no criterion at 0–1 on Strategy or Measurement. The pitch must be delivered live (video only with prior arrangement).

## 4. Reflection journal (7 marks, holistic)

Prompts appear at the end of every lesson (31 total; the capstone week adds a programme-end reflection). Minimum 120 words each.

| Mark | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| 7 | ≥ 28 entries; specific to the learner's own work; names failures and changes in practice; connects modules; honest about AI use. |
| 6 | ≥ 28 entries; mostly specific; some connections. |
| 5 | 24–27 entries or entries present but generic. |
| 4 | 18–23 entries. |
| 2–3 | 10–17 entries. |
| 0–1 | Fewer than 10 entries, or entries evidently AI-generated without personal content. |

## 5. Moderation

- Facilitators mark; a second marker samples **20% of project blocks and all capstones** (or at least 5 per cohort) blind; disagreements > 1 mark on a block or > 4 on the capstone are resolved by discussion and recorded.
- The programme team reviews mark distributions per cohort and adjusts guidance (not marks) where drift is found.
- Learners may request a review within 7 days of feedback; a different marker re-marks against the rubric.

## 6. Late and resubmission policy (summary)

- Up to 3 days late: −1 mark on the block (no penalty with prior notice).
- 4–10 days late: capped at 4/7 (capstone pack: −4).
- One resubmission per block for scores below 4/7 (i.e. below 3.5), within 10 days of feedback, capped at 5/7.
- Full detail in `certification-policy.md`.
