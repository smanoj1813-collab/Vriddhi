# Project Block 1 — SEO Audit

> **Project plan** · 60 minutes guided + 3–4 hours independent work · Submit by the end of Week 3 · Weight: 7% of the final grade

## The brief

Deliver a **prioritised SEO audit** for one real small-business website — the business you have been researching since Lesson 1.1 (or a competitor of it). The audit must be evidence-based (every finding shows its proof), prioritised (a client knows what to do first) and honest (what you did not check, and what cannot be checked without access).

This is the first portfolio-grade deliverable for the **SEO Executive** role: interviews and internships routinely ask exactly this — "here is our site, what would you fix first?"

## What to submit

One document (Google Doc, Word or PDF) containing:

1. **Cover** — business, URL, date, your name, and an **AI-use statement** (what you drafted with AI, what you verified yourself).
2. **Business context (half page)** — the SMART goal from your Lesson 1.4 brief, in client-friendly words.
3. **Keyword map (Lesson 2.1)** — at least 15 phrases: intent, volume bucket, difficulty, target page. Star the money phrases.
4. **On-page findings (Lesson 2.2)** — before/after for the home page and one money page: title, meta description, H1, heading outline, opening paragraph, internal-link plan.
5. **Technical + local checklist (Lesson 2.3)** — PageSpeed mobile score with the top two opportunities; Search Console evidence if available; Google Business Profile scorecard (fields, photos, reviews, replies, posts); NAP consistency check.
6. **Authority plan (Lesson 2.4)** — the citable resource outline, five-outlet pitch list and the pitch email.
7. **Prioritised roadmap** — a table of every finding scored **Impact (1–5) × Effort (1–5)**, sorted, with the top three actions written as instructions the owner could follow without you.
8. **What I could not audit** — a short honest list (no analytics access? no Search Console? say so) — and what you would need to close each gap.
9. **Reflection (250–300 words)** — the finding that surprised you most, and what you would do differently with two more days.

## Coverage requirements

Your audit must include at least:

- 1 indexability finding ( robots/noindex, duplicate versions, dead links )
- 2 on-page findings with rewritten titles/descriptions
- 1 speed finding with a specific fix (name the images or scripts)
- 1 local-SEO finding from the Business Profile scorecard
- 1 authority/link recommendation with a real, named outlet

## Process (suggested)

| When | Do |
| --- | --- |
| Week 2, session 2 | Pick the site; run the technical checklist and PageSpeed; screenshot evidence as you go. |
| Week 2, self-study | Keyword map; on-page before/after for two pages. |
| Week 3, session 1 | Peer review: swap audits; each partner must be able to follow the top-three instructions without asking questions. |
| Week 3, self-study | Prioritisation table, roadmap, reflection; submit. |

## Rubric (7 marks)

| Criterion | 0–1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Evidence** | Findings asserted without proof | Most findings have screenshots/data | Every finding carries visible evidence |
| **Prioritisation** | A flat list of problems | Sorted by severity | Impact × effort table; top three are instructions, not observations |
| **On-page craft** | Titles/descriptions copied | Rewritten, keyword-aware | Rewritten with intent-match, length discipline and a clear before/after |
| **Local + technical** | Missing or generic | Both present | Both present with specific, actionable fixes and profile gaps named |
| **Authority plan** | "Get more backlinks" | One realistic play | A citable resource, real outlets, and a ready-to-send pitch |
| **Honesty & scope** | Gaps hidden or invented | Limits acknowledged | Limits acknowledged with what would close them |
| **Professional presentation** | Hard to follow | Clear structure | A client could act on it without you in the room |
