# Project Block 3 — Integrated Campaign Plan

> **Project plan** · 60 minutes guided + 4–5 hours independent work · Submit by the end of Week 6 · Weight: 7% of the final grade

## The brief

Produce a complete **two-week integrated campaign plan** for your lab business — the planning document a client or agency team could execute without you in the room. It integrates the whole course so far: strategy (Module 1), organic search and content (2–3), paid media (4) and social operations (5), all wired to measurement that Module 6 will execute.

This is the flagship artefact for the **Digital Marketing Executive** role: agencies run on exactly this document.

## What to submit

One document (or a doc + spreadsheet) containing:

1. **Cover** — business, campaign name, dates, your name, **AI-use statement**.
2. **The one-page brief** (Lesson 1.4) — updated with everything you have learned since: SMART goal, audience, message/offer, channels and roles, numbers (baseline → target), budget and time, out of scope, risks.
3. **Funnel arithmetic** (1.2) — the campaign's funnel with expected stage rates, CPAs and the verdict against customer value.
4. **Organic component** — the keyword phrases the campaign content targets (2.1) and the content pieces supporting it (3.1 calendar rows for the campaign window).
5. **Paid component** — pre-flight arithmetic sheet (4.1); campaign → ad group structure with match types and negatives (4.2); audience ladder and creative briefs (4.3); landing page spec, tracking plan with UTMs, budget and pacing rules (4.4).
6. **Social component** — platform strategy one-liner and stop-list (5.1); the campaign-week content plan by format (5.2); reply matrix and escalation card (5.3); creator tier strategy, one scored candidate and their brief — or the UGC engine if you go organic-only (5.4).
7. **The campaign calendar** — a day-by-day grid for the two weeks: every post, ad, email and community action, with owner and channel.
8. **Measurement plan** — the KPI tree for this campaign (which metric is checked daily / weekly), the thresholds for kill/boost decisions, and the attribution question you will ask at conversion.
9. **Contingency** — the three most likely failures and the pre-decided response to each (budget exhausted early? page converts at half the assumption? a public complaint during the campaign?).
10. **Reflection (250–300 words)** — the hardest trade-off you made, what you would run first with ₹5,000 only, and what you would not do even for a good client.

## Coverage requirements

- At least two channels plus one conversion path (DM → WhatsApp counts).
- A named budget (any realistic figure) fully allocated with reasoning.
- At least one creative brief that names the hook, on-screen text and CTA.
- Measurement plan must state, in advance, the numbers that would make you pause the campaign.

## Rubric (7 marks)

| Criterion | 0–1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Strategic coherence** | Channels chosen by habit | Channels justified | Every component traces to the brief, the persona and the funnel |
| **Arithmetic** | Missing or decorative | Present and plausible | Pre-flight + funnel maths consistent, verdicted against customer value |
| **Paid craft** | "Run ads" | Structure present | Ad groups, match types, negatives, audience ladder, UTMs, tested-tag plan |
| **Social & community ops** | Posting plan only | Formats + calendar | Formats, reply matrix, escalation card, creator/UGC plan |
| **Measurability** | Hopes stated | KPIs listed | KPI tree with daily/weekly cadence and pre-committed kill/boost thresholds |
| **Contingency** | None | Risks listed | Pre-decided responses to the three likeliest failures |
| **Executability** | Needs the author | Needs some clarification | An intern could execute it from the document alone |
