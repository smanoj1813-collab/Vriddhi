# Facilitator Guide — VDM-101 Digital Marketing

Version 1.0.0 · Last reviewed September 2026 · For facilitators, placement-cell staff and faculty coordinators running the programme in affiliated colleges.

## 1. What this programme is

A 52-hour, 10-week certificate that takes non-technical undergraduates (B.Com, BBA, BA, B.Sc) from "I know Instagram" to job-ready digital marketing practitioners: strategy and funnels, SEO, content and AI-assisted workflows, paid media, social and community, analytics, email/CRM with consent discipline, and a career launch built on a portfolio of documented results. No coding. Free tools throughout; no ad budget required to pass.

Eight modules, 31 lessons, 4 project blocks, a capstone with a Demo Day pitch, and a final assessment. Every lesson has a 5-question bank; every module ends with a 10-question advanced mini-assessment, with 60% required to unlock the next module. In the app, learners must reach the end of each lesson/brief and submit its quiz before advancing. Everything a learner needs is in the course pack (`content/courses/digital-marketing/`) and rendered in the Vriddhi app under **Courses**.

**What makes it work:** every lesson ends in a lab that produces an artefact; artefacts accumulate into the four project blocks and the capstone; the capstone becomes the portfolio; the portfolio becomes the interview. Protect the labs above everything else.

## 3. Before the cohort starts

- [ ] **Tool check (2 hours):** create fresh free accounts (Google Business Profile, Search Console, Analytics, Looker Studio, Canva, one email tool, one ads platform) and run each module's Try-it-now on the college network. Note blocks and verification steps (SMS OTPs for GBP can stall on shared numbers — plan around it).
- [ ] **Capstone clients:** line up 8–10 willing "clients" in Week 1 — relatives' shops, campus canteen, tuition centres, local clinics, college clubs. A real stakeholder with a real deadline upgrades every artefact from exercise to experience.
- [ ] **Budget position, decided and announced:** the course is passable organic-only. Where small budgets are permitted (₹500–5,000), collect written parent/college consent in Week 1 and record it. Where not, approve organic-only capstones and ad-account walkthroughs on the facilitator's demo account.
- [ ] **Demo Day jury:** one faculty member, one practitioner (agency or freelancer), one local business owner where possible.
- [ ] **Policy:** share the certification policy and the AI-use statement template in Week 1.
- [ ] **Data protection:** decide where learner work is stored (institutional Drive), who has access, and the deletion date. Real-customer data in capstones must follow the Lesson 7.4 consent checklists.
- [ ] **Landscape review:** use §6 to re-check platform interfaces, free-tier limits, mailbox sender rules, ASCI guidelines and DPDP Rules status; record source and review date in the cohort calendar.

## 4. Running sessions well

- **Demo live, fail live.** Open a real ad account, a real GBP, a real GA4 property. When a setting has moved or a verification stalls, narrate the debugging — that is the job.
- **Pairs, mixed disciplines.** B.Com with BA (the numbers person meets the words person). Marketing is a team sport; labs run in pairs.
- **Protect lab time.** If the walkthrough runs long, cut the walkthrough, not the lab. Every lab artefact feeds a graded project.
- **Journals are private by default**; learners share only what they choose. Read a sample each week to gauge understanding.
- **Local businesses as the spine.** Every lesson's running example is Indian and small. Push learners to keep ONE business (their capstone client) as the subject of every lab, so the course assembles itself into the campaign.
- **Model the AI-use statement** on your own materials — the disclosure habit is graded and contagious.
- **Numbers on the board, every week.** Open each session with one learner's real metric and the question "would knowing this change what we do next?" — the Lesson 6.1 ritual, practised until it is reflex.

## 5. Module-by-module notes

**Module 1 — Foundations.** The misconception to kill: "digital marketing = posting on Instagram". Use the owned/earned/paid cards until learners can classify any asset in seconds. The KPI tree and the one-page brief from 1.4 are the course's backbone — do not let anyone leave Week 1 without a SMART goal written for a real business.

**Module 2 — SEO.** Patience is the lesson: set expectations that SEO compounds over weeks. The GBP lab (photos, categories, review replies) gives the fastest visible wins — schedule it early in the week. Insist that PB1's recommendations are ranked by impact × effort; unranked audit lists are the classic beginner failure.

**Module 3 — Content & AI.** The verification habit from 3.2 is the module's point: AI drafts, humans verify — facts, claims, tone, brand voice. Run the "AI drafts, human shapes" exercise live with a local business's page. PB2's calendar must show format reasoning, not just dates.

**Module 4 — SEM & paid.** Highest anxiety module (money!). Use the facilitator demo account for the auction walkthrough. Hammer the pre-flight order from 4.4: tracking BEFORE budget. Where budgets are not permitted, the module's labs run on the demo account and the capstone plans paid channels on paper with full budget maths — a paper media plan is gradable; unmeasured spend is not.

**Module 5 — Social.** The stop-list is the hardest sell — every learner wants five platforms. Make the café case (one thriving account beats five half-alive) with the effort arithmetic on the board. Drill the four-part complaint reply out loud; roleplay the angry customer. The creator lesson: engagement-rate maths by hand before anyone quotes prices.

**Module 6 — Analytics.** The vanity-metrics audit (bring last week's "success" posts) lands the 6.1 lesson fast. GA4: verify EVERY learner's test event in Realtime before the dashboard lab — unverified installs are the number-one failure. Attribution honesty: the Kochi bakery story travels well; retell it with a local example.

**Module 7 — Email & CRM.** Consent FIRST, lists second, journeys third — teach in that order and the module's ethics land before the mechanics. Draw-on-paper before the tool, every time. The Stage-0 sheet CRM is the deliverable; resist tool enthusiasm. The DPDP lesson pairs naturally with any college form audit the class can run live.

**Module 8 — Career launch.** Bring the placement cell in. Run the résumé clinic with real job descriptions pulled live from portals. Every learner does at least one human mock interview in addition to the recorded self-mock — the rehearsal protocol is graded in the Demo Day pitch anyway. Close by re-reading Week-1 goal sentences against actual results.

**Capstone.** Facilitator sign-off at Week 8 (client + goal + consent layer) prevents most failures. Enforce: at least 3 channels, at least one owned, two-week minimum run, baselines recorded BEFORE launch. Normalise the kill decision — a documented stop with its rule is exemplary work, not failure.

## 6. Tool landscape — verify before each batch (last verified September 2026)

Interfaces, free tiers and policies change monthly. The lessons therefore name **product families and concepts**, never interface screenshots or prices. Before each cohort:

| Category | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Search console / keyword tools (Google Search Console, Keyword Planner, Google Trends) | Account access without an ad campaign; India-level data; AI-driven keyword ideas availability |
| Local (Google Business Profile) | Verification options for shared premises; photo/category limits; review-reply policies |
| Analytics (GA4, Looker Studio) | Free tier limits; consent-mode behaviour in India; demo property availability for the labs |
| Ads (Google Ads, Meta Ads Manager) | Minimum budgets; coupon availability for education accounts; policy changes (especially for education/health categories) |
| Email (Mailchimp, Brevo, Zoho Campaigns) | Free-tier contact and send limits; SPF/DKIM setup flow; bulk-sender requirements |
| Social scheduling (Meta Business Suite, Buffer, Later) | Free-tier limits; native vs third-party scheduling reach differences |
| Design (Canva) | Free-tier templates and brand-kit availability; education plans |
| CRM (Google Sheets, Zoho Bigin, HubSpot Free) | Free-tier limits; WhatsApp integration status |
| Law and policy | DPDP Rules commencement stages; ASCI influencer-guideline updates; Google/Yahoo bulk-sender rules; platform ad-policy changes for education and finance categories |

Record findings in the cohort calendar under "Tool notes" and brief learners in Week 1. Where a tool is unavailable, name the substitute and note it for markers.

## 7. Assessment logistics

- **Quizzes** are auto-scored in the app (best attempt). Review the most-missed items at the start of each session — the banks are written so the wrong options are the lesson's named misconceptions.
- **Project blocks:** collect via institutional Drive folder per learner; mark within 10 days against `assessments/rubrics.md`; second-marker samples 20%.
- **Capstone Demo Day (Week 10):** 5-minute pitch + 5 minutes of panel questions per learner; jury scores on the capstone rubric; apply the pass threshold (12/30) and the no-fail rule on Strategy or Measurement.
- **Final assessment (Week 10):** see `assessments/final-assessment-blueprint.md`; choose two scenarios from the pool; arrange readers/extra time as needed; Part A closed, Part B open-AI with disclosure.
- **Journal:** collect at Weeks 5 and 10; mark holistically per `rubrics.md` §4.
- **Integrity concerns:** document and follow `assessments/certification-policy.md` §3; involve the programme lead early. The most common issue in this programme is **fabricated campaign numbers presented as real** — the fix is taught (label simulated data) and enforced (integrity caps).

## 8. Common learner difficulties and responses

| Difficulty | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "No ad budget" | Organic-only path is fully gradable: GBP, content, SEO, email, WhatsApp. Paid channels planned on paper with complete budget maths earn full marks with the constraint disclosed. |
| "I don't have a client" | Week-1 client drive: relatives, campus canteen, clubs, a tutor's tuition centre. One real stakeholder per learner is the single strongest predictor of a good capstone. |
| "The maths scares me" | All course maths is arithmetic — funnels divide, CPA divides, ROAS divides. Run the funnel maths on the board with the class's own numbers until the fear goes. |
| "My English isn't good enough for copywriting" | The best local marketing is often Kannada-first; Lesson 3.3's research method (steal the customers' words from reviews) works in any language. Copy may be submitted bilingually. |
| Accepting the first AI draft | Pair with the 3.2 verification routine; grade the AI-use statement strictly for the first two blocks. |
| Five-platform enthusiasm | The stop-list exercise from 5.1: effort arithmetic on the board. One thriving account, contractually. |
| Vanity-metric reporting | Every session opens with "would knowing this change what we do next?" until the reflex forms. |
| Campaign panic mid-capstone (CPA doubled, reach dropped) | Return to the diagnostic ladders (4.4, 6.4): tracking → bids/budget → creative → audience → page. The ritual exists precisely for this moment. |
| Fear of the Demo Day pitch | The 8.3 rehearsal protocol starts two weeks early; every learner records and reviews themselves at least twice before the day. |

## 9. Adapting the programme

- **Shorter (30-hour) version:** Modules 1, 2, 5 (5.1–5.3), 6 (6.1–6.2) and 8 (8.1, 8.3) with PB1 and a reduced capstone (2 channels, 1 week, no pitch jury); issue a "Digital Marketing Foundations" certificate, not the practitioner certificate.
- **Discipline-specific cohorts (B.Com, BBA, BCA):** keep all modules; swap the running examples for the cohort's field (retail, services, D2C); invite one local practitioner per module.
- **Faculty development:** run Modules 1, 2 and 6 for faculty first — strategy, SEO and analytics transfer directly to college marketing; they become co-facilitators.
- **Self-paced learners:** the app supports it; require the same artefacts; schedule two live checkpoints (Week 5 and Demo Day).

## 10. Feedback and improvement

Collect learner feedback at Weeks 3, 6 and 10 (three questions: what helped most, what confused you, what would you cut). Record platform failures and legal updates. Send a one-page cohort report to the programme team: enrolment, completion, grade distribution, tool notes, three changes recommended. The course pack is versioned; improvements go into the next version, not the running cohort.

*Thank you for facilitating. The learners who finish this programme will be the person in their first workplace who can plan a campaign, read a dashboard, respect consent, and say what they'd do next with numbers. That is worth doing properly.*
