# Assessment rubrics — VHR-101 HR Analytics

## 1. Marking principles

Assess the evidence and reasoning, not tool cost or visual polish alone. Use the project-specific acceptance criteria alongside these common anchors. A correct number without a traceable method is incomplete evidence. A transparent correction is stronger than an unsupported claim of perfection. Synthetic cases must remain labelled, and group submissions require individual contribution records and defences.

## 2. Project blocks — 7 marks each

| Criterion | Maximum | Full-credit evidence | Partial evidence | Weak or absent evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Correctness and reproducibility | 3 | Definitions, inputs and methods are correct; key results can be reproduced; checks reconcile | Some correct work but a material gap or unexplained assumption; 1–2 marks | No usable method or largely incorrect results; 0 |
| Professional judgement and safeguards | 2 | Alternatives, uncertainty, authority and safe data handling shape the decision | Safeguards or trade-offs mentioned but not applied; 1 | Unsupported, unsafe or unbounded recommendation; 0 |
| Clarity, evidence and reflection | 2 | Coherent artefacts, source/version index, specific review response and honest attribution | Understandable but incomplete traceability or reflection; 1 | Unclear or untraceable submission; 0 |

Use whole marks within each criterion. Four blocks contribute 28 overall marks. Read the specific brief before marking; generic process language cannot substitute for the requested domain work.

## 3. Capstone — 30 marks

| Criterion | Maximum | Strong performance | Developing performance | Insufficient performance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem and scope | 5 | 4–5: clear decision, audience, boundaries and success conditions | 2–3: mostly clear but important ambiguity remains | 0–1: no coherent problem or scope |
| Technical method and reproducibility | 8 | 7–8: correct, reconciled calculations and inspectable evidence | 4–6: useful work with limited errors or traceability gaps | 0–3: major errors or non-reproducible claims |
| Professional judgement and safeguards | 6 | 5–6: uncertainty, privacy, quality and authority meaningfully constrain choices | 3–4: sound intent with incomplete application | 0–2: unsupported or unsafe conclusions |
| Decision or handover quality | 6 | 5–6: feasible action, owner, timing, review and residual issues | 3–4: plausible action with missing execution detail | 0–2: vague recommendation or no responsible ownership |
| Presentation and individual defence | 5 | 4–5: clear story, calculations reproduced and contribution explained | 2–3: understandable but uncertain on some evidence | 0–1: cannot explain central work |

The capstone pass condition is at least 12/30. Integrity clearance is separate: a numerical pass does not authorise fabricated results or unsafe data handling. A learner should be able to recompute at least two central values or explain two central decisions from their own evidence during the defence.

## 4. Reflection journal — 7 marks

Award up to 2 marks for coverage across all eight modules, 3 for specific evidence-led corrections or trade-off reasoning, and 2 for a credible development plan with actions and review dates. Length alone is not quality. English, Kannada or Hindi is acceptable by arrangement. Do not require disclosure of personal or medical experiences. A missing entry is a coverage gap, not evidence of misconduct.

## 5. Final assessment marking

Part A has 40 MCQs worth 1.5 raw marks each, no negative marking, total 60. Each of two Part B cases is worth 20 raw marks: definitions/scope 4, method and calculation 6, interpretation/limitations 5, recommended action and safeguards 5. The total raw score out of 100 is scaled to 20 overall marks. Permit method credit for a well-explained approach with a minor arithmetic slip; do not award full interpretation credit for a conclusion contradicted by the learner’s own corrected values.

## 6. Moderation and feedback

Before marking, two facilitators independently score a sample submission, compare each criterion and agree how the anchors apply. Double-mark all borderline award/capstone cases and at least 20% of other work. Record the original marks, differences, agreed outcome and rationale. Do not change assessment weights during a cohort.

Feedback should contain one evidenced strength, one specific correction and one next practice task. Return block feedback within ten days where feasible. Apply late-work and resubmission rules from the certification policy, not improvised penalties. Provide a correction route for factual marking errors and the policy’s appeal route for disputed decisions.
