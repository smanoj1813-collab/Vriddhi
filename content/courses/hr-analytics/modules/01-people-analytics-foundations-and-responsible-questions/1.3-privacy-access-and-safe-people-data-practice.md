# 1.3 Privacy, access and safe people-data practice

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Apply minimisation, access controls and cautious reporting to sensitive workforce information.
- Produce a reviewable data-access and publication checklist using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

A classmate offers a real candidate spreadsheet containing phone numbers, salary expectations and interview notes. It would make the exercise look realistic, but the course does not need those records. The safe choice is to use the supplied synthetic data and practise the same analytical controls.

Namma People Services is a fictional organisation created for this course. All employee IDs, applications, costs, survey responses and workforce events are synthetic. The exercises model decisions an entry-level HR team might support, but they do not authorise live selection, dismissal, health inference or individual employee-risk scoring. Use the supplied practice records rather than collecting real personal data.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Purpose and minimum necessary data

Define why each field is needed before collecting it. Avoid personal identifiers when an aggregate or synthetic dataset answers the question. Access should be based on authorised need, with approved storage and sharing. Employment data can be sensitive even when it is not labelled as such. Never upload identifiable employee or candidate records to public AI tools or personal cloud accounts merely because analysis would be easier.

### 2. Pseudonymisation is not anonymity

Replacing names with IDs reduces direct exposure but can still allow re-identification through a key or combinations such as role, location and rare tenure. Separate any re-identification key from analytical data and restrict access. In this course, no real key is needed because records are fictional. Suppress or combine small groups in published examples and consider whether totals or adjacent charts could reveal the suppressed value indirectly.

### 3. Governance is broader than a consent checkbox

Use the institution’s authorised privacy, employment and information-security process to establish lawful handling, retention and rights procedures. Do not assume employee consent alone resolves all obligations or that one classroom threshold is a legal standard. India’s data-protection requirements and implementation timelines should be checked against current official sources by the programme owner. This course teaches analytical safeguards, not legal advice.

## Worked example

A dashboard shows a team of four employees and a single resignation. Even without names, colleagues may infer the person. For classroom publication use a minimum group-size rule of five, combine groups where meaningful, and inspect totals for back-calculation. Five is a teaching safeguard, not a universal legal threshold or a guarantee of anonymity. Restrict detailed analysis to authorised reviewers.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: data-access and publication checklist (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Reject the real-data offer and document why synthetic data meets the learning purpose.

2. Classify fields as necessary, optional or unnecessary for a recruitment-process question.

3. Design access roles for preparer, HR reviewer and public portfolio viewer.

4. Apply the classroom small-cell rule and check for indirect disclosure through totals.

5. Write storage, retention and deletion steps with an owner and a review date.

**Deliverable:** submit your data-access and publication checklist, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Calling an ID-coded employee dataset anonymous.
- Treating a five-person threshold as a law.
- Uploading real HR records to a public AI service.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Purpose and minimum necessary data:** Define why each field is needed before collecting it.
- **Pseudonymisation is not anonymity:** Replacing names with IDs reduces direct exposure but can still allow re-identification through a key or combinations such as role, location and rare tenure.
- **Governance is broader than a consent checkbox:** Use the institution’s authorised privacy, employment and information-security process to establish lawful handling, retention and rights procedures.
