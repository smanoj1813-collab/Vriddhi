# 4.3 Demand, supply and workforce scenarios

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Model staffing demand and supply using transparent assumptions instead of a single false-precision forecast.
- Produce a reviewable workforce scenario plan using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

The service team expects demand to rise, but the forecast range is wide. Recruitment takes time and attrition is uncertain. The HR analyst must explain a staffing gap under several scenarios rather than present one number as a guaranteed future.

Namma People Services is a fictional organisation created for this course. All employee IDs, applications, costs, survey responses and workforce events are synthetic. The exercises model decisions an entry-level HR team might support, but they do not authorise live selection, dismissal, health inference or individual employee-risk scoring. Use the supplied practice records rather than collecting real personal data.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Translate workload into capacity carefully

A simple demand model divides workload by sustainable productive capacity per FTE under a stated service and quality standard. Include time for training, breaks, support and non-case work appropriately. Do not equate all paid hours with case-processing time. Different skills and shift requirements may prevent staff from being interchangeable. A capacity model supports planning; it is not a licence to intensify work without operational review.

### 2. Project supply with distinct flows

Start with current capacity, then consider expected joins, exits, transfers, changed hours and availability. Use probabilities or scenarios where uncertainty is material. An accepted offer is not a guaranteed join. Separate already approved hiring from proposed positions and include lead time. Avoid subtracting attrition twice, once in the base supply and again in a generic contingency allowance.

### 3. Compare actions, costs and risks

Options include hiring, internal mobility, training, process improvement, demand smoothing and appropriately governed temporary capacity. Each has lead time, cost and quality implications. A workforce gap is not automatically solved by recruiting more people. State the trigger for moving from one scenario to another and review assumptions as evidence arrives. Preserve human review and institutional employment policies.

## Worked example

Expected monthly workload is 9,600 cases. Sustainable productive capacity is 800 cases per FTE, so demand is 12 FTE. Current supply is 10 FTE; expected confirmed capacity additions total 2 and planned departures remove 1, giving 11 FTE and a gap of 1. At 10,400 cases demand becomes 13 FTE, giving a gap of 2 if other assumptions hold.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: workforce scenario plan (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Build base, lower-demand and higher-demand scenarios with explicit workload inputs.

2. Calculate required FTE and supply after joins and departures.

3. Add recruitment lead time and identify when a hiring decision would need approval.

4. Compare hiring with one process or internal-mobility alternative and its risks.

5. Recommend a trigger-based plan and state which assumptions need monthly review.

**Deliverable:** submit your workforce scenario plan, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Counting offer acceptances as guaranteed available capacity.
- Treating every paid hour as productive case time.
- Publishing one forecast without uncertainty or action triggers.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Translate workload into capacity carefully:** A simple demand model divides workload by sustainable productive capacity per FTE under a stated service and quality standard.
- **Project supply with distinct flows:** Start with current capacity, then consider expected joins, exits, transfers, changed hours and availability.
- **Compare actions, costs and risks:** Options include hiring, internal mobility, training, process improvement, demand smoothing and appropriately governed temporary capacity.
