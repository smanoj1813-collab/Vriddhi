# 4.1 Headcount, FTE and movement reconciliation

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Calculate headcount and full-time equivalents and reconcile workforce movements at the correct boundary.
- Produce a reviewable workforce movement and FTE bridge using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

The operations team says it has ten people, but their contracted hours total the equivalent of eight full-time roles. Another report treats transfers into the team as external hires. These mistakes can distort both capacity planning and recruitment demand.

Namma People Services is a fictional organisation created for this course. All employee IDs, applications, costs, survey responses and workforce events are synthetic. The exercises model decisions an entry-level HR team might support, but they do not authorise live selection, dismissal, health inference or individual employee-risk scoring. Use the supplied practice records rather than collecting real personal data.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Headcount and FTE are different

Headcount counts people under defined inclusion rules. FTE expresses contracted or worked hours relative to a stated full-time standard. Choose one basis and do not mix contracted and actual hours silently. FTE is a capacity convention, not a measure of a person’s worth or individual productivity. People on leave or different contracts may need separate treatment depending on the planning question.

### 2. Reconcile movements at the right boundary

Opening plus joins minus exits yields closing headcount only when other changes are absent. At department level add transfers in and subtract transfers out; at organisation level those internal transfers cancel. Check effective dates and employment spells. A rehire may be a new spell for one metric but the same person for another. The dictionary must say how repeated employment is handled.

### 3. Compare stock and capacity

A stable headcount can conceal changing hours, leave or skill availability. Report FTE, critical skills and planned availability where relevant. Avoid counting overtime as permanent capacity without a sustainability and policy check. Workforce plans should show assumptions about workload and service quality, not just a target number of people. Reconcile the plan with approved roles and funding.

## Worked example

Six employees work 40 contracted hours and four work 20 hours weekly. Headcount is 10 and FTE at a 40-hour standard is (6×40 + 4×20)/40 = 8. A department starting with 20 people, adding 3 external joins and 2 transfers in, losing 1 exit and 2 transfers out, closes at 22.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Common mistakes

- Treating FTE as a rating of individual value.
- Counting internal transfers as organisation growth.
- Mixing contracted and worked hours without a label.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Headcount and FTE are different:** Headcount counts people under defined inclusion rules.
- **Reconcile movements at the right boundary:** Opening plus joins minus exits yields closing headcount only when other changes are absent.
- **Compare stock and capacity:** A stable headcount can conceal changing hours, leave or skill availability.
