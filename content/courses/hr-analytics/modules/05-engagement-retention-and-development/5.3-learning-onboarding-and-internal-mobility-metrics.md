# 5.3 Learning, onboarding and internal mobility metrics

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Evaluate development activities beyond attendance and distinguish observed change from demonstrated impact.
- Produce a reviewable development evaluation plan using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

All new hires completed an onboarding video, so a manager declares onboarding effective. Yet support errors remain high. Completion is evidence of participation, not necessarily learning, behaviour change or business impact.

Namma People Services is a fictional organisation created for this course. All employee IDs, applications, costs, survey responses and workforce events are synthetic. The exercises model decisions an entry-level HR team might support, but they do not authorise live selection, dismissal, health inference or individual employee-risk scoring. Use the supplied practice records rather than collecting real personal data.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Build an evidence chain

Track participation, learning evidence, workplace application and relevant outcomes as distinct levels. A quiz can test knowledge but may not show on-the-job behaviour. A supervisor observation can help if criteria are clear and consistently applied. Do not force every training activity into a monetary ROI calculation. Choose a measure aligned with the learning objective and the decision about improving or continuing the programme.

### 2. Use comparable pre and post measures

A pre/post comparison needs consistent tasks, scales, timing and eligible participants. Improvement can reflect practice effects, changing case mix or other support rather than training alone. A comparison group or phased rollout may strengthen evaluation where ethical and practical, but causal claims still require assumptions. Show missing follow-up and attrition from the evaluation sample.

### 3. Monitor access and mobility pathways

Internal mobility metrics can describe application, selection and movement within the organisation. Define eligible employees and opportunity availability. Unequal participation may reflect access barriers rather than low motivation. Support development decisions with job-related criteria and human review. Keep individual assessment details restricted and report aggregate patterns with the same privacy safeguards used elsewhere.

## Worked example

Twenty learners average 60% on a pre-test and 78% on a comparable post-test, an increase of 18 percentage points. Only fifteen have a later workplace observation, so report that denominator separately. If error rates improve during the same period as a new checklist, the data do not isolate the training effect. Recommend follow-up rather than attributing every change to the video.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: development evaluation plan (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Map an onboarding activity to participation, learning, application and outcome evidence.

2. Calculate the pre/post difference and identify missing follow-up.

3. Design a workplace observation checklist using job-related behaviour.

4. Define one internal-mobility access metric with its eligible denominator.

5. Write an evaluation recommendation that separates observed improvement from causal attribution.

**Deliverable:** submit your development evaluation plan, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Calling attendance proof of learning.
- Using unmatched samples as if identical.
- Attributing all change to training when other processes changed.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Build an evidence chain:** Track participation, learning evidence, workplace application and relevant outcomes as distinct levels.
- **Use comparable pre and post measures:** A pre/post comparison needs consistent tasks, scales, timing and eligible participants.
- **Monitor access and mobility pathways:** Internal mobility metrics can describe application, selection and movement within the organisation.
