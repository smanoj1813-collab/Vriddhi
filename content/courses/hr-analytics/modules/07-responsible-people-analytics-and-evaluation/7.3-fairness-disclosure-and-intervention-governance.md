# 7.3 Fairness, disclosure and intervention governance

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Review selection-rate disparities and reporting risks without treating a single ratio as a legal verdict.
- Produce a reviewable responsible-analytics review memo using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

A fictional recruitment audit compares two authorised aggregate groups. One has a lower selection rate, but the sample is small and role mix differs. A manager wants to use one ratio either to declare discrimination proven or to declare the process safe. Both conclusions exceed the evidence.

Namma People Services is a fictional organisation created for this course. All employee IDs, applications, costs, survey responses and workforce events are synthetic. The exercises model decisions an entry-level HR team might support, but they do not authorise live selection, dismissal, health inference or individual employee-risk scoring. Use the supplied practice records rather than collecting real personal data.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. A disparity is a signal to investigate

Selection rate is selected eligible applicants divided by eligible applicants under a defined process. Comparing groups can reveal a disparity worth investigating, but sample size, role mix, job-related criteria and missing data matter. Do not infer demographic attributes from names or photographs. Any real collection or use of sensitive group information requires authorised purpose and safeguards. Classroom groups are synthetic labels, not real identities.

### 2. Do not turn a heuristic into law

A selection-rate ratio compares one group’s rate with a reference group’s rate. The four-fifths heuristic is sometimes discussed in selection auditing, but it is not a universal fairness guarantee or an Indian legal compliance test. A ratio above a chosen screen can still hide harm, and a ratio below it is not by itself a complete legal finding. Escalate substantive concerns to qualified HR and legal reviewers.

### 3. Govern the proposed action

A responsible review covers purpose, data provenance, affected people, access, disclosure risk, error consequences, alternatives and human accountability. Consider whether a simpler process improvement answers the need without individual scoring. Provide a way to correct inaccurate data and contest consequential decisions under applicable policy. Document residual limitations and a review date. Technical performance is only one part of a defensible use case.

## Worked example

Group A has 20 selections from 50 eligible applicants, a 40% rate. Group B has 12 from 40, a 30% rate. B/A is 0.30/0.40 = 0.75. This flags a difference for careful investigation; it does not establish the reason, legality or correct remedy. Compare like roles, inspect selection stages and review sample limitations using authorised aggregate data.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: responsible-analytics review memo (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Calculate both selection rates and the ratio from the fictional group counts.

2. List three questions about comparability and sample quality before drawing conclusions.

3. Write a disclosure review that checks small cells and indirect inference.

4. Compare a structured-interview process improvement with an individual-ranking tool.

5. Prepare a memo recommending authorised review rather than a legal verdict.

**Deliverable:** submit your responsible-analytics review memo, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Inferring protected characteristics from names.
- Calling one ratio a universal fairness certificate.
- Making individual adverse decisions from an aggregate disparity.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **A disparity is a signal to investigate:** Selection rate is selected eligible applicants divided by eligible applicants under a defined process.
- **Do not turn a heuristic into law:** A selection-rate ratio compares one group’s rate with a reference group’s rate.
- **Govern the proposed action:** A responsible review covers purpose, data provenance, affected people, access, disclosure risk, error consequences, alternatives and human accountability.
