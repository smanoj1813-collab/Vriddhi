# 8.3 Capstone presentation and analytical development

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Defend a privacy-safe HR recommendation and create a focused plan for further analytical practice.
- Produce a reviewable capstone presentation and development roadmap using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

The jury asks you to reproduce a recruitment conversion, explain a suppressed dashboard cell and defend the next action. A successful presentation demonstrates traceability, judgement and the ability to correct mistakes—not merely confidence.

Namma People Services is a fictional organisation created for this course. All employee IDs, applications, costs, survey responses and workforce events are synthetic. The exercises model decisions an entry-level HR team might support, but they do not authorise live selection, dismissal, health inference or individual employee-risk scoring. Use the supplied practice records rather than collecting real personal data.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Present the decision path

Use five sections: question and audience, data and safeguards, method and checks, findings and limitations, and proposed action with evaluation. Show a small number of clear visuals. Keep a source and evidence index available for questions. Explain why a more complex method was unnecessary if a simple one answered the decision. Do not imply that completion confers an external HR, software-vendor or professional-body credential.

### 2. Prepare to reproduce and revise

Practise recalculating one rate, one duration and one headcount bridge. Be ready to show filter behaviour and explain a missing-data decision. If the jury finds an error, identify which claims depend on it, correct the analysis and update the recommendation. A transparent correction demonstrates analytical discipline. Defending a known wrong result to avoid embarrassment undermines trust.

### 3. Plan growth through bounded practice

Choose two or three next skills linked to target roles: spreadsheet joins, recruitment cohort tracking, dashboard QA, structured-interview support or evaluation design. Define a practice task, evidence, reviewer and date. Seek supervised opportunities using authorised data; do not collect employee records independently to build a portfolio. Advanced modelling is optional and should follow strong definitions, privacy and evaluation foundations.

## Worked example

A jury discovers that a dashboard used 40 organisation offers as the denominator for 8 department acceptances, instead of the department’s 10 offers. Correct the rate from 20% to 80%, identify every chart and memo statement affected, and explain whether the recommendation changes. A useful development task is to test all, single-group and empty filter cases in three new synthetic dashboards.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: capstone presentation and development roadmap (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Build a five-minute presentation using the five-section decision path.

2. Rehearse reproducing a rate and movement calculation from source values.

3. Prepare an answer about privacy, incomplete follow-up and causal limitations.

4. Record jury feedback as specific corrections or development actions.

5. Write a thirty-day plan with two tasks, review dates and evidence of completion.

**Deliverable:** submit your capstone presentation and development roadmap, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Treating course completion as an external professional credential.
- Defending a known denominator error.
- Seeking real employee data without authorisation for portfolio practice.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Present the decision path:** Use five sections: question and audience, data and safeguards, method and checks, findings and limitations, and proposed action with evaluation.
- **Prepare to reproduce and revise:** Practise recalculating one rate, one duration and one headcount bridge.
- **Plan growth through bounded practice:** Choose two or three next skills linked to target roles: spreadsheet joins, recruitment cohort tracking, dashboard QA, structured-interview support or evaluation design.

## Reflection journal

1. What did you change after checking the example or receiving a review comment? Name the exact input, statement or calculation.
2. Which assumption has the greatest effect on your recommendation, and how would you test it before a real decision?
3. How would you explain this work in a two-minute interview for HR Executive without claiming a classroom simulation was paid experience?

Write 80–120 words in English, Kannada or Hindi with clear technical labels. Keep a specific correction or decision in your portfolio journal.
