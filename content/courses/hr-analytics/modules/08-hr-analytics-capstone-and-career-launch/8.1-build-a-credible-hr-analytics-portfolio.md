# 8.1 Build a credible HR analytics portfolio

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Assemble a reproducible case study with a data dictionary, quality log, dashboard and bounded recommendation.
- Produce a reviewable HR analytics portfolio case study using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

A learner’s dashboard is visually strong but its recruitment rates cannot be traced to source rows. Another learner has a modest chart with clear definitions, checks and a useful recommendation. A recruiter assessing an entry-level analyst needs evidence of trustworthy work, not only design flair.

Namma People Services is a fictional organisation created for this course. All employee IDs, applications, costs, survey responses and workforce events are synthetic. The exercises model decisions an entry-level HR team might support, but they do not authorise live selection, dismissal, health inference or individual employee-risk scoring. Use the supplied practice records rather than collecting real personal data.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Tell the decision story

Structure the case as question, population, data, cleaning, method, finding, recommendation and limitation. Label every dataset and result as synthetic classroom work. Explain why a metric was chosen and what action it supports. Do not invent employer names, paid consulting experience or measured business savings. A rigorous simulation is a legitimate portfolio artefact when its scope is honest.

### 2. Make the analysis reproducible

Include a data dictionary, immutable source copy, cleaning log, formula or pivot notes, QA checks and evidence index. A reviewer should be able to reproduce two headline values without asking you to narrate every step. Keep versions aligned between the memo and dashboard. Export only safe aggregate views for public use; preserve editable work in the institution-approved submission location.

### 3. Match evidence to the role

An HR Executive portfolio can emphasise accurate records and process coordination; an HR Analyst portfolio should show definitions, analysis and interpretation; a Talent Acquisition Associate portfolio can demonstrate funnel management, candidate communication and structured selection support. Job titles vary by employer. Use the actual job description to select evidence and distinguish your own contribution from group work.

## Worked example

A defensible résumé bullet is “Analysed a synthetic 200-application recruitment cohort, reconciled stage conversions and produced a privacy-reviewed dashboard with three process recommendations.” An indefensible bullet is “Reduced a company’s attrition by 40%” when only a classroom scenario was analysed. Claim the analysis and reasoning you performed, not an operational impact you never observed.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: HR analytics portfolio case study (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Create an evidence index linking each headline finding to a formula and source table.

2. Reproduce two metrics from raw data and record the checks.

3. Write a one-page case study with a synthetic-data label and a clear limitation.

4. Prepare one portfolio bullet for each target role without exaggerating experience.

5. Review the public export for hidden data, identifiers and unsupported claims.

**Deliverable:** submit your HR analytics portfolio case study, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |
| Sharing is appropriate | Synthetic-data label and removal of unnecessary identifiers |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Claiming simulated outcomes as real employer impact.
- Submitting only screenshots with no reproducible method.
- Including confidential source data in a public portfolio.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Tell the decision story:** Structure the case as question, population, data, cleaning, method, finding, recommendation and limitation.
- **Make the analysis reproducible:** Include a data dictionary, immutable source copy, cleaning log, formula or pivot notes, QA checks and evidence index.
- **Match evidence to the role:** An HR Executive portfolio can emphasise accurate records and process coordination; an HR Analyst portfolio should show definitions, analysis and interpretation; a Talent Acquisition Associate portfolio can demonstrate funnel management, candidate communication and structured selection support.

## Reflection journal

1. What did you change after checking the example or receiving a review comment? Name the exact input, statement or calculation.
2. Which assumption has the greatest effect on your recommendation, and how would you test it before a real decision?
3. How would you explain this work in a two-minute interview for HR Executive without claiming a classroom simulation was paid experience?

Write 80–120 words in English, Kannada or Hindi with clear technical labels. Keep a specific correction or decision in your portfolio journal.
