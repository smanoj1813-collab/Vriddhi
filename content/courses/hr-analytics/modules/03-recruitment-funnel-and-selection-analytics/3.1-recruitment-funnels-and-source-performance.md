# 3.1 Recruitment funnels and source performance

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Calculate stage conversion and compare candidate sources using consistent cohorts.
- Produce a reviewable recruitment funnel and source note using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

The company’s dashboard divides this month’s acceptances by this month’s offers, even though many acceptances relate to offers from the previous month. Another chart counts duplicate applications as separate people. The recruitment lead needs a coherent cohort view before changing sourcing spend.

Namma People Services is a fictional organisation created for this course. All employee IDs, applications, costs, survey responses and workforce events are synthetic. The exercises model decisions an entry-level HR team might support, but they do not authorise live selection, dismissal, health inference or individual employee-risk scoring. Use the supplied practice records rather than collecting real personal data.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Choose a cohort or a period flow

A cohort follows the same eligible applications or requisitions through stages. A period-flow report counts events in a calendar interval and may include different people at each stage. Both can be useful, but dividing unrelated flows can produce misleading conversion rates. State the entry rule, observation end date and treatment of still-open cases. Recent cohorts have had less time to progress and should not be compared naively with mature cohorts.

### 2. Define each conversion denominator

Application-to-screen conversion, screen-to-interview conversion, offer acceptance and joining yield use different eligible denominators. Show counts beside rates and identify the stage owner. A drop can reflect quality thresholds, candidate withdrawal, process delay or incomplete follow-up. Do not assume all funnel loss is bad: a valid selection process should screen out unsuitable applications using job-related criteria.

### 3. Compare sources beyond volume

Source comparison should consider qualified applicants, conversion, cost, time and downstream outcomes under an agreed definition. Referral and job-board populations may differ by role and seniority. A high raw joining rate does not prove a source causes better performance. Retain multi-source attribution rules and avoid rewriting source labels after seeing outcomes. Recommendations should balance efficiency, access and job-related quality.

## Worked example

A mature cohort has 200 applications, 80 screens passed, 40 interviews, 20 offers, 15 acceptances and 12 joins. Screen conversion is 40%; interview-to-offer conversion is 50%; acceptance is 75%; application-to-join yield is 6%. These percentages describe different transitions. The 188 applications not resulting in a join are not all recruiter failures; investigate stage reasons and eligibility.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: recruitment funnel and source note (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

1. Build the six-stage cohort table and calculate each adjacent conversion.

2. Calculate application-to-join yield and show the original counts.

3. Add open cases and explain how the observation cut-off limits interpretation.

4. Compare two fictional sources within the same role group and cohort definition.

5. Recommend one process check at the largest decision-relevant drop, not simply the largest count drop.

**Deliverable:** submit your recruitment funnel and source note, an assumptions note and a short recommendation. Use a descriptive filename containing the lesson number, your learner identifier and a revision number. Include the case period, units, source label “synthetic classroom case”, author and review date inside the artefact, not only in its filename.

### Lab review and acceptance

Reserve eight lab minutes for partner review. Trace one conclusion to its input, reproduce a calculation or classification and identify a possible misunderstanding. Keep the review comment and your correction or explanation in a two-column change log.

| Acceptance check | Evidence to leave in the submission |
| --- | --- |
| Scope is explicit | The question being answered and what is outside the exercise |
| Work can be reproduced | Source values, rules or formulas, and a manual check |
| Recommendation is actionable | Named role, next action and review trigger |
| Uncertainty is visible | An assumption and the effect if it proves false |

Working alone? Reopen the file as a reviewer. Check the version, evidence basis and distinction between observed facts and proposed actions.

## Common mistakes

- Dividing events from different cohorts without labelling the method.
- Calling every rejected application a process failure.
- Selecting a source based only on raw application volume.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Choose a cohort or a period flow:** A cohort follows the same eligible applications or requisitions through stages.
- **Define each conversion denominator:** Application-to-screen conversion, screen-to-interview conversion, offer acceptance and joining yield use different eligible denominators.
- **Compare sources beyond volume:** Source comparison should consider qualified applicants, conversion, cost, time and downstream outcomes under an agreed definition.
