# 3.2 Time-to-fill, time-to-hire and recruitment cost

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read and discuss 25 min · Worked example 10 min · Hands-on lab 40 min · Quiz and reflection 15 min.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Define recruitment duration boundaries and calculate comparable cost per hire.
- Produce a reviewable recruitment speed and cost scorecard using the supplied practice case.
- Explain one limitation, one verification check and the next decision the evidence supports.

## Opening scenario

A hiring report uses “time-to-hire” for both requisition approval to offer acceptance and candidate application to acceptance. The same label now describes two different clocks. A finance report excludes internal hiring effort while another includes it, making cost comparisons equally unreliable.

Namma People Services is a fictional organisation created for this course. All employee IDs, applications, costs, survey responses and workforce events are synthetic. The exercises model decisions an entry-level HR team might support, but they do not authorise live selection, dismissal, health inference or individual employee-risk scoring. Use the supplied practice records rather than collecting real personal data.

Write your initial recommendation before analysing the case. Keep it for comparison after the lab: what did the evidence change, and why?

## Core ideas

### 1. Name the start and end events

Conventions vary, so this course defines time-to-fill as requisition approval to accepted offer, and time-to-hire as candidate application to accepted offer. State calendar versus working days and whether day zero is excluded. Joining lag is a separate interval. Open requisitions are not completed durations; report their age separately so unresolved work does not disappear from the analysis.

### 2. Use comparable cost boundaries

Cost per hire divides eligible recruitment costs by hires under a defined period or cohort. Specify internal and external costs, allocations, currency and any exclusions. Recruitment costs can occur before the hires they support; match the attribution method carefully. Training and salary are not automatically recruitment cost in every convention. A lower cost per hire does not prove a better hiring process if quality or access deteriorates.

### 3. Diagnose delay before assigning blame

Break duration into approval, screening, scheduling, interview feedback and offer stages when timestamps are reliable. Role difficulty, hiring-manager availability and candidate notice periods can influence outcomes. Compare like roles and use distributions, not only one overall mean. A service target should not encourage skipping fair selection or pressuring candidates. The analyst supports a process improvement decision, not a simplistic recruiter ranking.

## Worked example

A requisition is approved on 1 June; a candidate applies on 11 June and accepts on 21 June. Using elapsed calendar days with start day excluded, time-to-fill is 20 days and time-to-hire is 10 days. Eligible recruitment costs of ₹60,000 for six joined hires give ₹10,000 per hire under a stated joining-cohort allocation. Different cost or hire definitions would produce a different measure.

### Reproduce before interpreting

Recreate the example from its inputs. Label units, show formulas or decision rules and keep an unchanged source copy. Change one assumption and identify which conclusion changes. Check one calculation or classification manually.

Test a relevant edge case: a missing date, zero denominator, incomplete observation or absent approval. Unknown information is not automatically zero or permission to invent a replacement. Record how the uncertainty affects your recommendation.

## Try it now

In pairs, explain the worked example without reading it aloud. The reviewer asks, “What evidence would change this recommendation?” Name the specific record, owner or observation you would request and how it could affect the decision. Swap roles and compare answers.

## Hands-on lab: recruitment speed and cost scorecard (40 min)

Use the fictional case in this lesson. Do not contact real employees, candidates, customers or suppliers for these exercises. A spreadsheet, document and paper sketches are sufficient; named software is a convenience, not a requirement for marks.

## Common mistakes

- Using identical labels for different clocks.
- Dropping all open vacancies from visibility.
- Comparing cost per hire with incompatible cost definitions.

Do not improve the appearance of a result by removing inconvenient records or changing an agreed definition halfway through the exercise. If the rule is wrong, propose a revision and show both versions. The goal is a defensible decision, not a perfect-looking chart or an artificially green status.

## Key takeaways

- **Name the start and end events:** Conventions vary, so this course defines time-to-fill as requisition approval to accepted offer, and time-to-hire as candidate application to accepted offer.
- **Use comparable cost boundaries:** Cost per hire divides eligible recruitment costs by hires under a defined period or cohort.
- **Diagnose delay before assigning blame:** Break duration into approval, screening, scheduling, interview feedback and offer stages when timestamps are reliable.
