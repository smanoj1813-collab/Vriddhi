# Facilitator Guide — VGC-101 GenAI for Career Impact

Version 1.1 · Last reviewed September 2026 · For facilitators, placement-cell staff and faculty coordinators running the programme in affiliated colleges.

## 1. What this programme is

A 60-hour, 10-week certificate that takes non-technical undergraduates (B.Com, BBA, BA, B.Sc) from AI-curious to job-ready practitioners: prompting, content creation (visual, video, audio), custom assistants and no-code automation, a real product pilot, responsible AI including India's DPDP framework, and career launch. No coding. Free tools throughout.

Eight modules, 35 lessons, 5 project blocks, a capstone and a final assessment. Each lesson has a 15-question bank; every module ends with a 10-question advanced mini-assessment, with 60% required to unlock the next module. In the app, learners must reach the end of each lesson/brief and submit its quiz before advancing. Everything a learner needs is in the course pack (`content/courses/genai-certification/`) and rendered in the Vriddhi app under **Courses**.

**What makes it work:** every lesson ends in a lab that produces an artefact; artefacts accumulate into projects; projects become the portfolio; the portfolio becomes the interview. Protect the labs above everything else.

## 3. Before the cohort starts

- [ ] **Tool check (2 hours):** create fresh free accounts and run each module's Try-it-now on the college network. Note blocks (some colleges block image or video tools) and daily limits. Record substitutes in the cohort calendar.
- [ ] **Devices:** confirm lab access; learners on phones only can complete Modules 1–4 and 8 well; Modules 5–6 need a laptop for at least the lab hours — schedule lab rooms.
- [ ] **Language support:** identify facilitators or seniors who can check Kannada/Hindi/Tamil/Telugu outputs for PB2, PB3 and the capstone.
- [ ] **Stakeholders for PB4 and the capstone:** line up 6–10 willing "clients" (departments, clubs, canteen, library, local businesses, NGOs) with real small problems.
- [ ] **Jury for the showcase:** one faculty member, one practitioner, one user-community representative where possible.
- [ ] **Policy:** share the certification policy and the AI-use statement template in Week 1; agree the college's position on recording sessions.
- [ ] **Data protection:** decide where learner work is stored (institutional Drive), who has access, and the deletion date.
- [ ] **Time-sensitive review:** use §6 to re-check tool availability, free-tier limits, data terms, consent/licensing steps, language quality and current legal/policy dates. Verify legal claims against current official sources, record the source/review date in the cohort calendar, and tell learners what changed.

## 4. Running sessions well

- **Demo live, fail live.** When a tool hallucinates or misrenders text in front of the class, stop and run the fact-check routine together. These moments teach more than slides.
- **Pairs, mixed disciplines.** B.Com with BA, BBA with B.Sc. Prompting for someone else's discipline exposes assumptions.
- **Protect lab time.** If the walkthrough runs long, cut the walkthrough, not the lab.
- **Journals are private by default**; learners share only what they choose. Read a sample each week to gauge understanding.
- **Bilingual by design.** Invite Kannada/Hindi prompts in every lab; compare outputs with English ones. Quality differences are content, not distractions.
- **Model the AI-use statement** on your own materials.
- **Keep a "parking lot"** for tool-specific questions; answer them in a shared doc so the session stays on the concept.

## 5. Module-by-module notes

**Module 1 — Foundations.** Common misconception: "the AI looks things up". Use the next-token demonstration in 1.2 repeatedly. Make sure every learner leaves Week 1 with working accounts and a toolkit sheet; late account setup derails Module 2.

**Module 2 — Prompting.** Insist on the evidence log from 2.1; PB1 depends on it. Watch for learners who accept the first output — pair them with the rubric in 2.6 early. Research lesson (2.5): demonstrate a fabricated citation live by asking a non-search chat for references on a niche topic, then verify.

**Module 3 — Visual.** Credits vanish fast; teach the brief-first habit in 3.1 before anyone generates. The video lesson (3.4) is the longest lab in the programme — start it in session and finish in self-study. Ethics (3.5): insist on the four-question routine on every PB2 asset.

**Module 4 — Audio.** Quiet rooms are the constraint; book them. Voice cloning (4.2): read the consent rules aloud; collect consent recordings before any cloning happens. PB3 listener tests must include non-classmates.

**Module 5 — Assistants & automation.** The steepest module for non-technical learners. Slow down on 5.3; make the "Try it now" sentiment flow a whole-class exercise before pairs build their own. Enforce "test to your own email only". The process-design lesson (5.4) is the most employable skill in the programme — spend time on the proposal.

**Module 6 — Capstone.** Facilitator sign-off at Week 7 (problem + interviews) prevents most failures. Push teams toward narrower users. Approve the privacy notice before launch. Expect and normalise pivots and honest "stop" decisions — they are graded well.

**Module 7 — Responsible AI.** Case studies must come from opened sources; ban AI-summarised cases. The bias protocol (7.2) is a lab, not a discussion — every learner runs paired prompts. For 7.4, check the current status of the DPDP Rules commencement stages and any IT Rules changes on synthetic media before teaching, and say what changed.

**Module 8 — Career launch.** Bring the placement cell in. Run a resume clinic with real JDs. Every learner does at least one human mock interview in addition to AI mocks. Close by re-reading Week-10 goal sentences.

## 6. Tool landscape — verify before each batch (last verified September 2026)

Product names, free-tier limits and model versions change every few weeks. The lessons therefore name **product families** and never versions or prices. Before each cohort:

| Category | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Chat assistants (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, Meta AI, DeepSeek, Perplexity) | Free tier exists; file upload and voice available; data-training setting location; custom-assistant builder access (GPTs / Gems / Projects) |
| Research (Perplexity, NotebookLM, deep-research modes) | Source limits per notebook; audio overview availability; Indian-language support |
| Images (chat-assistant generation, Canva Magic Studio, Microsoft Designer, Adobe Firefly/Express, Ideogram, Leonardo) | Daily generation limits; commercial-use terms on free tier; text-rendering quality |
| Video (CapCut, Canva Video, Veo via Gemini/Flow, Runway, Pika, Luma, HeyGen, Synthesia) | Clip length and credits; watermarking; avatar consent flow |
| Audio (ElevenLabs, Google/Azure/OpenAI TTS, Bhashini and Indian-language specialists, Suno/Udio, Descript, Adobe Podcast) | Characters per month; Kannada/Hindi/Tamil/Telugu voice quality; cloning consent flow; music licence terms |
| Assistants & RAG (GPTs, Gems, Claude Projects, NotebookLM, Copilot agents) | Knowledge-file limits; sharing options; data-use terms |
| Automation (Make, n8n, Zapier, Apps Script, Power Automate) | Free operations per month; AI-step availability; college 365/Workspace entitlements |
| No-code apps (Glide, AppSheet, Softr, Airtable, Bubble, Power Apps) | Free user/row limits; AI columns; publishing rules |
| Law and policy | DPDP Rules commencement stage; IT Rules on synthetic media labelling; EU AI Act deadlines; any MeitY advisories; platform AI-labelling rules |

Record findings in the cohort calendar under "Tool notes" and brief learners in Week 1. Where a tool is unavailable, name the substitute and note it for markers.

## 7. Assessment logistics

- **Quizzes** are auto-scored in the app (best attempt). Review the most-missed items at the start of each session.
- **Project blocks:** collect via institutional Drive folder per learner; mark within 10 days against `assessments/rubrics.md`; second-marker samples 20%.
- **Capstone showcase (Week 9):** 5-minute pitch + 5 minutes questions per team; jury scores on the six-criterion rubric; facilitator applies individual adjustments from build logs.
- **Final assessment (Week 10):** see `assessments/final-assessment-blueprint.md` for construction and administration; choose two scenarios from the pool; arrange readers/extra time as needed.
- **Journal:** collect at Weeks 5 and 10; mark holistically.
- **Integrity concerns:** document and follow `assessments/certification-policy.md` §3; involve the programme lead early.

## 8. Common learner difficulties and responses

| Difficulty | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "My English is not good enough for prompting" | Prompt in Kannada/Hindi; compare; the evidence log will show which tool handles it best. Precision matters more than vocabulary. |
| Fear of "cheating" by using AI | Reframe with Automate–Augment–Avoid and the AI-use statement; show how disclosure is professional practice. |
| Accepting the first output | Pair with a sceptic; use the rubric from 2.6; celebrate caught errors publicly. |
| Over-ambitious PB4/capstone scope | Force three Musts; approve scope in writing; remind them that honest "Wizard of Oz" is legitimate. |
| Tool blocked on college network | Use the substitute list; allow phone hotspots for lab hours where policy permits. |
| Team conflict in capstone | Return to the charter; facilitator mediates once; use build logs for individual adjustment. |
| Anxiety about the ethics module ("AI is dangerous") | Keep it analytical: harm, power, remedy, conditions. Run the analysis on their own builds so it feels like craft, not fear. |

## 9. Adapting the programme

- **Shorter (30-hour) version:** Modules 1, 2, 5 (5.1, 5.3, 5.4), 7 (7.1, 7.4), 8 (8.1, 8.4) with PB1, PB4 and a reduced capstone; issue a "Foundations" certificate, not the practitioner certificate.
- **Discipline-specific cohorts:** keep all modules; swap the examples in 2.4, 5.4 and 8.4 for the cohort's field; invite an industry guest per module.
- **Faculty development:** run Modules 1, 2, 7 and 5.1 for faculty first; they become co-facilitators.
- **Self-paced learners:** the app supports it; require the same artefacts; schedule two live checkpoints (Week 5 and the showcase).

## 10. Feedback and improvement

Collect learner feedback at Weeks 3, 6 and 10 (three questions: what helped most, what confused you, what would you cut). Record tool failures and legal updates. Send a one-page cohort report to the programme team: enrolment, completion, grade distribution, tool notes, three changes recommended. The course pack is versioned; improvements go into the next version, not the running cohort.

*Thank you for facilitating. The learners who finish this programme will be the person in their first workplace who knows how to use these tools well and how to say no when it matters. That is worth doing properly.*
