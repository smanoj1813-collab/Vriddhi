# 4.2 Editing, music & voice cloning

> **Lesson plan** · 75 minutes — Read 25 · Lab 40 · Quiz 10

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Record usable voice on a phone and clean it with AI noise removal and levelling.
- Edit audio by editing text (transcript-based editing) and assemble a short piece with music.
- Generate background music and jingles with AI music tools and use them within their licences.
- Explain how voice cloning works, when it is legitimate, and the consent and disclosure rules you must follow.

## Opening scenario

A BA student produces a college radio segment. Her recordings are made in a hostel room — fan noise, a door, a scooter outside. She used to lose an evening to editing. Now: transcript-based editor, delete the stumbles as text, one-click noise removal, a 20-second AI-generated intro tune, voice levelled, export. Thirty minutes. The segment sounds like it came from a studio, and the time she saved goes into better interviews — the part no tool can do for her.

## Core ideas

### 1. Recording well is still step one

AI cleans up a lot, but it cannot invent what the microphone missed.

- **Room:** small, soft, quiet — a cupboard full of clothes beats a marble hall. Switch off the fan for the take.
- **Phone:** hold 15–20 cm from the mouth, slightly off-axis to avoid plosives ("p", "b" pops). Airplane mode. Use the voice-memo app at the highest quality setting, or a free recorder app that saves WAV.
- **Delivery:** stand or sit upright; read from a script written for the ear (Lesson 4.1); if you stumble, pause two seconds and repeat the sentence — the pause makes the edit easy.
- **Room tone:** record ten seconds of silence in the room. Noise tools and editors use it.

### 2. AI-assisted editing

Modern editors (Descript, Adobe Podcast / Premiere, CapCut, Audacity with AI plug-ins, Canva audio) give you:

- **Transcript-based editing:** the tool transcribes; you delete words and the audio is cut. Remove "um", repeated phrases and false starts in seconds. Always listen back — deleting a word can leave an unnatural gap or clipped breath.
- **Noise removal / speech enhancement:** removes fan, hum and echo; some tools ("enhance speech") make a phone recording sound close to a studio microphone. Overuse produces a watery, robotic sound — use the lightest setting that works.
- **Levelling and loudness:** evens out loud and quiet passages and sets the standard loudness for the platform (roughly −16 LUFS for podcasts, −14 for many streaming platforms; your editor usually has a preset).
- **Filler and silence removal:** automatic, but review — pauses carry meaning.
- **Overdub / regenerate a word:** some tools can regenerate a mispronounced word in your voice from text (a form of cloning — see below).
- **Auto-chapters, show notes and titles:** generated from the transcript; edit in your voice.

### 3. AI music

Tools such as Suno, Udio, AIVA, Soundraw, Mubert and the music generators inside Canva or CapCut produce full tracks, loops and jingles from a text description.

- **Prompting music:** genre and mood, instruments, tempo, structure, length, "instrumental" if you do not want vocals, and what it is for: *"Warm, unhurried acoustic instrumental with a light tabla groove, 90 bpm, for a 3-minute explainer podcast about savings; no vocals; simple intro, steady bed, gentle outro."*
- **Use beds, not songs.** Background music should sit under the voice at −15 to −20 dB; choose tracks without strong melodies or vocals.
- **Licences vary and matter.** Free tiers commonly allow personal, non-commercial use only and may restrict monetised uploads; paid tiers grant commercial licences. Editor libraries (CapCut, Canva, YouTube Audio Library) are the simplest safe choice for social content. Keep a record of the source and licence for every track — you will need it for the rights record in Project Block 3.
- **Ethics:** do not prompt for an identifiable living artist's voice or a copyrighted song; do not present generated music as a human artist's work; disclose AI music where an audience would care (a "composed by" credit).

### 4. Voice cloning

**How it works.** A model learns the characteristics of a voice — timbre, rhythm, typical intonation — from as little as a minute of audio, and can then speak any text in that voice, in several languages. The same technique powers "dubbing" tools that translate a video while keeping the speaker's voice.

**Legitimate uses:**

- Your own voice: fixing a mispronounced word without re-recording; producing a Kannada version of your own explainer; scaling a personal channel.
- A consenting colleague or client, for their own content, with a written agreement covering what may be produced and for how long.
- Accessibility: people losing their voice to illness banking it for later use.
- Multilingual localisation of a brand spokesperson, with contract and disclosure.

**Never:**

- Clone any person without documented consent — friend, teacher, public figure, family.
- Produce statements a consenting person did not approve.
- Use a cloned voice to deceive — impersonation calls, fake testimonials, "leaked" audio. These are crimes (cheating by personation, identity theft and related IT Act offences), and "it was a prank" is not a defence.

**The rules for this course:**

1. Only your own voice, or a classmate's with a signed consent line kept in your project record.
2. Every cloned output is labelled as synthetic in show notes or on screen.
3. Store the consent and the record of each use with the project.
4. If a tool asks for a consent recording ("I, [name], consent to…"), do it properly; do not fake it.

**Protecting yourself.** Family "emergency" scam calls using cloned voices are already common in India. Agree a private safe word with your family; verify by calling back; never act on urgency alone. Teaching this to your family is a small, real public service.

### 5. Assembling a short piece

Standard structure for a 2–3 minute audio explainer:

| Segment | Length | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Music intro | 3–5 s | Sting or bed fade-in |
| Cold open / hook | 10–15 s | The problem or a surprising line |
| Intro | 10 s | Who you are, what listeners will get |
| Body | 90–120 s | Three points, signposted ("first… second… finally…") |
| Close | 15 s | One action; where to find more |
| Music outro | 3–5 s | Fade |

Voice track first; music bed under it; duck the bed; level; export MP3 at 128–192 kbps (or WAV for further editing).

## Try it now

Record 45 seconds of yourself reading a paragraph in a noisy room. Run it through an "enhance speech" or noise-removal tool at low, medium and high strength. Listen with headphones. Where does it start to sound artificial? That is your ceiling for that tool.

## Hands-on lab: clean, cut, score (40 min)

1. **Record (8 min):** a 90-second segment from your Lesson 4.1 script, in your own voice, with a deliberate stumble or two and ten seconds of room tone.
2. **Edit (12 min):** transcript-based edit to remove stumbles and fillers; noise removal at the lightest effective setting; level to the podcast preset.
3. **Music (10 min):** generate or select a bed that fits; record the source and licence; place it under the voice, ducked.
4. **Clone (optional, 5 min):** if a tool with a free voice-clone tier is available, clone *your own* voice with a proper consent recording and regenerate one sentence you mispronounced. Label the output.
5. **Export and check (5 min):** MP3, loudness preset, listen on phone speaker and headphones.

**Deliverable:** raw and edited clips, the music licence note, the consent recording if you cloned, and a one-paragraph note on what the tools fixed and what they could not.

## Common mistakes

- **Maximum noise removal.** It smears the voice. Lightest setting that works.
- **Music with vocals or a strong melody under speech.** Listeners cannot follow either.
- **No licence record.** Platforms take down content; clients ask questions.
- **Cloning a classmate "as a joke".** It is a consent violation and possibly a crime.
- **Editing by transcript without listening back.** Gaps and clipped breaths sound worse than the original stumble.

## Key takeaways

- Record well first; AI cleans, it does not restore.
- Transcript editing, noise removal, levelling and auto show-notes make studio-grade output routine.
- AI music: prompt for beds, duck under voice, record the licence, credit and disclose.
- Voice cloning is powerful and legitimate only with documented consent, approved content and labelling; deception is a crime.
- Build a standard structure: intro sting, hook, intro, three signposted points, close, outro.
