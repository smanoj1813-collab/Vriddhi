# 1.3 The GenAI ecosystem: tools, tiers & your starter toolkit

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 45 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Map the generative-AI landscape into six categories and name at least two tools in each.
- Explain the practical differences between the major chat assistants for a student's daily work.
- Read a free-tier limit and decide when a paid plan is (and is not) worth it.
- Set up and secure the accounts you will use for the rest of the course.

## Opening scenario

Priya wants to make a promotional reel for her college's cultural fest. In one evening she tries a chat assistant for the script, an image tool for the poster, a video app for the animation and a music generator for the background track. By midnight she has four half-finished files, three new accounts, one card charge she did not intend, and no reel.

The problem was not effort — it was the lack of a map. Knowing *which category of tool does what*, and *which free tier is enough*, is the difference between an evening of chaos and a finished product. By the end of this lesson you will have both the map and the accounts.

## Core ideas

### 1. The six categories

Every tool you will meet in this course fits one of these boxes. New products appear weekly; the boxes do not change.

| Category | What it does | Representative tools (2026) | You will use it in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Chat assistants** | Text in, text (and increasingly images, files, code) out. The general-purpose workhorse. | ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Microsoft Copilot, Meta AI, DeepSeek | Every module |
| **Research & study** | Assistants that search the live web or *your* documents and show sources. | Perplexity, NotebookLM, Google Scholar with AI features, Semantic Scholar | Module 2, 5, 6 |
| **Image & design** | Generate and edit pictures; design layouts around them. | Image generation inside ChatGPT / Gemini, Microsoft Designer, Canva Magic Studio, Adobe Firefly, Ideogram, Leonardo.Ai | Module 3 |
| **Video** | Text or image to video; talking avatars; AI-assisted editing. | CapCut, Canva Video, Runway, Pika, Luma, HeyGen, Synthesia, Google Veo | Module 3 |
| **Audio** | Text-to-speech, voice cloning, music, cleanup. | ElevenLabs, Google / Azure / OpenAI TTS, Suno, Udio, Descript, Adobe Podcast Enhance | Module 4 |
| **Assistants, automation & apps** | Build your own assistant; connect tools into workflows; build apps without code. | Custom GPTs, Gemini Gems, Claude Projects, Zapier, Make, n8n, Google AppSheet, Glide, Softr, Bubble, Lovable | Module 5, 6 |

**Rule of thumb:** start every task in a chat assistant. Move to a specialist tool only when you need something the assistant cannot produce well (a polished video, a workflow that runs automatically, an app with a database).

### 2. Choosing a chat assistant

The major assistants are more alike than different, and their model versions change every few weeks. Rather than memorise rankings, learn the *durable* differences:

| Consider | Practical guidance |
| --- | --- |
| **Ecosystem** | Gemini is built into Google Workspace (Docs, Gmail, Sheets) and Android; Copilot into Microsoft 365 and Windows. If your college or employer runs one of these, that assistant will be the most convenient. |
| **Files and images** | All major tools accept PDFs, images and spreadsheets. Check the free-tier limits on file size and number of uploads per day. |
| **Web access** | Most now search the web when asked, but you should *check* for a "sources" or "searching" indicator; otherwise treat the answer as training-data recall. |
| **Indian languages** | All major assistants handle Kannada, Hindi, Tamil and Telugu for everyday text. Quality varies for poetry, legal and technical register — test with your own domain. |
| **Custom assistants** | ChatGPT (GPTs), Gemini (Gems) and Claude (Projects) each let you save instructions and knowledge — Module 5. |
| **Privacy controls** | Look for a setting to turn off training on your conversations. Turn it off for anything work-related. |
| **Price** | Free tiers cap the number of messages on the best model per day and then fall back to a lighter model. For coursework that is usually fine. |

Use at least **two** assistants throughout the course and compare their answers on the same prompt. Disagreement between tools is one of the cheapest error-detection methods you have.

### 3. Free tiers, paid tiers and "student" pricing

Free tiers exist to convert you into a paying user, so read them carefully:

- **Message caps** — e.g. a limited number of messages on the top model per few hours, then a downgrade.
- **Generation credits** — image, video and audio tools usually give a small daily or monthly allowance (a handful of images, a few seconds of video, a few minutes of speech). Plan the Module 3 and 4 labs so you do not burn credits on experiments.
- **Watermarks and licences** — many free tiers watermark output or restrict commercial use. Check before you use a generated image for a paid client.
- **Trials that need a card** — set a reminder to cancel; Priya's midnight charge is common.

Several vendors offer discounted or free plans for verified students, and some Indian colleges have institutional access through Google Workspace for Education or Microsoft 365 Education. Ask your college IT office before paying anything. **For this course, nothing beyond free tiers is required.**

### 4. Open models and "local" AI

Beyond the big brands, there are open-weight models (from Meta, Mistral, Alibaba's Qwen, DeepSeek and others) that anyone can download and run — on a laptop or a college server — without sending data to a vendor. You will not run one in this course, but you should know they exist because:

- Businesses handling sensitive data (hospitals, banks, government offices) increasingly prefer them.
- They power many of the cheaper Indian AI products and Indic-language projects (such as the government-backed BharatGen and Sarvam models).
- "Which model, hosted where, trained on what?" is a question you will hear in job interviews for AI-adjacent roles (Module 7 gives you the vocabulary).

### 5. Account hygiene from day one

Treat AI accounts like your bank login:

1. Use a strong, unique password and turn on two-factor authentication.
2. Never paste passwords, Aadhaar numbers, bank details or other people's personal data into an AI tool.
3. Find and set the "do not train on my data" option where offered.
4. Use your personal email for personal accounts and your college email for anything the college provides — do not mix them, or you will lose access when you graduate.
5. Keep a simple sheet of which tools you have signed up for and what tier you are on. You will thank yourself in Week 6.

## Try it now

Ask two different assistants the same question and compare:

```text
I am a first-year BA student in Karnataka with a phone, a shared laptop and no budget.
Recommend a free AI toolkit for (a) studying, (b) writing assignments honestly,
(c) making a club poster, (d) making a 30-second Instagram video.
For each recommendation give one limitation of the free tier.
Keep the answer under 200 words.
```

Note where the two answers agree, where they disagree, and whether either recommended a tool you cannot access in India. Recording disagreements is the habit; you will formalise it in Lesson 2.6.

## Hands-on lab: build and document your toolkit (45 min)

1. **Create the essentials (20 min).** Sign up (or sign in) to:
   - Two chat assistants (recommended: Gemini with your Google account, plus ChatGPT or Claude).
   - One research tool (Perplexity, and NotebookLM with your Google account).
   - One design tool (Canva, free tier) and one image tool if not included (Microsoft Designer or Ideogram).
   - One automation tool (Zapier or Make — you only need the account for now).
   
   Turn on two-factor authentication and locate the privacy / training setting in each.

2. **Record the limits (15 min).** Make a "My AI Toolkit" sheet with columns: *Tool · Category · Account email · Free-tier limit as written today · Watermark or licence note · Date checked.* Fill it from the tools' own help pages, not from memory.

3. **Do a five-minute smoke test (10 min).** In each tool, complete one tiny task (a two-line caption, a single generated image, one web search with sources). If a tool fails or is unavailable in your region, note it and pick the alternative from the table above.

**Deliverable:** your toolkit sheet (a screenshot or link). You will update it in Modules 3, 4 and 5.

## Common mistakes

- **Collecting tools instead of finishing tasks.** Two assistants, one design tool and one automation tool cover 90% of this course.
- **Assuming a tool is free forever.** Limits change; re-check the help page before a project deadline.
- **Using a personal account for college or client work.** Data, files and custom assistants stay with the account. Decide deliberately.
- **Ignoring region restrictions.** Some features roll out to India later; the table gives alternatives for every category.

## Key takeaways

- Six categories cover the whole GenAI landscape: chat, research, image & design, video, audio, and assistants / automation / apps.
- Start in a chat assistant; escalate to specialists only when needed. Use two assistants and compare.
- Read free tiers carefully — caps, credits, watermarks, licences. Nothing beyond free tiers is required for this course.
- Open models and local AI matter for sensitive data and will be part of your professional vocabulary.
- Protect your accounts and your data from day one.
