# 6.4 User insights, growth & the pitch

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 50 · Quiz 10

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Set up a feedback loop and simple analytics for a pilot without any coding.
- Turn raw feedback and usage data into prioritised iterations using AI for synthesis and your judgement for decisions.
- Decide honestly whether to persevere, pivot or stop, and say why.
- Build and deliver a five-minute pitch with a demo, evidence and an ask, using AI to draft and rehearse.
- Prepare the capstone report and presentation to the rubric.

## Opening scenario

Day six of the Yelahanka pilot. The sheet says: 10 growers enrolled, 7 listen most days, 4 replied with their own prices, 2 changed market at least once, 1 asked to stop. Meera's interviews add texture: the growers trust the note because it comes at the same time every morning; two want prices for a crop the team did not include; one thinks the voice sounds "like a government office". The team's pitch will not say "we built an AI app". It will say: *"Seven of ten small growers listened daily for a week; two changed where they sold; one gained ₹600 on a single day. Here is what we learned, what we would build next, and what we need."*

## Core ideas

### 1. Feedback loops that fit a pilot

Set up all three before launch:

| Loop | How | Yields |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **In-product signal** | Reply keywords (GOOD / BAD / STOP), a thumbs up/down in the app, a one-tap emoji | Volume and trend; low effort |
| **Short conversations** | Three-question voice or WhatsApp check-in on day 2 and day 6, in the user's language | Why; unexpected uses; language and tone issues |
| **Observation** | Watch two users use it (in person or on a call) | What they actually do versus what they say |

Log everything in one "Feedback" tab: date, user, channel, verbatim, theme (fill later), action.

### 2. Simple analytics without code

Your data store can measure what matters. Define four numbers from your success measure:

- **Reach** — how many users enrolled (and how many left).
- **Engagement** — how many used it on each day (listened, opened, replied, submitted).
- **Outcome** — how many did the thing the product exists for (changed a market, resolved an issue, completed a task).
- **Quality** — AI feature accuracy from your review sample; complaints per 100 messages.

Track them daily in a sheet with a small chart; ask an assistant to write the formulas. Most no-code app builders show usage; most automation platforms show run counts and errors. Add a UTM-style tag or a distinct sign-up link per channel if you are testing where users come from.

Beware vanity: 300 sign-ups with 5 daily users is a failed pilot; 10 sign-ups with 7 daily users is a strong signal.

### 3. From feedback to iteration

1. **Synthesise** with AI, strictly from your log: *"Here is our feedback log. Group entries into themes; for each theme give count, representative verbatim, and whether it concerns value, usability, trust, language or something else. Do not add themes not present in the data."*
2. **Combine with the numbers**: a theme mentioned by two users who churned outweighs one mentioned by five happy users.
3. **Score candidate changes** on impact (on the outcome number) versus effort (hours), 1–5 each. Do the high-impact low-effort items first; put high-impact high-effort items in the "next sprint" list; drop the rest.
4. **Ship one improvement, measure again.** Small changes, quickly measured, teach more than a redesign.
5. **Close the loop with users**: "You asked for beans prices — added from Monday." Users who see their feedback acted on stay.

### 4. Persevere, pivot or stop

Be honest at the decision date you set in Lesson 6.3:

- **Persevere** if the outcome number moved toward the target and users are asking for more.
- **Pivot** if users are engaged but for a different job than you designed for (the growers who *sent* prices were revealing a two-way product), or a different user type responded far better.
- **Stop** if engagement collapsed after novelty and interviews show no real pain. Stopping with evidence is a successful capstone; the rubric rewards learning, not survival.

Whatever you decide, write it as a short memo: evidence, decision, what you would do with four more weeks.

### 5. The five-minute pitch

Structure (timings for five minutes):

| Time | Section | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:30 | **Hook** | One user, one moment. "At 6 am, Lakshmamma decides which market…" |
| 0:30–1:00 | **Problem and who has it** | The statement; one number from interviews. |
| 1:00–1:30 | **What we built** | One sentence and the architecture in one picture. |
| 1:30–3:00 | **Demo** | Live if it is reliable; a 60-second recorded video otherwise; show the AI feature and one graceful failure. |
| 3:00–4:00 | **Evidence** | Reach, engagement, outcome, quality; one verbatim; what we changed because of feedback. |
| 4:00–4:30 | **Responsibility** | Privacy, language quality, limits, what we refused to build. |
| 4:30–5:00 | **Decision and ask** | Persevere/pivot/stop and why; what you need next (a partner, data access, mentorship, ₹ for credits). |

Slides: eight to ten, sentence titles (Lesson 3.3), one visual per slide, numbers large. Draft the script with an assistant from your build log and memo; then rewrite in your own spoken voice. Rehearse with voice mode as a sceptical judge: *"Interrupt me with the three hardest questions a jury of a professor, a start-up founder and a district official would ask."* Time it; cut until it fits with fifteen seconds to spare.

Delivery: everyone speaks; hand-overs rehearsed; demo has a backup video; the person who knows the numbers answers the numbers questions.

### 6. The capstone report and the portfolio

The two-page report (see the capstone brief) is the professional artefact: problem, users and evidence, solution and architecture, AI feature and its evaluation, results, responsibility, decision, individual contributions. Write it from the build log; keep every number traceable to a sheet. The same material becomes a portfolio case study in Module 8 — write it once, well.

## Try it now

Open your pilot data. Compute reach, engagement and outcome for the last three days. Ask an assistant to draft three chart titles that state the message, not the topic. Which number would you lead the pitch with, and why?

## Hands-on lab: insights, iteration and pitch draft (50 min)

1. **Synthesis (12 min):** run the theme prompt on your feedback log; combine with numbers; list five candidate changes scored on impact and effort.
2. **Iteration (10 min):** implement the top low-effort change now, or schedule it for tomorrow with an owner; write the "we heard you" message to users.
3. **Decision memo (8 min):** persevere / pivot / stop with evidence and the four-week plan.
4. **Pitch outline (15 min):** the seven sections with timings; sentence titles for eight slides; who says what; demo plan with backup.
5. **Rehearsal (5 min):** one run-through of the first ninety seconds with a phone timer; note what to cut.

**Deliverable:** themed feedback synthesis, prioritised change list, decision memo, pitch outline and slide titles, rehearsal note. Final pitch and report are due at the capstone showcase.

## Common mistakes

- **Reporting vanity numbers.** Lead with outcome and daily engagement.
- **Letting AI invent themes.** Strictly from the log; count them.
- **Pitching features instead of evidence.** Judges remember the user and the number.
- **A demo that depends on live Wi-Fi.** Backup video, always.
- **Hiding the failure.** Show it handled; it is your most credible slide.

## Key takeaways

- Three feedback loops (in-product signal, short conversations, observation) and four numbers (reach, engagement, outcome, quality), logged daily.
- Synthesise with AI from the log only; prioritise by impact versus effort; ship small changes and close the loop with users.
- Persevere, pivot or stop — decided on evidence at the date you set, written as a memo.
- Five-minute pitch: hook, problem, what we built, demo with a failure handled, evidence, responsibility, decision and ask; sentence titles; rehearsed with a sceptical AI judge and a timer.
- Report and pitch come from the build log; every number traceable.

## Reflection journal

Write 120 words: *What did users do with your product that you did not design for? Which single number will you lead your pitch with, and what would have to be true for you to lead with a different one?*
