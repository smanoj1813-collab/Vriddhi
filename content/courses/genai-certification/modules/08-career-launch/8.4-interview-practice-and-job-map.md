# 8.4 Interview practice & your AI-era job map

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 50 · Quiz 10

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Build a bank of STAR stories from your course projects and experiences, and deliver them in under two minutes.
- Run realistic mock interviews with AI (text and voice), including HR, role-specific and "how do you use AI" questions, and extract usable feedback.
- Practise group discussions and presentations with AI-generated scenarios and critique.
- Map realistic roles for commerce, management, arts and science graduates where GenAI skills are an advantage, and plan your first ninety days of job search.

## Opening scenario

Priya from the Yelahanka team walks into a campus interview for an operations associate role. The interviewer asks the standard question — "Tell me about a time you solved a problem" — and then, unusually, "How do you use AI in your work?" Priya has a two-minute STAR story about the pilot (situation, task, action, result — including the failure at 6 am and the kill switch), and a one-minute answer about her prompt playbook, her verification routine and the ethics audit she ran on her own product. The interviewer stops taking notes and starts asking real questions. That is what preparation looks like from the other side of the table.

## Core ideas

### 1. The STAR story bank

Interviewers ask for behaviour: *tell me about a time you…* (solved a problem, worked in a team, handled conflict, failed, learned something fast, persuaded someone, dealt with ambiguity). Answer with **STAR**: Situation (two lines), Task (your responsibility), Action (what *you* did — the bulk), Result (numbers, outcome, what you learned).

Build eight to ten stories, each tagged with the competencies it evidences. Your course work supplies most:

| Story source | Competencies it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Capstone: pivot after interviews | Learning, humility, user focus, decision-making |
| Capstone: 6 am integration failure and fix | Problem-solving, ownership, working under pressure |
| PB4: stakeholder who feared automation | Empathy, persuasion, change management |
| PB2: bilingual poster typo caught by native reader | Attention to detail, process, asking for help |
| PB5: bias found in own Placement Coach | Integrity, analytical thinking, ethics |
| PB3: listeners preferred the "wrong" voice | Openness to feedback, testing over opinion |
| PB1: A/B test result | Rigour, evidence-based decisions |
| Team charter conflict resolved | Teamwork, communication |

Write each story in 150–200 words; practise aloud until it is under two minutes without notes. Use an assistant to *tighten* ("keep my facts, cut to 150 words, make the Action section 60% of the story") and to *interrogate* ("what follow-up questions would a sceptical interviewer ask about this story?").

### 2. The "how do you use AI" answer

Increasingly asked, and badly answered by most candidates ("I use ChatGPT for everything"). A strong answer has four parts, sixty to ninety seconds:

1. **Where it helps you** (two specific tasks with a tool and a result).
2. **How you keep it honest** (your verification routine; a time it was wrong and you caught it).
3. **Where you do not use it** (a judgement call — personal data, final decisions about people, anything you cannot check).
4. **Something you built** (the automation, the assistant, the audit) — with a number.

Prepare it as a STAR-like story and have the portfolio link ready.

### 3. Mock interviews with AI

**Text mocks (structured practice):**

```text
You are a hiring manager for [role] at [type of company] in [city]. Conduct a
25-minute interview with me: 2 HR questions, 4 behavioural questions targeted at
this JD [paste], 2 role-specific scenario questions, 1 question about how I use
AI, and time for my questions. Ask one question at a time, follow up on weak or
vague answers, and do not give feedback until the end. At the end, score me on
clarity, evidence, relevance and confidence (1–5 each) with two specific
improvements per answer.
```

**Voice mocks (delivery practice):** use the assistant's voice mode with the same brief; record yourself; listen for fillers, pace, rambling beyond two minutes, and whether your Result was clear. Some tools give feedback on pace and filler words directly.

**Panel and stress variants:** ask for a panel with different personas (technical lead, HR, a sceptical senior); ask for interruptions and "why should we hire a B.A. graduate for this?" questions. Practise staying calm and returning to evidence.

**After each mock:** log the questions that hurt, the stories that worked, and one fix. Three mocks per target role is a reasonable minimum. AI mocks are practice partners, not oracles — its scoring is a prompt for reflection, not a verdict. Do at least one mock with a human (placement cell, senior, faculty).

### 4. Group discussions and presentations

**GD practice:** ask an assistant for five GD topics current in your field (business, social, abstract), with a two-minute briefing and three arguments per side. Practise the structure: open with a framing (definition + scope), add one evidence-based point at a time, build on others ("adding to what X said…"), disagree with reasons, summarise if you can. Record a group session with classmates; have the assistant transcribe and score each participant on contribution quality, not volume.

**Presentation practice:** deliver your capstone pitch to voice mode acting as a jury; ask for questions after; ask for feedback on structure and clarity. For slides, apply Lesson 3.3; for delivery, rehearse with a timer until you finish fifteen seconds early.

Salary reality: entry-level ranges vary widely by city and sector; use current postings and placement-cell data, not assistants, for numbers.

### 6. Your ninety-day plan

Week-by-week is too fine; think in three blocks:

- **Days 1–30 — Prepare and apply:** master resume + three tailored versions; portfolio complete; LinkedIn live with four posts; ten outreach messages; twenty targeted applications; three AI mocks per target role plus one human mock.
- **Days 31–60 — Iterate:** track applications in a sheet (company, role, date, source, status, feedback); adjust resume and stories based on interviews; two informational conversations a week; one small freelance or volunteer project that adds a portfolio piece.
- **Days 61–90 — Convert:** focus on the two or three pipelines with traction; prepare company-specific stories; negotiate respectfully with evidence; if nothing has converted, review with a mentor and change the target, the materials or the geography — not your effort.

Keep the playbook, the toolkit sheet and the journal alive; they are the habits that made the course work, and they will make the job work.

## Hands-on lab: story bank, mock, job map (50 min)

1. **Story bank (15 min):** eight STAR stories, 150–200 words each, tagged with competencies; assistant tightens; you rehearse two aloud.
2. **Mock interview (20 min):** one full text or voice mock for a real posting using the prompt above; log scores, painful questions and fixes.
3. **Job map (10 min):** with an assistant and real job boards, list ten current postings that match your discipline row; note the recurring skill words; pick three to apply to this week.
4. **Ninety-day plan (5 min):** fill in the three blocks with dates and numbers; share with a study partner for accountability.

**Deliverable:** story bank, mock-interview log, job map with three chosen postings, ninety-day plan. Congratulations — this is the last lab of the course. Your Week-10 goal sentence from Lesson 1.1: read it again and write what changed.

## Common mistakes

- **Stories with no Result.** End with the number and the lesson.
- **"I use ChatGPT for everything."** Specific tasks, verification, boundaries, something built.
- **Treating AI mock scores as verdicts.** Reflection prompts; do a human mock too.
- **Searching by job title only.** Search by tasks and tools; titles vary.
- **Applying everywhere with one resume.** Tailor; track; iterate.

## Key takeaways

- Eight to ten STAR stories from your projects, under two minutes each, tagged by competency; rehearsed aloud.
- The "how do you use AI" answer: where it helps, how you keep it honest, where you refuse, what you built.
- AI mocks (text and voice, panel and stress variants) plus one human mock per target role; log and fix.
- GD and presentation practice with AI-generated scenarios, transcripts and critique.
- A discipline-specific job map, task-based searching, and a ninety-day plan with numbers.
