# 8.2 LinkedIn & your professional brand

> **Lesson plan** · 75 minutes — Read 25 · Lab 40 · Quiz 10

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Build a LinkedIn profile that recruiters find and trust: headline, About, Featured, Experience, Skills.
- Plan and produce four weeks of credible content from your course work, in your own voice, with AI as an editor.
- Write outreach messages to alumni, recruiters and professionals that get replies.
- Manage your brand responsibly: honesty, privacy, consistency and disclosure.

## Opening scenario

Two graduates from the same college apply to the same start-up. The founder does what founders do: types their names into LinkedIn. One profile: a headline that says "Student at X College", no photo, no posts. The other: *"B.A. Journalism · Built a bilingual AI audio series reaching 1,200 listeners · Certified GenAI Practitioner · Looking for content & communications roles"*, a Featured section with the demo video and the ethics audit, and four short posts describing what she learned building things. The founder has already decided whom to call before reading either resume.

## Core ideas

### 1. What LinkedIn is for (and what it is not)

LinkedIn is a searchable database of professionals plus a publishing platform. Recruiters search by keywords, location and role; hiring managers check profiles before interviews; alumni are reachable. It is not a place for inflated titles or motivational noise. Your goal: **findable, credible, specific.**

### 2. The profile, section by section

| Section | What good looks like | AI's role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Photo and banner** | Clear, recent photo, plain background, face 60% of frame; banner from your PB2 brand kit or a plain colour with a line of text | Background removal/cleanup only — no AI-generated "professional headshot" of a face that is not quite you |
| **Headline** (220 chars) | Degree/role · two proof points with numbers or names · certification · what you seek. Not "Student at…" | Draft five variants; you pick and edit |
| **About** (first 3 lines visible) | Line 1: who you are and what you do. Lines 2–5: two or three achievements in ATRE form. Last lines: what you are looking for and how to reach you. 120–200 words, first person, plain | Structure and tightening; your voice, your facts |
| **Featured** | Portfolio link, capstone demo video, one strong post, certificate | — |
| **Experience / Projects** | Each course project as a Project entry (or under a "Vriddhi GenAI Certificate" experience) with one or two ATRE bullets and media | Bullet rewriting without added facts |
| **Education** | Degree, college, university, years; relevant coursework and this certificate | — |
| **Skills** | 15–25 real skills matching your target roles' JDs; pin top three; earn endorsements from teammates | Extract skill vocabulary from target JDs |
| **Licences & certifications** | Certified GenAI Practitioner (Vriddhi) with the credential link when issued | — |
| **Custom URL and Open to Work** | `linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname-xyz`; Open to Work (recruiters only, or public) with target roles and locations | — |

Set your location to where you can actually work; recruiters filter by it.

### 3. Content that builds credibility

You do not need to be an influencer. **Eight to twelve honest posts over four weeks**, then one a week, is enough to be visibly real.

**A four-week plan from your course work:**

| Week | Post ideas (pick two) |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | What I built in PB4 and the number that mattered · One mistake AI made in my project and how I caught it |
| 2 | The capstone user who changed our design · A 60-second demo video with two lines of context |
| 3 | What auditing an AI tool for bias taught me (PB5) · Three things about DPDP every small business should know |
| 4 | My prompt playbook — one entry that saves me an hour a week · What I learned about Kannada/Hindi AI quality |

**Formats:** text posts of 80–200 words with a line break after the first sentence; a carousel from PB2's design skills; a short video; a document post (your one-page proposal, anonymised).

**Writing with AI without sounding like AI:** draft from your own notes ("here is what happened, here is the number, here is what I think"); ask the assistant to tighten and to remove clichés; then re-add one concrete detail only you know; read aloud; delete anything you would not say to a person. Lesson 2.6's AI tells apply doubly here.

**Engagement:** comment thoughtfully on five posts a week in your field (real questions, real additions); follow companies and people in your target roles; reshare with your own take, not just "great post".

### 4. Outreach that gets replies

The people most likely to help you are alumni of your college and people one step ahead of you in the role you want. Messages that work are **short, specific, easy to answer and not asking for a job**:

```text
Hi [Name], I'm a final-year B.Com student at [College] — I saw you moved from
[college/role] into [role] at [Company]. I've just built [one-line project with
a number] and I'm trying to understand what [role] work actually looks like day
to day. Could I ask you two questions by message, or take 15 minutes of your time
next week? Happy to share the project if useful. Thank you — [Name]
```

Rules: personalise the first line truthfully; one ask; make saying yes cheap; never attach a resume unasked; follow up once after a week; thank and report back ("I took your advice and…"). Track outreach in a sheet: name, date, message, reply, next step. Expect 20–30% replies; that is success.

For recruiters: apply through the posting first, then a two-line note referencing the application and one proof point.

### 5. Brand responsibility

- **Honesty:** no inflated titles ("Founder & CEO" of a class project), no invented metrics, no claiming team work as solo. Say "co-built" and name your part.
- **Privacy of others:** anonymise clients, users and classmates in posts and media; no faces of pilot users without consent; nothing from an employer's confidential material.
- **Consistency:** the same name, photo, headline story and numbers across resume, portfolio and LinkedIn — discrepancies read as dishonesty.
- **Disclosure:** where a post is substantially AI-drafted, say so lightly ("drafted with an assistant, edited by me"); where you share AI-generated visuals, label them.
- **Tone:** no politics-by-accident, no complaining about employers or professors, no hot takes you cannot defend in an interview.
- **Security:** a strong unique password and two-factor authentication; be wary of "recruiters" asking for payments or documents.

## Try it now

Write your headline in the formula: *Degree/role · proof point 1 · proof point 2 · certification · seeking.* Ask an assistant for five variants under 220 characters using only the facts you gave. Pick one, edit it, and update your profile now.

## Hands-on lab: profile, four posts, three messages (40 min)

1. **Profile (15 min):** headline, About, Featured (portfolio + one video), two project entries with ATRE bullets, skills from two target JDs, custom URL, Open to Work settings.
2. **Content plan (10 min):** eight post ideas from the four-week table; draft two posts fully in your voice; run the AI-tell check; schedule them.
3. **Outreach (10 min):** identify three people (two alumni, one professional one step ahead); write personalised messages; log them in a tracker; send one today.
4. **Consistency check (5 min):** compare resume, portfolio and LinkedIn for names, numbers and dates; fix mismatches.

**Deliverable:** profile link, two drafted posts, outreach tracker with three messages, consistency-check notes.

## Common mistakes

- **Headline = "Student at X".** Wasted search real estate.
- **About in the third person, full of adjectives.** First person, facts, ask.
- **AI-generated headshots** or posts that sound like a press release.
- **Outreach asking for a job in message one.** Ask for advice; make it cheap to say yes.
- **Inconsistent numbers across resume, portfolio and LinkedIn.**

## Key takeaways

- Findable, credible, specific: headline with proof points, first-person About with ATRE achievements, Featured evidence, JD-matched skills.
- Eight to twelve honest posts over four weeks from your course work, in your voice, AI as editor with an AI-tell check.
- Outreach: personalised, one cheap ask, no resume unasked, follow up once, report back; track it; 20–30% replies is success.
- Brand responsibility: honesty, others' privacy, consistency, light disclosure, tone, account security.

## Reflection journal

Write 120 words: *What is the one line in your About section that only you could have written? Which outreach message are you most nervous to send, and what is the realistic worst outcome?*
