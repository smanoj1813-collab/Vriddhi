# 8.1 Resume & portfolio that pass both machines and humans

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 30 · Lab 50 · Quiz 10

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Explain how applicant tracking systems (ATS) and AI screeners read a resume, and format for them without gaming.
- Write achievement statements (Action – Task – Result – Evidence) from your course projects and other experience.
- Tailor a resume to a specific job description using AI as an editor, not a ghost-writer.
- Assemble a portfolio from your course work that a hiring manager can review in five minutes.
- Use an AI-use statement appropriately in job applications.

## Opening scenario

Harini, B.Com, has "Proficient in MS Office, good communication skills, team player" on her resume — like four hundred other applicants. After this course she has: *"Built a GST-invoice triage automation for a Mangaluru trading firm (Make + AI classification) that cut weekly manual entry from 6 hours to 40 minutes; error rate 6% → under 1% over a 3-week pilot; hand-over doc adopted by the firm."* One line, one project, three numbers, one verified outcome — and a link to the demo video. The recruiter watches ninety seconds and calls.

## Core ideas

### 1. How resumes are actually read

- **ATS parsing**: the system extracts text into fields (name, contact, education, experience, skills). Two-column layouts, tables, text boxes, icons, headers/footers and images break parsing. Use a single-column, standard-font PDF (or DOCX if requested) with standard section names: Summary, Experience, Projects, Education, Skills, Certifications.
- **Keyword matching**: systems and recruiters search for terms from the job description — tools, skills, role words. Include the ones you genuinely have, in the words the JD uses ("customer support" and "client servicing" are different searches).
- **AI screening and ranking**: increasingly, models summarise and score resumes against the JD. They reward specific, quantified, relevant achievements and penalise vagueness. They can also be biased (Lesson 7.2) — one more reason to make achievements concrete and verifiable.
- **Human skim (6–10 seconds)**: the top third of page one decides whether the rest is read. Put the summary and the strongest two achievements there.
- **Do not**: stuff hidden keywords (white text), invent tools, use a "resume hack" prompt in the document to influence AI screeners (this is prompt injection; it is detectable and disqualifying).

### 2. Achievement statements

Format every bullet as **Action – Task – Result – Evidence**:

> *Built* (action) *a bilingual Kannada–English audio explainer series on PM-Kisan eligibility* (task) *that reached 1,200 listeners in three weeks and was adopted by a self-help group of 40 members* (result) *— links in portfolio* (evidence).

Rules: start with a strong verb (built, designed, automated, analysed, reduced, launched, tested, trained); one achievement per bullet; at least one number; name the tool only when the JD cares; no adjectives about yourself.

Mining the course for achievements — each project block yields at least one:

| Course work | Achievement angle |
| --- | --- |
| PB1 Prompt Playbook | Designed and evidence-tested a library of N reusable prompts for [discipline] tasks; documented verification checks |
| PB2 Campaign Kit | Produced a multi-format bilingual campaign (poster, posts, 30-s video, infographic) for [client]; accessibility and rights record |
| PB3 Audio Episode | Produced a bilingual audio explainer; listener-tested with N people; published with transcript |
| PB4 Automation/App | Automated [process] for [stakeholder]; hours saved; error reduction; hand-over adopted |
| Capstone | Led/co-led an AI product pilot with N users over N days; outcome metric; privacy notice; decision memo |
| PB5 Ethics Audit | Audited [tool] for bias, transparency and privacy; N-run paired test; recommendations |
| Course overall | Certified GenAI Practitioner (Vriddhi), 60 hours, 6 assessed projects |

Also mine: part-time work, family business roles, club positions, volunteering, internships — with the same ATRE discipline.

### 3. Tailoring with AI as an editor

The workflow that keeps the resume honest and yours:

1. **Paste the JD** and ask: *"List the 10 most important requirements in this JD in priority order, with the exact phrases used. Then list which of my resume items (pasted below) map to each, and which requirements I do not evidence."*
2. **Decide** what to add, reorder or cut. If you lack a requirement, do not invent it; consider whether a course project covers it partially and say so honestly.
3. **Edit bullets**: *"Rewrite these three bullets in ATRE format using the JD's vocabulary where truthful. Do not add numbers or tools I did not provide. Keep each under 30 words."*
4. **Summary**: three lines — who you are, what you have done (two proof points), what you are looking for. Draft with the assistant; rewrite in your voice.
5. **Check**: run the fact-check routine (Lesson 2.6) on the AI's edits; ATS-format check; read aloud.
6. **Save as** `Firstname_Lastname_Role_Company.pdf`; keep a master resume with everything and tailored versions per application.

Ask the assistant to play a sceptical recruiter for the target role and to name the three weakest lines; fix them.

### 4. The portfolio

A portfolio is proof. For non-technical graduates it is rare, which makes it powerful.

**Structure** (a simple site — Canva, Google Sites, Notion, Carrd — or a well-organised shared folder with an index PDF):

1. **Home**: one paragraph, photo optional, contact, resume download, links.
2. **Projects** (4–6): each as a one-screen case study — *Problem → What I did → Tools → Result (numbers) → Evidence (video, screenshots, documents) → What I learned → My role (if a team).*
3. **Prompt playbook** (selected, cleaned entries).
4. **Writing samples**: the ethics audit summary, the process proposal, a blog/LinkedIn article.
5. **Certificate and AI-use statement**.

Quality rules: every claim has evidence; every video under two minutes; remove personal data of others (blur, anonymise); consistent design (your brand sheet from PB2); mobile-friendly; a link that works from the resume and LinkedIn.

### 5. AI-use in applications: disclosure and integrity

- Using AI to edit your resume is normal and acceptable; **inventing** experience is fraud regardless of tool.
- Some employers ask whether AI was used in the application; answer honestly ("Yes, for editing and tailoring; all content is mine").
- Portfolio case studies should state AI use as you did in each project's AI-use statement — it demonstrates exactly the professional habit employers want.
- Never paste other people's personal data (from your projects or previous employers) into an AI tool while preparing applications.

## Try it now

Take your worst resume bullet. Rewrite it in ATRE format by hand. Then ask an assistant to *"make it tighter without adding any facts"*. Compare. Keep the more truthful one.

## Hands-on lab: master resume, tailored version, portfolio skeleton (50 min)

1. **Achievement mining (15 min):** write ATRE bullets for all six course projects and three other experiences; verify every number against your project records.
2. **Master resume (10 min):** single-column ATS-friendly template; summary; sections; all bullets.
3. **Tailored version (15 min):** pick a real job posting (placement portal, LinkedIn, company site) for a role you want; run the tailoring workflow; produce the PDF; run the sceptical-recruiter critique.
4. **Portfolio skeleton (10 min):** create the site or folder; home page; one complete case study (capstone or PB4) with evidence links; list the remaining case studies with owners and dates.

**Deliverable:** master resume, tailored resume with the JD and mapping table, portfolio link with one complete case study. Bring the tailored resume to Lesson 8.4 for mock interviews.

## Common mistakes

- **Designer resumes** with columns, icons and photos that ATS cannot parse.
- **Duties instead of achievements.** "Responsible for social media" says nothing; "grew followers 1.2k → 4k in 3 months" says everything.
- **Letting AI invent numbers or tools.** Your interview will be the audit.
- **A portfolio of screenshots without context.** Case studies, not galleries.
- **One resume for every job.** Tailor; it takes fifteen minutes with the workflow.

## Key takeaways

- Resumes are parsed by ATS, matched on keywords, scored by AI, then skimmed by humans; format single-column, use the JD's vocabulary truthfully, lead with the strongest evidence.
- Every bullet: Action – Task – Result – Evidence, with a number; mine all six course projects.
- Tailor with AI as an editor: requirement mapping, honest gaps, ATRE rewrites, sceptical-recruiter critique, fact-check.
- A portfolio of 4–6 evidence-backed case studies is rare among non-technical graduates and powerful.
- Disclose AI use honestly; never invent; protect others' data.
