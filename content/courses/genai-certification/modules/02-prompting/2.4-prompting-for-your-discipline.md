# 2.4 Prompting for your discipline

> **Lesson plan** · 90 minutes — Read 35 · Lab 40 · Quiz 15

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- Apply CO-STAR and the advanced patterns to tasks specific to commerce, management, arts/humanities and science.
- Use bilingual (Kannada / Hindi + English) prompting for study, translation and community-facing communication.
- Identify the discipline-specific risks — wrong law, wrong formula, wrong citation, wrong cultural register — and the checks that catch them.
- Start a discipline-specific section of your prompt playbook.

## Opening scenario

Four friends from the same college — Divya (B.Com), Farhan (BBA), Lakshmi (BA Kannada & Political Science) and Nithin (B.Sc Chemistry) — each try to use an assistant for their week's work. Divya gets a GST rate that changed two years ago. Farhan gets a marketing plan that assumes a US shopping-mall culture. Lakshmi gets a beautiful but invented quotation attributed to Kuvempu. Nithin gets a lab calculation with the right method and a slipped decimal.

Each discipline has its own way of being wrong. This lesson gives you prompt sets that work — and the specific checks that catch each discipline's failure.

## Core ideas

### 1. Commerce & accounting (B.Com)

**What AI is good for:** explaining concepts with worked examples, drafting journal entries from a narrative, converting data into ledgers, preparing exam-style questions, explaining Excel/Sheets formulas, drafting professional correspondence, comparing accounting standards.

**Where it fails:** current tax rates, thresholds and deadlines (staleness); arithmetic on long lists; Indian standards (Ind AS) versus international ones; anything that depends on a notification issued after training.

Prompt set:

```text
You are an accounting tutor for a B.Com student following the Karnataka
university syllabus. From the transaction narrative below, prepare:
(1) journal entries in a table (Date | Particulars | Debit ₹ | Credit ₹),
(2) a one-line reason for each entry citing the accounting rule applied,
(3) a list of any assumptions you had to make.
Use Indian formatting (₹, lakh/crore) and do not calculate GST unless the
narrative states a rate.
```

```text
Explain the difference between straight-line and written-down-value
depreciation using a ₹5,00,000 machine over 5 years at 10%. Show a year-by-year
table for both methods, then a 3-line summary of when a business prefers each.
Mark the arithmetic so I can check it in a spreadsheet.
```

**Discipline check:** anything with a rate, a threshold or a date → confirm on the official source (CBIC, Income Tax portal, ICAI). Anything with sums → recompute in Sheets.

### 2. Management & marketing (BBA)

**What AI is good for:** frameworks applied to a specific case (SWOT, 4Ps, STP, Porter's forces), customer personas, survey questions, campaign ideas, presentation outlines, role-play for negotiation and interviews.

**Where it fails:** context that is not Indian or not local (assumes credit cards, malls, Western holidays), invented market statistics, generic "strategy" language with no decisions in it.

Prompt set:

```text
CONTEXT: A family-run bakery in Davangere, 15 years old, ₹18 lakh annual sales,
loyal older customers, weak with under-25s. Budget for marketing: ₹15,000/month.
OBJECTIVE: A 90-day plan to grow under-25 walk-ins by 20%.
STYLE: Decisions, not theory. Every recommendation has a cost and a metric.
TONE: Practical.
AUDIENCE: The owner, who is not on Instagram himself.
RESPONSE: A table (Week | Action | Cost ₹ | How we measure it), then three
risks. Do not invent market statistics; where a figure is needed write
[ESTIMATE] and explain how to get it.
```

```text
Create 3 customer personas for a budget hostel in Manipal targeting students
and their parents. For each: name, age, background, what they worry about, how
they decide, where they get information. Keep them realistic for coastal
Karnataka; avoid stereotypes about gender or community.
```

**Discipline check:** every statistic → [ESTIMATE] or a real source; every recommendation → a cost and a metric; run a persona-panel critique with a sceptical local business owner.

### 3. Arts, humanities & social sciences (BA)

**What AI is good for:** structuring arguments, summarising and comparing readings *you supply*, translation and glossing, explaining theory in plain language, generating counter-arguments, editing for clarity, practising viva questions.

**Where it fails:** quotations and citations (the highest hallucination risk of all), nuance in regional literature and history, cultural and caste-sensitive topics where stereotypes hide in "neutral" prose, dates and attributions.

Prompt set:

```text
I am writing an essay on the representation of rural life in modern Kannada
fiction. Attached are my notes on three works I have actually read. Using ONLY
these notes, help me build an argument: (1) propose a thesis in one sentence,
(2) outline four supporting points each tied to a specific note, (3) suggest
one counter-argument and how I might answer it. Do not add quotations or works
that are not in my notes.
```

```text
Translate this paragraph from Kannada to academic English, keeping the
author's tone. Then list three words or phrases where the translation loses
meaning, and explain what is lost.
```

```text
Act as a viva examiner for a BA Political Science student. Ask me one question
at a time about the 73rd Constitutional Amendment. After each answer, tell me
what a strong answer would have added. Stop after 5 questions and give me an
overall assessment.
```

**Discipline check:** never use a quotation the assistant supplied unless you have seen it in the source; check dates and attributions; read "neutral" descriptions of communities critically (Module 7).

### 4. Science (B.Sc)

**What AI is good for:** explaining concepts at different levels, checking the *method* of a calculation, writing lab-report sections from your data, generating practice problems, explaining error analysis, drafting code or spreadsheet formulas for data handling.

**Where it fails:** arithmetic and unit conversions in long calculations, made-up constants or references, safety information, anything numerical where a slipped decimal matters.

Prompt set:

```text
Here is my titration data (table below). (1) Explain the method for finding
the concentration of the unknown, step by step. (2) Show the calculation with
units at every step. (3) Then give me a spreadsheet formula I can use to
re-check the arithmetic myself. (4) Point out the two most likely sources of
error in this experiment.
```

```text
Explain enzyme kinetics at three levels: a 12-year-old, a first-year B.Sc
student, and a postgraduate. Keep each under 100 words. Then give one common
misconception at each level and why it is wrong.
```

**Discipline check:** recompute every number in a spreadsheet or calculator; verify constants in a textbook; never rely on the assistant for lab safety — use the lab manual and your instructor.

### 5. Bilingual prompting for all disciplines

Indian graduates work across languages every day: notes in English, explanations to parents in Kannada, WhatsApp to a client in Hindi. Assistants handle this well when you are explicit:

- **Study in your strongest language.** "Explain this in Kannada first, then give the English technical terms in brackets."
- **Draft in one language, deliver in another.** "I will write my points in Kannada; produce a formal English email."
- **Check register.** "Is this Hindi too informal for a bank manager? Suggest a more respectful version."
- **Glossaries.** "Make a two-column English–Kannada glossary of the 20 key terms in this chapter."
- **Community communication.** "Write a notice for parents about the fee deadline in simple Kannada, then English; avoid bureaucratic words."

Quality varies by language and tool; test both your assistants and note which is stronger for Kannada, Hindi, Tamil or Telugu in your evidence log.

## Try it now

Whatever your discipline, run this and study the result closely:

```text
I study [B.Com / BBA / BA / B.Sc — your subject]. List the 10 tasks in my
degree where a chat assistant is most useful, and for each, name the ONE
mistake it is most likely to make and the check I should run. Then tell me the
two tasks where I should not use it at all, and why.
```

Compare the answer with the discipline sections above. Where do you disagree?

## Hands-on lab: your discipline prompt set (40 min)

1. **Choose four real tasks (5 min)** from your current semester, ideally one from each of: explain, produce, analyse, communicate.
2. **Write CO-STAR prompts for each (15 min)** using the discipline section above as a model. Include at least one bilingual prompt.
3. **Run, then apply the discipline check (15 min).** Actually verify: look up the rate, recompute the number, check the citation, review the cultural register. Record what you found.
4. **Package (5 min).** For each prompt: when to use it, what to check afterwards, and the tool that did best. Add all four to your playbook.

**Deliverable:** four prompts with outputs, verification notes and playbook entries. At least one verification should have caught something — if none did, look harder or note honestly that the outputs passed.

## Common mistakes

- **Asking for "the current rate".** Ask for the *method* and look up the rate yourself.
- **Letting the assistant supply sources for humanities essays.** Only sources you have read.
- **Accepting Western defaults.** State the place, the currency, the culture and the reader.
- **Trusting long arithmetic.** Ask for a formula you can recheck; recheck it.

## Key takeaways

- Each discipline has a characteristic failure: stale rules (commerce), foreign context and fake statistics (management), invented quotations (arts), slipped numbers (science).
- Pair every discipline prompt with its check.
- Bilingual prompting is a superpower for Indian graduates — be explicit about languages, direction and register.
- Your playbook should be organised by *your* tasks, not by tool.

## Reflection journal

Write 150 words: *Which discipline check caught something in your lab, or which one do you think is most likely to save you embarrassment in your first job? What will you do differently on your next assignment?*

- Swap prompt sets with a classmate from a different degree and try theirs. Note what transfers across disciplines.
- For your subject, find one official source (a portal, a standard, a manual) that you will use for verification and bookmark it in your toolkit sheet.
