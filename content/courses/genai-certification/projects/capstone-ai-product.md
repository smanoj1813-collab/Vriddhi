# Capstone — AI Product Sprint

> **Project plan** · 90 minutes guided showcase + the Module 6 sprint (Weeks 7–9) · Showcase in Week 9 · Weight: 30% of the final grade

## The brief

In a team of two to four, identify a real problem for a specific group of users, build a working AI-enabled solution with no-code tools, pilot it with real users, and present the evidence. The capstone integrates everything in the course: prompting, content creation, assistants, automation, apps, responsible AI, and professional communication.

You are assessed on **the quality of the problem, the evidence of validation, the working product, the responsibility of the build, and the clarity of the pitch and report** — not on how sophisticated the AI is.

## What to submit (by the showcase)

1. **Two-page report (PDF)** with these headings:
   1. Problem and users — statement, interview evidence (count, method, key findings, verbatims).
   2. Alternatives and why users would switch.
   3. Solution — one-page architecture diagram with data flows; the AI feature and its prompt specification.
   4. AI-feature evaluation — accuracy on a fixed sample, language review, unacceptable-output controls.
   5. Pilot results — reach, engagement, outcome, quality; what changed because of feedback.
   6. Responsibility — privacy notice, data checklist, human checkpoints, kill switch, limits and refusals.
   7. Decision — persevere / pivot / stop, with evidence and a four-week plan.
   8. Contributions — who did what (must match the build log); AI-use statement.
2. **Working product** — access for the jury (link/test account) plus a 60–90 second backup demo video showing the core flow, the AI feature and one graceful failure.
3. **Five-minute pitch** — slides (8–10, sentence titles) delivered live at the showcase by the whole team, followed by five minutes of questions.
4. **Evidence pack (shared folder)** — team charter, interview guide and syntheses, lean canvas (v0 and final), backlog with definitions of done, daily build log, test plan with results, feedback log and themed synthesis, analytics sheet, privacy notice and consent records, hand-over document.
5. **Individual reflection (300 words each)** — what you built, what you learned about users and about AI, what you would do differently, and the one skill you will carry into your first job.

## Constraints

- Real users, real consent, real pilot (minimum 10 users, minimum 5 days) — or, if the pilot could not run, a documented reason and a completed usability test with at least five users.
- No personal data sent to AI steps unless necessary, minimised and disclosed; privacy notice live before the first user.
- Nothing irreversible or external runs without a human checkpoint until the test log shows it is safe.
- All AI-generated artefacts in the product were tested by a named team member (logged).
- Free tiers and institutional accounts only; document any limits you hit.
- The product must run at the showcase or the backup video must be shown; a pitch without either fails the "working product" criterion.

## Rubric (30 marks)

| Criterion | Marks | Distinction looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Problem and validation** | 6 | Specific user; five-plus interviews about past behaviour; honest alternatives table; riskiest assumptions named and tested; lean canvas evolved with evidence. |
| **Working product** | 7 | Runs end to end at the showcase (or backup shown); architecture sensible and documented; AI feature useful, narrow, validated, with fallback; integration issues found and fixed. |
| **Pilot evidence and iteration** | 6 | Real users; four numbers tracked daily; feedback synthesised from the log; at least one iteration shipped and measured; decision memo honest and evidence-based. |
| **Responsible build** | 5 | Privacy notice and consent; data minimised and mapped; human checkpoints; kill switch; unacceptable outputs blocked; language quality reviewed; limits and refusals stated. |
| **Pitch and report** | 4 | Five minutes, evidence-led, every member speaks, demo with a failure handled, hard questions answered with data; report clear, traceable numbers, two pages. |
| **Teamwork and individual contribution** | 2 | Build log shows daily entries and credited work; contributions in the report match; reflections specific. |

**Passing the capstone requires at least 12/30** (40% of its weight) — see the certification policy. Marks are awarded to the team; the facilitator may adjust individual marks by up to ±20% based on the build log, reflections and observed participation.

## Jury guidance

Juries typically include a faculty member, a practitioner from industry or a start-up, and where possible a representative of the user community. Jurors are asked to weigh **evidence over polish** and to probe: *Who exactly is the user? What did they do differently because of this? What would you refuse to build? What happens when it fails? Who owns the data?*

## Tips from previous cohorts

- Teams that stopped a bad idea in Week 7 and restarted with a narrower user did better than teams that pushed on.
- Manual steps behind the scenes ("Wizard of Oz") are legitimate for a pilot and should be stated plainly; juries respect honesty and punish concealment.
- The graceful-failure slide is the most credible thing in the pitch.
- Write the report from the build log every evening; the last-night report is always visibly worse.
- The strongest pitches led with one user and one number, and put the technology on slide four.
