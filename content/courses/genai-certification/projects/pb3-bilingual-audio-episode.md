# Project Block 3 — Bilingual Audio Episode

> **Project plan** · 90 minutes guided + 2–3 hours independent work · Submit by the end of Week 6 · Weight: 7% of the final grade

## The brief

Produce a **three-minute audio explainer** (podcast episode or voiceover) on a topic that matters to a specific audience, in **English plus one Indian language** (Kannada, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Marathi, Urdu, Konkani or Tulu). The episode must be accurate, well-paced, properly scored, correctly licensed, and published with show notes and a transcript.

You may produce **one bilingual episode** (segments alternate or the Indian-language version follows the English) or **two parallel episodes** of the same content. Total finished audio: 3–5 minutes.

## What to submit

1. **Scripts** — English and Indian-language versions, marked up for the ear (segment structure, pauses, emphasis), with a **fact sheet** listing every claim, number and date and the source you verified it against.
2. **Audio files** — final MP3(s) at the platform loudness preset, plus a 30-second raw recording if you used your own voice.
3. **Production sheet** — voice choices per language (recorded / synthesised, tool, voice name), pronunciation fixes applied, editing steps, music source and licence, loudness target, export settings.
4. **Show notes** — summary, timestamps, credits (voice, music licence, AI tools), and a full transcript in both languages.
5. **Consent and rights record** — consent recordings/lines for any cloned or recorded voices other than your own; music licence; any third-party material.
6. **Listener test** — feedback from at least three listeners from the intended audience (at least one for each language): what they remembered, what confused them, what they would change; and what you changed as a result.
7. **Reflection (250 words)** — what the tools did, what you did, where language quality fell short, what you would do differently.
8. **AI-use statement** on the cover.

## Constraints

- No cloned voices except your own or a consenting classmate's with a documented consent recording.
- Music must be licensed for your distribution channel; the licence must be recorded.
- Every fact verified against a primary source; scheme/health/legal content must cite the official page.
- Indian-language script proofread by a competent native reader, named in the production sheet.
- Synthetic voices disclosed in the show notes.

## Rubric (7 marks)

| Criterion | 0–1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Audio craft** (3) | Mispronunciations, noise, uneven levels, music over voice, robotic pacing. | Clean and listenable; minor pacing or level issues. | Studio-grade: clean, well-paced, natural pauses, music ducked, consistent voice, correct loudness. |
| **Script and accuracy** (2) | Read from a document; unverified claims; no fact sheet. | Written for the ear; fact sheet mostly complete. | Re-told for the ear with signposting and a clear action; every claim verified; Indian-language version idiomatic. |
| **Rights, consent and disclosure** (1) | Missing or violated. | Present with gaps. | Complete: music licence, consents, synthetic-voice disclosure, transcript published. |
| **Listener test and reflection** (1) | None. | Feedback collected but not acted on. | Feedback from both language audiences, specific changes made, honest reflection. |

**Pass threshold for this block:** 3.5 / 7. Any cloned voice without documented consent fails the rights criterion and caps the block at 3.

## Tips from previous cohorts

- Write the Indian-language script *as speech*, not as a translation of the English; the best episodes were re-told, not translated.
- Audition voices with your listeners, not by yourself.
- Do the pronunciation pass before generating the full script — it saves credits and time.
- If you use your own voice, record in a cupboard. Really.
