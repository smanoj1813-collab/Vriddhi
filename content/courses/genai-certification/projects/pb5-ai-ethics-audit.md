# Project Block 5 — AI Ethics & Safety Audit

> **Project plan** · 45 minutes guided + 2 hours independent work · Submit by the end of Week 9 · Weight: 7% of the final grade

## The brief

Audit **one AI system you used or built in this course** — a commercial tool (a chat assistant, image generator, TTS service, automation platform) or your own build (Placement Coach, PB4, the capstone) — against a fairness, transparency, privacy and safety checklist. Produce an evidence-based audit report with a decision and conditions, of the kind a manager could act on.

This is an individual project.

## What to submit

An audit report (4–6 pages including tables) with:

1. **System description** — what it is, who uses it, what decisions or content it influences; scope of the audit.
2. **Stakeholder and harm analysis** — the eight-step ethics analysis from Lesson 7.1 (condensed to one page).
3. **Bias test** — the protocol from Lesson 7.2: attributes, paired prompts, at least 10 runs per variant for one text test and/or 10 images per variant for one image test, coded sheet attached, findings with examples, severity judgement, and the mitigation you applied with before/after evidence.
4. **Transparency review** — the model/system card checklist from Lesson 7.3 applied to the tool (or your own system card if auditing your build); gaps identified; the explanations available to affected people.
5. **Privacy and security review** — data flows; what personal data is involved; DPDP compliance table from Lesson 7.4; training/retention settings; a prompt-injection or misuse test with result; deepfake/likeness considerations if relevant.
6. **Findings summary** — a table: issue · evidence · severity (high/medium/low) · who is affected · recommendation · owner · retest date.
7. **Decision** — proceed / proceed with conditions / redesign / do not use — for a stated use case, with the conditions spelt out.
8. **Limits of this audit** — what you could not test and why.
9. **Reflection (200 words)** — what surprised you; what you will do differently as a user and as a builder.
10. **AI-use statement.**

## Constraints

- Every finding must be tied to evidence you produced (runs, screenshots, settings, documents) or a cited primary source.
- The assistant may tabulate your coded sheet and draft sections from your notes; it may not judge bias in outputs or invent case material.
- Do not test on real people's personal data; use synthetic profiles.
- Where you audit your own build, be as harsh as you would be on a vendor.

## Process (suggested)

| When | Do |
| --- | --- |
| Week 8, self-study | Choose the system; scope; run the bias test (Lesson 7.2 lab). |
| Week 9, session 1 (guided) | Transparency and privacy reviews; injection test; findings table peer-reviewed. |
| Week 9, self-study | Decision, limits, reflection; submit. |

## Rubric (7 marks)

| Criterion | 0–1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Evidence quality** (3) | Opinions without runs; too few runs; unclear method. | Protocol followed; adequate runs; findings mostly supported. | Rigorous: paired prompts, volume, coded sheet, before/after mitigation, sources opened; limits stated. |
| **Coverage of FATE + privacy/security** (2) | One dimension only. | Most dimensions covered; some thin. | Fairness, transparency, accountability, privacy and security each assessed with specific findings. |
| **Decision and recommendations** (1) | Missing or "ban AI"/"all fine". | Present but generic. | Actionable: conditions, owners, retest dates, tied to severity; defensible to affected people. |
| **Clarity and honesty** (1) | Disorganised; overstated. | Clear. | Manager-ready; honest about what was not found and could not be tested. |

**Pass threshold for this block:** 3.5 / 7.

## Tips from previous cohorts

- The most convincing audits tested one thing thoroughly rather than five things thinly.
- Auditing your own capstone earns respect from juries — especially when you find something and fix it.
- Indian-language tests found problems that English tests missed; include one.
- "Do not use for X, fine for Y, with conditions Z" is a stronger conclusion than a single verdict.

## AI-use statement template
