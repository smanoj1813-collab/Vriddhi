# Final Assessment Blueprint — VGC-101 GenAI for Career Impact

> **Format** · 90 minutes total · Part A: 60 MCQs in 60 minutes (open-web tools **not** permitted) · Part B: two scenario responses in 30 minutes (AI tools permitted, with an AI-use statement) · Weight: 15% of the final grade

## Purpose

The final assessment confirms that a learner can **recall and apply** the course's core concepts without assistance (Part A) and can **reason through a realistic workplace situation** with the judgement the certificate promises (Part B). It is deliberately not a test of tool button-clicks — those are assessed through the project blocks and the capstone.

## Part A — 60 MCQs (60 marks, scaled to 10)

### Source and construction

- Items are drawn from the per-module quiz banks (`modules/*/quiz.json`, 175 items) plus a **held-back supplementary bank** of the same style maintained by the programme team (not published to learners). At least 40% of items in any paper must come from the held-back bank.
- Each paper is assembled per the module weighting below; items are shuffled; option order is randomised.
- One mark per item; no negative marking; single correct option.
- Items are reviewed annually for tool-version drift (see facilitator guide, "Tool landscape").

### Module weighting

| Module | Items | Emphasis |

### Cognitive level mix (Bloom-lite)

| Level | Share | Example stem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recall | ≈ 30% | "What do the letters in CO-STAR stand for?" |
| Understand | ≈ 35% | "Why does a shared assistant with personal data in its knowledge files constitute a breach?" |
| Apply / judge | ≈ 35% | "A task scores 20/30 on the automation scorecard. The recommended action is…" |

### Item-writing standards

- Stem states a complete problem; options are parallel in form and length; exactly one defensible answer; distractors are plausible misconceptions from the lessons.
- No "all of the above"/"none of the above"; no negatives unless bolded.
- Version-agnostic wording (product families, not versions or prices).
- Indian context where it aids clarity; no culturally exclusive references.
- Every item has an explanation for post-assessment feedback.

## Part B — Two scenario responses (2 × 10 marks, scaled to 5)

### Format

Learners receive two scenarios chosen by the facilitator from the pool below (or new ones of equivalent structure), each answered in **200–300 words**. AI tools are permitted; each response must end with a one-line AI-use statement. Responses are evaluated on judgement and specificity, not prose polish.

### Scenario pool (rotate; do not publish the chosen pair in advance)

1. **The manager's request.** Your manager at a small firm asks you to paste 300 customer records into a free chatbot to "find patterns". What do you do, in what order, and why?
2. **The perfect first draft.** A colleague wants to send an AI-drafted proposal to a client without changes. Describe the checks you would run in fifteen minutes and what you would look for.
3. **The screener.** Your college placement cell proposes an AI resume screener. Write the conditions you would insist on before it goes live.
4. **The campaign.** An NGO asks for a poster with a realistic image of "a happy tribal family" for a fundraising drive. How do you respond and what do you produce instead?
5. **The automation that misfired.** An automation you built sent a wrong confirmation to 60 registrants at 6 am. What do you do in the first hour, the first day, and what changes afterwards?
6. **The voice note.** A local politician's team asks you to produce a Kannada voice message "in his voice" from a script he approved by WhatsApp. What do you need before you agree, and what would make you decline?
7. **The grounded assistant.** A pharmacy wants a staff assistant answering from its SOPs. Design the grounding test and state the score below which you would not deploy.
8. **The vanity pilot.** A start-up founder shows you 2,000 sign-ups as proof of success. What numbers would you ask for and how would you decide persevere/pivot/stop?
9. **DPDP in a club.** Your club collects names, phones and photos through a form for an event. What must you put in place now, and what would change once the DPDP duties apply?
10. **The interview question.** Answer "How do you use AI in your work?" as you would in an interview for a role you want, in four parts.

### Scenario rubric (10 marks each)

| Criterion | 0–1 | 2 | 3 (and 4 where marked) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Identifies the real issue(s)** (3) | Misses the core risk or opportunity. | Identifies the main issue. | Identifies the main issue and second-order issues (e.g. consent *and* training settings *and* contract). |
| **Applies course frameworks correctly** (3) | None or misapplied. | One framework applied correctly. | Two or more frameworks applied and combined (e.g. Automate–Augment–Avoid + DPDP checklist + human-in-the-loop). |
| **Specific, ordered actions** (3) | Vague ("be careful"). | Concrete actions, partly ordered. | Concrete, ordered, with owners/thresholds/what to say to whom. |
| **Honest limits and disclosure** (1) | Absent. | — | States what they do not know or would escalate; AI-use statement present. |

## Administration

- **Mode:** in-app (Part A auto-scored from the bank; Part B typed into the response box or uploaded) or paper-based for centres without reliable connectivity (Part A answer sheet; Part B handwritten, AI tools then not available — facilitators should adjust expectations and note the mode on the record).
- **Timing:** Part A 60 minutes, then a 5-minute break, then Part B 30 minutes.
- **Permitted materials:** Part A — none (no web, no notes). Part B — any AI tool, the learner's own playbook and notes; no communication with other people.
- **Accommodations:** additional time (typically +25%) and a reader/scribe for learners with documented needs; Kannada/Hindi glossaries of technical terms may be provided for Part A on request; Part B may be answered in English, Kannada or Hindi.
- **Integrity:** randomised papers; seat spacing or in-app lockdown where available; similarity checks on Part B; suspected misconduct handled under the certification policy.
- **Re-sits:** one re-sit permitted within 30 days for learners scoring below 40% on the final; the re-sit mark is capped at 60% for grade calculation (see certification policy).

## Scoring

- Part A raw /60 → scaled /10. Part B raw /20 → scaled /5. Final assessment total /15.
- Reported to learners with per-module Part A performance and the Part B rubric scores, plus item explanations for the items they answered incorrectly.

## Maintenance

- Review all items each cohort for tool drift, legal updates (DPDP commencement stages; IT Rules on synthetic media; EU AI Act deadlines) and difficulty (retire items with facility > 0.95 or < 0.25; check discrimination).
- Log item statistics and scenario rubric distributions in the programme's assessment register.
